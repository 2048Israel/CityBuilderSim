earth-2.bin: earth-1.bin's container and layers, and six more (the Earth 2 study, docs/studies/spec-earth2-terrain.md).

THE CONTAINER is earth-1's, unchanged (FORMAT.txt): the same eight bytes "EARTH-1\n" (they name the layout, not the
file), layout version 1, the same header, index and blob rules. EarthData reads earth-2.bin as it is; a layer it does
not know is never asked for. The file is told from earth-1 by its meta layer (name "earth-2") and its SHA-256
(earth-2.sha256); a save names it as "earth-2:<hash>". tools/earth/earthread.py reads it.

EARTH-1'S LAYERS are copied byte for byte (sea, lake, lakes.small, rivers, landcover, far.land, far.lake, geology,
provinces, places); bake2.py takes them from earth-1.bin and check2.py holds every blob to the original. Only meta is new.

NEW LAYERS (4-bit shares are 0..15, share = v/15, as earth-1's sea and lake)

  slope         kind 0, 240/deg (15"), a byte a cell: the cell's LAND (1 - (sea + lake)/15 of earth-1; at 120/deg,
                its four 15" cells' land summed) by slope:
                  bits 0-2  F x 7: the share of its land at or above FOOTHILL (10% grade, 5.7 degrees)
                  bits 3-5  S x 7: the share at or above STEEP (30% grade, 16.7 degrees); S <= F
                  bits 6-7  (V / S) x 3: the part of the steep ground that is VERY STEEP (60% grade, 31 degrees)
                So with F = (b & 7)/7, S = ((b >> 3) & 7)/7 and V = S x (b >> 6)/3, a cell's land is flat 1 - F,
                foothill F - S, steep S - V and very steep V. A cell with no land holds 0.
                The slope of a 90 m cell: NASADEM's 1" heights averaged over 3 x 3 (3", 90 m), the grade by Horn's
                3 x 3 method over that grid; 25 such cells to a 15" cell (100 to a 30" one), a share their count over
                that number x the land share, capped at 1. (Eight levels, not sixteen. 15" since 2026-10-12, Jerus's
                D3: the layer at 15" is about 204 MB, at 30" 57 MB, projected from 2,037 random tiles; the first bake
                was 30". The reader takes the grid from the layer's header, cpd; bake2.py makes 30" with
                CBS_SLOPE_CPD=120.)
  slope.est     kind 2: 8,100 bytes, a bit a 1-degree tile (tile i is bit 7 - i%8 of byte i/8): set where the slope
                shares are an estimate - north of 61 N, south of 56 S, and any tile NASADEM has no heights for -
                from GEBCO 2026's 15" slope (Horn over 15") through a table: the mean shares of NASADEM's land cells
                at 45-60 degrees north and south in the same bin of GEBCO slope (meta.slope.estimate gives the bins).
  shallow       kind 0, 240/deg, a byte a cell: the share of the cell's SEA no deeper than 30 m (depth.SHALLOW_M),
                0..15; 0 where the cell has no sea. GEBCO 2026's elevation bilinear between cell centres at 4 x 4
                points a cell gives q, the points' share above -30 m; the sea is taken to fill the deepest part of
                the cell, so with earth-1's sea share s the shallow part of the sea is clamp((q - (1 - s)) / s, 0, 1).
  depth         kind 0, 60/deg (1'), a byte a cell: the mean depth of the cell's sea, its 16 15" cells' GEBCO depth
                (elevation below 0; 0 where GEBCO has land under earth-1's sea) weighted by their sea share:
                  0 no sea (or under 0.5 m)   1..200 that many metres   201..254 200 m + 10 m x (v - 200)
                  255 750 m or deeper
                A sea plot is never shallower than 1 m in the game (depthAt() over 0 means sea).
  landmass      kind 0, 240/deg, an id a cell: the land cell's landmass, 0 no land. A landmass is ground cut off by
                more water than a SHORT CROSSING spans (landmass.CROSS_M, 34 plots, 1,020 m of fresh water or shallow
                sea; deep sea is never crossed):
                  land       a cell whose sea + lake levels come to 7 or less (EarthTerrain.COAST_BELOW's side), unless
                             a GRWL river wider than 1,020 m covers it (a segment of that mean width cuts the cells
                             within half its width of its line, and every cell the line passes through)
                  crossable  a water cell that is fresh (lake share at least sea share, or a river's cut) or shallow
                             (this file's shallow share 8 of 15 or more); the rest is deep
                  joined     land cells along their four edges; and two pieces of land across crossable water when,
                             each crossable cell taking its nearest land's label and its distance from that shore (a
                             distance transform less the half cell to the shore), two neighbouring cells of different
                             labels come to shore-to-cell + cell-to-cell + cell-to-shore of 1,020 m or less - worked in a
                             window round each tile, so across tile edges and the 180th meridian. Joins chain: islands
                             each within a crossing of the next join the shore they lead to.
                Ids are by area, largest first (1 is Afro-Eurasia). A uniform tile is -id in the index (0: no land). A
                blob, zlib: uvarint n, then n uvarints, the tile's ids ascending as deltas (the first from 0), then
                240 x 240 palette indexes, rows from the north, a byte each when n <= 256, else a big-endian short each.
  landmasses    kind 2: UTF-8 text, a line a landmass in id order (line 1 is id 1): area in km2 (its cells' dry share
                x cell area, two decimals), its count of 15" cells, and the name of the most populous Natural Earth
                place on it (empty when none), tab-separated.
  meta          as earth-1's, with name "earth-2", the earth-1 file it was made from and its hash, the thresholds,
                the shallow depth, the depth code, the stats and every source (earth-1's and the new).
