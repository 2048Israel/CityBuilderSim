## What to build

**One screen that answers "is my bank healthy, and why" without a click, then pages behind it.**

### The landing — "The bank at a glance"
- **A status sentence** from the model (one getter that names the bank's state in words: healthy and lending freely / rebuilding capital and lending carefully / under the minimum and lending only to keep borrowers going / failed — with the number that decides it).
- **A scorecard** (vitals cells): profit this month and over the last twelve; return on equity (annualised); the capital ratio on a small bar against the minimum, the bank's own target and the top of its band; credit losses (provisions) over the last twelve months as % of loans; net interest margin; costs as % of revenue; loans; deposits.
- **The rate ladder** — the centrepiece: the policy rate → what savers get (and the share of the policy rate the bank chose) → the funds-transfer price → prime (with its four parts: funding, running costs, expected loss, capital charge) → what each kind of borrower actually pays (each sector's quoted rate, the household rate, the carry rate), with the spread between each step labelled in points. Link the policy rate to the Policy tab's monetary page (`limitCell`'s go-form or equivalent).
- Then the five page entries, each with its one-line headline figure.

### The pages (a chip strip, as today)
1. **Profit** — the income statement this month against last (`bookLine`), and the last twelve months: interest earned by book (business, households, the city's paper, carry, reserves at the central bank, the discount accreted), paid to savers, paid at the window → net interest income; fees (accounts, business loan fees, household loan fees); provisions (with write-offs against the allowance in the detail); the trading desk (opening into what it did); gains on the city's paper; staff and branches; profit before tax; tax; what it kept; and what it did with it (dividends, buybacks, retained). Efficiency ratio and net interest margin under it.
2. **Lending** — who owes it: each sector (owed, rate, leverage against the restructure line — warn from `INSOLVENCY_TRIGGER`, not 2.0 — stage 1 or 2, the allowance held against it, blocked months), the households (book, stage, allowance), the city's paper, the carry book; the allowance total and this month's provision; who is in trouble and the write-offs this month and since founding; this month's new lending against the limit the capital rule set; **how the next loan's rate is built** (the four parts, then a sound sector's prime and the actual quotes); the weight table (face, maturity, risk weight, weighted — footing to the weighted book, desk shares included).
3. **Funding** — deposits (households, sectors, the world's hot money) and what the bank can reach with its branches (in today's money); the deposit rate, the share of the policy rate it passes on and why (its funding position); window borrowing and its cost; reserves at the central bank and what they earn; the funding mix as a bar; **branches** (standing, what one more would add, its cost and running cost, whether it would pay — reading the model's own verdict, both halves of `wantsBranch()`), with a link to the Build tab.
4. **Capital & owners** — the capital ratio in its band (minimum, the bank's target and why it chose it — the worst loss it has seen or the standard buffer — the top); what the rule does with this month's profit and why; dividends and buybacks this month and over the year; how the equity moved this month (opening, kept, capital put in, paid out, absorbed, and a residual that must be zero — from a model getter that includes the founding settlement); the share price, market value, and who owns it (households, the world, and the treasury if it ever does); rescue history; the rescue button when the bank has failed (the guard in the model, not the screen).
5. **History** — trend charts: the rates over time on one chart (`policyRate`, `bankDepositRate`, `bankPrime`), the capital ratio against its target (`bankCapitalRatio`, `bankCapitalTarget`), return on equity, provisions against write-offs, lending against deposits and capacity, fees. Replace the premium-era statistics with ones that mean something now (months under target, months under the minimum, the worst year's provisions).
