# The 1900 city grows

The 1900 city stalled because it paid 1901's wages for today's homes,
while its employers wanted tradesmen the city could not replace. In the
first measured village, rent took 0.61 of take-home at month 60 against
the model's 0.30 target. Native-born workers gained skills only through a
school, and the village had no school, so its tradesmen died out beside
empty diploma posts and unemployed labourers. Relieving either limit
alone left the other. The dated money and the unfitted machinery were
not the cause.

Jerus chose to keep the simpler 1900 home and have its cost move with
wages. Its starting construction cost, materials, points and upkeep are
about 0.29 of today's, derived from the same household on the two wage
ladders; its plot keeps the land market's price. Thereafter the home is
repriced monthly from the last twelve months' household pay, capped at
today's home. The trailing mean mattered: repricing on each month's jump
churned the landlords and stalled the city. On the measured 0.9.174 model,
the mean's city reached 1,852,269 people at month 2,400, with rent averaging
0.307 of take-home; the same pay index applied monthly without smoothing
reached only 6,037. These are that model's measurements, not a new pace
measurement after later main merges.

Without a school, young adults now learn trades at the year's historical
share. School graduates count toward that share first, so an apprentice
does not duplicate a graduate. This counts gross school diplomas: degree
completions move existing diploma-holders out of their band without reopening
apprentice places. The twelve pay observations are saved and
restored before homes are priced on load. Homes are always struck from
their catalogue figures, keeping a loaded city's costs equal to the city
that kept running. A separate reload fault found by the growing city was
also repaired: a farm fitting its last tractor clears its old captive
horse feed instead of continuing to subtract it from every harvest.

The founding screen and playtest now choose 1900 by default. All fitted
remains available, and the playtest selects today's founding with
`-Dplaytest.founded=today`. The accepted pace choice is settled. Jerus's
remaining call is to revisit the home once HO1 adds owning a home and
mortgages. The longer playtest affects gates and nightly runs: the measured
1900 run took 38 minutes on this PC against 18 for today's city; the earlier
box dry run's LongPlaytest took 248.9 seconds. The final ensemble and gate
follow the orchestrator's review and slot approvals.

The causes, alternatives, checks and exact versions are recorded in
[R1g's notes](../docs/queue/R1g-notes.md). In the game, look at the 1900 chip
on founding and the home's changing construction price and rent burden
as household pay changes; this is a visual check, not an open design call.
