# Manufacturing waits for its own recovery

Manufacturing used to compare the estimated earnings of its next works with
the cost of that works alone. That can look attractive immediately after
losses forced the sector to sell plant: a smaller remaining business can
pay for one month without proving that its market can support expansion.
The estimate is still useful, but the existing business supplies evidence
about prices and costs that the estimate alone misses.

MR1 arms a recovery hold when Manufacturing reaches the distress rule's
twenty-four consecutive operating-loss months while it is lent. It can order
new plant again after covering its running costs for twelve consecutive
closed months. A full year is the recovery dial. A short self-funded loss
spell leaves ordering unchanged. Proceeds from scrapping plant do not count
as trading recovery; interest alone does not break it. The sector can enter for
the first time without a prior record, and a business that has sold its last
plant can try again after the same window. Once the record permits an order,
the usual earnings, workers, ground and financing tests still apply. The
recovery count survives a save and opening a plant does not erase it.

This follows the option value of waiting before committing sunk capital,
rather than a claim that every real company stops investment after a
divestiture. Productive investment can continue during restructuring; the
rule here concerns additional capacity in the same sector after recent
operating losses. The evidence, controlled bleed fixture, checks and current
played-city measurements are in [the MR1 notes](../docs/queue/MR1-notes.md).
