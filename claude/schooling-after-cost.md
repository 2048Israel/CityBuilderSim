# Schooling after cost

A higher salary used to pull people into an adult course without valuing its
years out of work. A university degree could even promise the doctor's wage,
although the degree alone did not give its holder a medical licence. WG3 makes
the comparison the wages study approved: what the qualification pays after the
course's family fees, wages given up and waiting have been valued over the
working life left. A degree reads its best job open without a licence; each
professional qualification reads its own job.

Both young and established adults may study. Their expected work left comes
from the two stages already in the population model, so the same course can
be worthwhile to the young and less attractive to the established. The old
return curve and affordability rule still decide participation. High school
keeps its own coverage and affordability rule; it has no adult wage to give
up. The interest comparison uses the dearer of student-loan interest, policy
interest and the study's estimated three percent annual price of waiting.

Policy's Wages area now has The ladder beside The floor. Each qualification
shows its current paid wage and a level month after cost, with posts, holders,
course length and the course's family bill beneath it.
Choose either adult stage to see its horizon. A step may sit below its
prerequisite or extend left of zero; the picture keeps that sign. Its verdict
explains why a large lead may still point to too few seats or unaffordable
fees, and why a course with no lead will attract few students. These are
today's prices and wages held still for comparison, rather than a prediction
of what a future graduate will earn.

The old historical founding wage table remains separate and unchanged. No
new saved state or student-loan accounting is introduced by this batch.
