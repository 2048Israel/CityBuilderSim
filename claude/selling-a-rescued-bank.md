# Selling a rescued bank

The city's hand can offer the rescue book's shares to the market. Its sale
posts before the world and households choose their purchases, so they can
buy it on their usual return and savings rules. Shares bought by those
investors transfer to them and the fund receives the price paid. The
bank's own buyback still cancels the shares it buys, under its capital
policy and monthly pace.

The rule pauses its buying of a company while the hand sells it that
month. Its own bid therefore no longer appears above an ask it cannot
fill. The sale lasts for the month, as before. A price entered by the
player is binding: an investor taking an ask at 99% under fair value
really pays that cheap price. There is no guaranteed buyer or price.

A book whose best bid exceeds its best ask because trading between
those participants is barred has no meaningful midpoint. Its quote uses
the last price paid, or fair value before the first trade, and the caption
says the book is crossed. Ordinary uncrossed books retain their midpoint.

At a desk cap or the bank's own buyback capital target, rounding residue
does not buy a dust lot and set the last price. Real cheap trades retain
their actual price. Existing rescue holdings, orders and ledger records
carry the sale; no new offering state or save format is required.
