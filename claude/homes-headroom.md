# The homes' headroom

A margin over demand means homes for a little more than the jobs would
bring. Until RN1-H the landlords read that margin the other way round:
with room for 100 people they waited until demand exceeded 105, then
ordered homes only for the demand itself. Even a city able to build had
no reserve from that rule.

The plan now seeks capacity for demand times one plus
`BusinessInvestment.TARGET_HEADROOM`. At the current five percent, demand
for 100 means a target of 105 places. The reserve is five percent of
demand, or about 4.76 percent of capacity. Standing homes and homes on
site both count towards it. Building sizes, the jobs a new block brings,
the mix of studios and family doors, the rents that pay for them, land
and the lender's door still shape what actually stands vacant.

HousingCheck makes demand exactly equal capacity and proves the plan
orders the reserve, not another reserve on homes already ordered. Its
1,080-place city orders exactly nine Houses, 54 more places, then stops
at 1,134 whether those homes are on site or complete. Restoring the old
reading makes the check fail.

The same dial also names several businesses' queue thresholds. Those
wait for demand to exceed supply by five percent, then fill bare demand:
at supply 100 and demand 106, they plan to 106 rather than 111.3. RN1-H
leaves them alone. The recommended call is to name their hysteresis with
a separate dial, retaining their tested behaviour until Jerus chooses
whether those markets should also hold spare supply.
