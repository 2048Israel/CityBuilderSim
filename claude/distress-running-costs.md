# Paying plant in financial distress

A business can earn its running costs and still lose money because its debt
costs too much. The distress rule used its net-loss history, so an overdrawn
sector refused credit could sell a quarter of its biggest holding each month
even while that plant earned an operating profit.

QR1 asks for the operating-loss history as well. Plant must have lost on its
own running costs for the distress runway before this rule sells it. A
recovery, including break-even, restarts that clock. A sector with sustained
operating losses still sells at the existing quarter rate when overdrawn;
the lent price-taker bleed keeps its existing pace.

This follows the distinction between reorganizing an operating business and
liquidating it. The model already handles an unpaid overdraft through interim
financing or the bank's restructuring backstop. Financial distress still
reaches that resolution; it does not itself order the sale of paying plant.
The evidence, sources and checks are in [QR1's notes](../docs/queue/QR1-notes.md).
