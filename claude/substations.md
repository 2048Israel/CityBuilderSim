# Substations

The city's power now needs wires as well as plants. A Substation represents a district's
lines, transformers and meters and carries 100 MW at the peak. The automatic builder
orders them through the same funding, land and construction rules as roads. The free
10 MW founding network remains; beyond it, a grid short of substations leaves demand
unserved in the dispatch's existing block shortfall figures, even with ample generation.

The wires' price follows the substations standing: capital recovered over forty years
at four percent real, repairs at the builders' current prices, and posts actually filled.
These are costs the treasury already pays through construction, repairs and utility
payroll. Power bills recover them for the treasury without paying them twice. Industry
still pays the transmission share and other billed businesses the whole wires share.
The closing dispatch keeps its cost snapshot in the save so a load cannot reprice a bill
using buildings or wages from a different month.

The study prices a whole 100 MW district, so an underused one costs more per kWh
than the 5.94¢ typical-load benchmark. PW5-F1 traces this explicitly: a town with
a 45 MW billed peak pays 11.38¢ for one district. Three unnecessary districts
would charge 33.99¢ and stop its coal chain. The view fixture now supplies the
one district its load needs and runs at the native commodity prices. The game's
automatic builder already sizes from peak demand, including forecast growth.
The steel-town test's earlier trade fall came from omitted districts: it exceeded
its free founding carry and paid the shortage-cap energy price. Both hand-built
rail fixtures now stand real wires before their months; the original freight,
capacity, money and save checks remain.
