# The window against collateral

The young bank could buy a large share portfolio on its capital and pay for
it with an unlimited overnight advance. Capital tests answered whether the
bank could absorb a loss; they did not answer where the purchase money came
from. The exchange checked those capital limits at every fill, but never
checked a stable cash budget. The next monthly settlement sent the whole
shortfall past deposits to the central bank. Interest on that stock of
advances then came back to the treasury as an unusually large remittance.

The window now advances only against eligible domestic loans and government
paper after haircuts. Government paper is valued at market, loan values are
net of allowances, and equity inventory does not qualify. It also cannot
supply the stable funding needed for long assets. The bank must find term
funding abroad for that part, priced in local money from the expected policy
path and existing credit and term premia. It cannot borrow at a cheap dollar
rate while owing a local-currency principal. Those foreign loans and their
interest are separate from central-bank money in both the statements and the
money audit.

New desk purchases share one cash budget across every company and every
fill, after money that hot depositors can withdraw is taken off. Existing
inventory is refinanced rather than erased or sold below fair. The exchange
keeps its capital, company and inventory limits. Mature companies no longer
revive a stale offering quote only when earnings reach zero: their existing
income-and-spare-money valuation reaches its price floor continuously, and
the bank records any mark loss. No GDP cap, remittance cap or hidden transfer
makes the result look smaller.

The same copied month-133 city was played forward for 120 months. Before the
repair, central-bank remittances supplied 20.96% of its revenue and the bank
failed six times. In the repaired run the share was 1.41% and there were three
failures. Every month closed the unchanged cent audit, respected collateral
and kept the desk outside window funding. The copied starting stock was
preserved: this is a change in financing and future purchases, not a load-time
write-off. The full ensemble comparison is recorded in the batch notes.

Nine saved rules make the regulator's choices visible. Their starting values
are the repaired window's spread of 0.25 percentage points and its full
haircutted collateral limit, the existing 8% capital minimum, no discretionary
countercyclical buffer, a 3% leverage floor, no reserve requirement, the existing
85% mortgage loan-to-cost cap, no additional mortgage debt-to-income cap, and
the existing 90% working-capital ceiling. The conservation buffer keeps its
research date and mortgages retain their payment-coverage test. The new income
limit is against a property's annual net rent; it does not silently replace
that coverage test. Policy and the central-bank page use the same saved rules.

A single-borrower cap waits for a borrower ledger. An industry is many firms;
calling it one connected borrower once sent nearly all business credit abroad,
and that rule was withdrawn. A loan ticket or building count is no substitute
for ownership. LE1 first supplies persistent borrower and connected-group
identity across loans, mortgages, interim finance and bank-held bonds, then
applies Basel's exposure limit with its sovereign exemption.
