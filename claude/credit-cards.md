# Credit cards inside the household cell

HC1, with HC2 folded in, gives the household ledger an inside. A cell still
keeps its money per household, but a balance now belongs to a share of its
households. The amounts among those holders have a lognormal spread. The
model reads its probabilities and partial means directly; it never draws
individual families or adds a random household list.

That distinction matters for savings as much as for debts. An average buffer
does not mean everybody can pay an unexpected bill. Two cells with the same
income can contain different shares who need the card, and a cell with a
comfortable average can still contain a thin-buffered tail. Emergency bills
are paid from that spread of buffers and then from credit the bank accepts.
What is actually paid becomes one imported service purchase through Business
Services, with its seller, imported input, consumption and money-audit timing
recorded on the existing ledgers.

Jerus's agreed defaults are 21.35% at the bank's neutral price, a 3% minimum,
and a 35% cap. The minimum also covers the month's interest plus 1% principal
when that is larger. A current cardholder uses part of spare income for extra
paydown. A household unable to carry the minimum can fall behind; late
balances cure or charge off. A charge-off uses that share's savings for the
creditor first, puts the remaining loss on the bank, excludes that share
from credit for a year on the stock's decay, and sends a quarter away.
Neighbours in the same cell retain their own access. There is no longer a
cell-wide bankruptcy threshold or countdown.

The statistics distinguish holders from everybody. Means and medians are
among holders, both per household and per adult, with separate income-tier
and life-stage groups. The adult figures split each household's balance
among its actual grown-ups. The city also records debt over disposable
income and assets, required debt service, the share over the 40% burden
line, arrears and charge-offs. Yearly surveys have their own year axis.
The total-debt distribution and the 60-day-late share are the study's
explicit approximations; future owner mortgages and separate car loans
are not invented as extra debt. Today's car finance remains on the card.

The household page follows HS3's shipped stage layout. Its debt section can
be read per household or per adult, by tier and stage, for the current month
or the latest yearly survey. It leads with the share holding a card balance,
the payment burden and late balances, with the comparison by kind behind
details. Opening one household shows its own minimum and the share excluded
from credit, rather than saying every neighbour has been discharged.

These holder shares change the meaning of an existing saved balance, so
the batch raises both save-format numbers together. An older save is
refused. It is not converted into a city with balances and no borrowers.
