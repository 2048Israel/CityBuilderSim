### {next} — FC2, construction traded with the world — 2026-11-22, ready, see docs/queue/FC2-notes.md
Jerus, 2026-11-22: "make it a imported/exported good... the base cost is still in domestic currency but the excess is in foreign currency". Foreign crews are always on offer at the city's base price for a point plus an excess of 15% of the world's base price for the same work, in foreign money (`BuildersShort.FOREIGN_EXCESS`), so a strong D$ makes them cheaper and a weak one dearer.
An order's work goes abroad past the months queued at which the builders' premium reaches that excess: never under the 9-month knee, and at most 21 months whatever the currency (`SURE_MONTHS`, the excess capped at the premium at 21 months, 16%); 20.5 at par.
Each month hands them the queue at home past the threshold, and their month is their book in the threshold's months, so a 240-month backlog is built at the threshold's pace. Their excess rises with their month, half way to +80% at twice the builders' own (`SUPPLY_HALF`), and they bring their own crews.
Under the knee the builders sell the points no site took abroad, at the world's base and the city's own excess, booked once as the builders' exports in the audit, the national accounts and the current account.
The Construction screen's gauge and rows, the build card's line and the BUILDERS flag show both offers; the Trade tab carries the builders' export on a row of its own, so its sold abroad is still the balance of payments' exports. FC1's 5% floor, its double price and its quarter-month capacity are gone.
The figures rest on the World Bank's 7.5% domestic preference, the cost of flying crews in (up to 10% of a job), expatriate costs, and demand surge after disasters (10-50%).
ForeignCrewsCheck proves each bound at three rates, under domestic inflation, for the export and for a 240-month backlog; ConstructionTrapCheck moved to the new truth. A saved split factor under 1e-12 now loads as saved. No format bump.
