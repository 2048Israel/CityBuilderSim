*Five kinds of paper, each priced payment by payment on one curve built from where the market expects the central bank's dial to go; sold at an auction in which the households, the businesses, the world and the bank bid by the rules they already follow; kept on the books by effective interest; and closed early by a reverse auction or, where it was sold callable, a call. Since 0.7.13 the treasury can roll what falls due without the player's hand. What lends to everybody else is [the bank](#14-the-bank).*

Jerus asked for the rebuild on 12 October: *"better pricing, show actually what the coupon and ytm is as well as gain and loss if you close it now compared to market value and face value, and all that stuff like in real life, and a more better ui"*. It ran as six batches, CD1 to CD6 (0.8.15 to 0.8.36), each shipping its own screen (D31, Jerus: *"each CD batch ships with its own screen"*). The T-bill, the serial bond and the term loan, whose coupon was a third of the market's and whose face was grossed up to make up the difference, are gone.

| Paper | Terms | Sold | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bill | 1, 3, 6 or 12 months | lots of $1M, at a discount | no coupon; a price per 100, a discount rate and a yield |
| Bond | 2, 5, 10, 20, 30 or 50 years | lots of $100M; coupon the clearing yield rounded down to an eighth; **lines reopen**; **callable** from 10 years | a coupon a month, the face at the end |
| Amortizing bond | 5 to 30 years, in fives | lots of $10M | principal in equal yearly slices, one coupon on what is left |
| Floating-rate note | 2 or 5 years | lots of $100M | a month's policy rate plus a margin fixed at the sale |
| Inflation-linked bond | 10 or 30 years | lots of $100M | a real coupon on a principal that follows prices, never repaid under the face |
| Dollar bill or bond | the same terms | to the world, not at auction; treasury only | as the bill and the bond |

Bills, amortizing bonds, floaters and linkers are not reopened: each sale is its own line. Jerus chose the lineup (*"Lineup + extras"*), the bond terms (*"2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 50"*, so the 40-year goes) and the monthly coupon, *"monthly, i know unrealistic but makes the game alot more coherent"*, so there is no accrued interest and a piece has one price.

> **Why the sale and the buyback read one curve**
>
> The money button has been found here twice. The old simple-interest premium made issue-then-repurchase free cash, $2,145 at ten years and $28,059 at thirty, until the face was solved from present value (0.7.1). And CD2's review found a sale priced at one flat yield against a buyback priced on the curve gaining about 2 basis points on a rising curve, from the coupon's rounding down. The sale is priced on the curve now, the stop over fair as a spread on every month's zero rate (`YieldCurve.pvAtSpread()`), so a sale with no tail is worth exactly the fair value a buyback starts from (`DebtManager.marketValue()`) and a round trip loses the fee, the tail and what the holders ask over fair, and nothing else (`RestructureCheck`, `AuctionCheck`).

Two independent measures move the rest, debt against a year of GDP and against a year of tax, each a linear ramp to five points of spread (`MAX_SPREAD_PER_MEASURE`), full at a hundred and fifty years of the thing (`FULL_STRESS_MULTIPLE`). The city's overdraft counts as principal, and since 0.7.0 so does what it owes its central bank in advances. The cheapest money the market will offer is 0.5% (`MIN_RATE`). Every quote is struck with the issue on the books, the spread reading what the city would owe once the paper is sold (`DebtManager.quoteCurve()`), so asking for twice as much is charged more than twice as much and what the screen shows is what gets booked. A letter grade, AAA to CCC, reports the city's place within the market's own band, and since 0.7.14 a reloaded city's rate is the live city's to the bit.

**The curve** is built from the dial the market expects. Jerus, 0.7.1: *"the short term rates, aka the one you choose, those should be basically the tbill rate, the others change just as in real life."* Until 0.8.15 it sat on the dial in parallel, so a dial held low by hand bought thirty-year money at 2.27% in a city whose rule said 7.5%. Since CD1 every price of the city's paper is read off `DebtManager.curve()`, which `YieldCurve` builds:

```
pi(t)  = pi_inf + (pi_now - pi_inf) * e^(-t / ADAPT_MONTHS)      pi_inf: where the city expects inflation to settle
p(t)   = rule(pi(t)) + (floor now - rule(pi_now)) * e^(-t / DRIFT_MONTHS)     the dial the market expects
f(t)   = p(t) + the two credit spreads, between MIN_RATE and the ceiling      the forward; f(0) is today's quote
z(m)   = the compounded forwards + termPremium(m) - compression(m)           curveRate(m), the zero rate
d(m)   = (1 + z(m)/12)^(-m)                                                  what a payment m months off is worth
```

`ADAPT_MONTHS` is 12 and `YieldCurve.DRIFT_MONTHS` 6, Jerus's to move. His call (D9) was that the market expects the dial to drift back to the rule **whoever holds it**, and of the construction, *"if it is realistic then yes"*. The month strikes the two inputs, the year's inflation and the inflation the city expects, once, straight after the autopilot sets the dial (`Game.strikeTheCurve()`), held for the month and saved; on the autopilot the gap is nothing and the path is the rule's. *Zero rates a year, as `CurveCheck` holds them to the hundredth:*

A calm city prices as the old curve did. A hiking city's curve inverts, its ten-year 1.6 points under the parallel curve's 7.62%, because the market expects the rule to cut as inflation falls; and a dial held low by hand buys no cheap long money, the behind city's ten-year being 5.35% where it was 1.62%. On the default playtest the curve was inverted in 1,658 of the run's 4,000-odd months and steep in 441: inversion is the common case on the autopilot. Every payment is discounted at its own month's factor, so a piece's price is the sum (`DebtManager.marketValue()`, dollar paper on the world's curve) and its yield is solved from the price, a result and not the rate it was handed (`yieldOf()`). `BondMarket` reads the curve as the benchmark under every corporate bond, and `getRate()` stays the short end.

*The term premium, in points over the dial — DebtManager.TERM_PREMIUM_10Y … 50Y, provisional, Jerus's numbers to settle*

The credit spread is the same at every maturity, so a city that over-borrows pays more everywhere, and paper on the books keeps its coupon and its months. Other terms are refused in words: *"Bonds are issued at 2, 5, 10, 20, 30 or 50 years."*

### Selling it: the auction

Since 0.8.17 (CD2) the city's paper is sold at an auction (`Auction`, pure), where until then the households took a share at the quoted rate and the bank the rest by construction, whatever its room, and nobody could say what an issue cost to sell. The city names the kind, the term, the face and the highest yield it will take; each bidder bids by a rule it already follows; and the sale clears at **one yield** (Jerus, D5: *"one clearing yield"*), the lowest at which the bids cover the issue.

| Bidder | Its rule |
| --- | --- |
| Households, first | `HOUSEHOLD_PAPER_APPETITE` (20): a fifth of the issue for each point the yield pays over the deposit rate, out of savings past their cushion, never more than the issue; filled in full before anyone else (D32, Jerus: *"yes take all three as you listed"*; before, the rule was read at fair and capped at half the issue) |
| Businesses | `OutwardInvestment`'s rule with the city's paper beside the deposit and abroad: `APPETITE` (40) of their idle wealth per unit over the better of the two, to `MAX_SHARE` (90%), less what they hold, at `OUT_SPEED` (4% of the gap) |
| The world, as hot money | `CapitalFlows`' rule: the stock it would hold at the excess of the better of the deposit rate and the yield over the world's rate and the country premium (to `MAX_SPREAD`, 25 points), less the stock here, at `ARRIVAL_SPEED` (8%); nothing in a run |
| The bank | its room at fair: equity over `CAPITAL_RATIO` (8%) less its weighted book, over `RISK_CITY` (20%), plus what its funding costs past the floor; with no equity yet it takes the rest, funded at the window; in resolution it bids nothing |
| The central bank | never, bar its add-on rolling its own maturing paper (D8, arm's length) |

**Nobody bids under fair**, since the paper trades at fair next month. **The stop** is the lowest yield at which the bids cover the issue, never under fair and never over the highest yield taken; the households are filled first and the others pro rata. **The tail** is the stop over fair, what selling cost; **the cover** is what was bid up to the highest yield over the issue. **The highest yield** is three chips: fair plus a point (`Auction.MAX_TAIL`, D27, Jerus's to move), a yield named, or **Fill it**, Jerus's own on 12 October: *"increased yield if you allow for it to get fully filled"*. Capped, a sale the bids do not reach sells only what was bid, and a red card says so before the button; filled, the stop rises until the bidders' rules cover the issue, up to the world's rate plus its premium plus `MAX_SPREAD`, where no rule adds anything. A short sale under the minimum issue, or one that raises nothing, is no sale.

**What it sells.** A new bond line's coupon is the stop rounded **down** to an eighth (`COUPON_STEP`, D2), so it sells just under 100: in the calm city a D$300M ten-year stops at 3.602%, carries 3½% and sells at 99.148 for D$297.4M. A sale at a term whose line has nine tenths of its life left (`BENCHMARK_LIFE`, D14: 108 months of a ten-year) **reopens** it (Jerus's call, D6), at its coupon and date and the stop's price; a line is named as a treasury names it, *3½% Jan 2211*. A bill's ask is the cash, its face the lot that brings it; an amortizing bond's coupon is the stop itself. **What it costs:** no underwriter, so the 0.75% spread goes from the city's paper (D21; the businesses' bonds keep it); the fixed cost stays, $12k at founding prices struck at the expected price level (`Game.FIXED_ISSUE_COST`), off the proceeds and never added to the face; and **the minimum issue**, a twentieth of a year of tax with a floor so a young city can borrow.

In the study's model (D33) a D$300M ten-year with the bank's room nearly gone stops 28 basis points over fair; before D32 and the firms it sold only D$247.8M at its cap, a point over, and filled five points over. It takes D$600M to sell short at the cap or to fill at a tail of 139. The game's count: over 1,200 months of the automatic builder's city 82 sales raised D$40.0B of D$40.0B asked, every one at fair. `AuctionCheck` (350 assertions, five mutations each red) holds the rules, the caps and the quote that is the deal. The default playtest ended at 573,349 people, the ensemble at 23 of 25.

- **The rest is issued pro rata, at an auction, sized to raise the cash it needs** (Jerus: *"Enough to raise the cash"*). In the same structure each piece rolls into its own kind: a bill into the nearest bill, a bond into the nearest of the six terms (reopening its line), an amortizing instalment into a new amortizing bond of the nearest of its years, a floater into a floater, a linker into a linker, dollars into dollars or, with the window shut, into local paper of the same term; on 12-month bills everything becomes a local twelve-month bill. Below the smallest issue nothing is issued and the share is paid from cash.

**The rollover sells at its own cap** (D28, Jerus: *"each its own"*): its card on the hub has the same chips as Borrow. **A short sale leaves the maturity paid** (D29): it is a promise, paid in full from the cash and past it from the central bank's advances, and the inbox says so: *"The rollover's 10-year sale covered D$X at its cap of Y%; D$Z of January's maturity was paid by the central bank's advances."* The central bank still rolls its own par at issue, outside the auction, and rolling for cash still capitalises interest.

**Automatic building sells on the same paper** (D30, Jerus: *"stays open the ask until money raised, and what already raised plus cash can be used"*): the gap on the twenty-year bond (`Game.BUILD_BOND_YEARS`) at its own cap. A short sale sells what was bid and **the ask stays open**: the order is placed once what was raised for it plus the cash over its reserve covers it, the ask offered again each month at the builder's cap; if the order drops out the ask is withdrawn and what was raised stays in the treasury, the paper being sold. The panel lists the open asks with Withdraw. **The build screen's funding page** is the auction's preview for a six-month bill (`BUILD_NOTE_MONTHS`) or the twenty-year bond, sized to the run's gap; a short one offers a higher cap, Fill it, or not borrowing, and the button books exactly the quote. The time skip's report lists every automatic sale.

> **What limits the paper at home**
>
> Until CD2 nothing did: the price stopped at the dial plus ten points and the term premium, the bank bought what the households left at a window with no cap, and debt service was a promise paid past the advances ceiling. Lean seed 0's $476.6T was that, and so were the spirals of 0.7.14 and 0.7.15. Now a buyer can refuse: at the default cap a sale sells what the bidders bid within a point of fair, and Fill it stops where no rule bids more. But the promise is still paid from the advances, and no batch since has measured the Lean city again. A debt-service rule at home is Jerus's to design.

Jerus: *"yes households should be able to hold."* Since 0.7.1 every domestic piece says who holds it, and since CD2 five holders are named on every line, each as the auction allotted it and paid at the settle. **The households** buy first and in full; **the bank** buys the room it has at fair; **the central bank** never buys at issue, since buying from its own treasury is printing, bar the par of its own maturing paper, taken again as an add-on at the issue's price. **The businesses** hold what they buy on a paper book a sector (`OutwardInvestment`), paid pro rata, and sell it to the bank's desk when the spread goes, when a sector borrows or after its dollars. **The world** holds what it buys on `CapitalFlows`' paper book, outside the bank's room: a financial inflow when bought, its coupons an income debit, its principal and departures outflows. Coupons and principal follow the shares struck before the payment: the households' into savings as untaxed investment income, the central bank's destroyed and remitted. A household short of money sells its paper back to the bank's desk before its dollars and shares, and a central bank buying past what the bank holds takes the households' paper, then the businesses' and, last, the world's (`HoldersCheck`).

**The discount accretes** since 0.7.1, and since CD3 by effective interest ([the books](#the-books)). A term bond's discount was once booked as one month's interest at the settle: $60M in one month on seed 0, taxed, 45% of it payable to savers. The bank keeps its own straight-line accretion (`Bank.unearnedDiscount`) on purpose: moving it would change its income, tax and dividends, and the bank's books are another study's.

### The books

Since 0.8.29 (CD3) the paper is kept by amortised cost on effective interest (Jerus, D10: *"effective interest"*). Each piece carries a carrying value and an effective rate. At the sale the carrying value is what the treasury received, net of the fixed cost, and the effective rate is the yield at which the remaining payments are worth that (`Debt.effectiveRateOf()`). Every month, before the payments, `Debt.amortise()` books carrying value times rate over twelve as the expense, the coupon as its cash part and the rest as the discount wearing off (a premium runs the other way), and steps the carrying value by the expense less the payment; the last month takes the rounding, so a piece closes to nothing. The calm D$300M ten-year at 99.148, effective 3.602%, books D$893k in month 1 and D$900k in month 119 against a coupon of D$875k. A reopening solves one **blended** rate on the whole line (D16): fungible paper is one liability. Dollar paper is kept in dollars, and what the D$ does to it is a revaluation, not interest.

**On the statements.** The budget's "Interest on the city's paper" is the coupons in cash plus the discount wearing off (`NationalAccounts.getInterestExpense()`); "Gain (loss) on debt retired" is carrying value less price; both are in the balance the model reads. The bridge has a row for what was no cash: *change = surplus + not-cash + raised − repaid + journal + residual*. The balance carries the paper at what the books carry it at (`getCarryingValue()`), with its fair value as a note, what the market would pay, which a government discloses and does not book. A gain or a loss is a plain figure, never coloured. The audit counts cash and these are none: `MoneyCheck` sells, reopens and buys back for 36 months with every audit closed, and `TreasuryCheck` 9 holds the calm ten-year's figures, a premium, dollar paper and a buyback through a save. `SAVE_FORMAT` and its floor are 44, an older piece having no carrying value. Of CD3's open calls Jerus took one, *"★5 to CD4"*: the year's "Paid out" was off by a buyback's gain, so History keeps the gain in a key of its own.

### Closing early, and the call

Since 0.8.33 (CD4) a piece is held to its date, bought back in a **reverse auction**, or, if sold callable, **called**. Before, a buyback cleared a whole piece at fair and paid every holder its pro rata share, the households who held it for a spread bought out at the bank's price.

**The reverse auction** (`Buyback`, pure). The city names the face of one line it wants back and, if it likes, the most it will pay per 100. Every holder but the central bank offers what it holds in ten slices, each at the price where its own buying rule would stop wanting it, taken at the holding that remains, and never under fair (D17): the households at the deposit rate plus the spread their remaining share would still ask; the businesses at the better of the deposit and the world plus their remaining share; the world at its rate plus its premium plus what its remaining stock asks; the bank at fair (D34, Jerus's call: the half spread was the share desk's, not the bond's); dollar paper's world at fair (D26); the central bank not at all (D18). The city takes the cheapest first and **pays each its own price** (D19); the stop is the dearest taken. `Game.quoteBuyback()` strikes it once and `buyBack()` pays exactly that, in cash only: a buyback dearer than the cash is refused. Any part of a line may go (`Debt.retirePart()`), a line with an unsettled sale only whole.

**The call.** A bond of 10 years or more may be sold callable (D22, Jerus: *"market + callable"*): first call at 5 years for a ten-year and 10 for a 20-, 30- or 50-year, whole, at 100. The right is the city's, so the coupon pays for it: the option is priced on a Ho-Lee tree fitted to the curve's discount factors (`CallTree`), at the volatility of the city's own quoted rate over 60 months, never under 0.75 points (`RATE_VOL_FLOOR`, D23, a number to settle). On the calm curve it adds 34.7 and 58.4 basis points to a ten- and a thirty-year at that floor and 49.4 and 82.0 at one point. A callable sale reopens only a callable line of its own call, and the buyback's fair and the balance's fair value see the option. From its first call month the city calls the line whole, in cash, paying every holder its face, the central bank included, since a call is the paper's own term. It is **worth calling** when the payments left on today's curve, as a bond that cannot be called, less the face and the cost of selling again, pass 3% of the face (`WORTH_CALLING`, D24, US municipal practice).

**What it books.** Each holder is paid the sum of its own slices; the gain against carrying value, "Gain (loss) on debt retired", is no cash. A buyback is read three ways: against carrying value (booked), against face (what debt-to-GDP sees) and over fair (what the auction cost). Beside them an **honest line**, since a buyback at fair moves nothing in present value, only when the city pays: *it stops D$81k a month of coupon; D$17.2M borrowed again at today's 8.5-year rate, 13.42%, would cost D$192k; so it books a loss and raises the city's interest by D$112k a month* (a rendered city's). The default playtest never buys back, so the ensemble was not run.

### Floating and linked paper

Since 0.8.35 (CD5) the auction sells two more kinds (Jerus: *"floating-rate notes on the policy rate"*; D25). **A floating-rate note** (`FloatingRateNote`) pays each month on the face the policy rate plus a margin fixed at the sale, never under nothing; it sells near 100 whatever the dial does and the city carries the dial's risk: +150 basis points moves its price −0.12 per 100 against −0.76 for a fixed two-year bond, since the coupon reads the dial of the month it is paid. **An inflation-linked bond** (`InflationLinkedBond`) pays a real coupon on the face times the index ratio, and at the end the face plus an uplift of the face times (the larger of 1 and the ratio, less 1): the deflation floor is the larger, as TIPS have it.

At the auction fair is the term's par yield and the ask carries the coupon that prices the paper at 100 on the curve (`fairMargin()`, `fairRealCoupon()`); a floater's margin is that plus the tail, a linker's real coupon the same rounded down to an eighth. On the calm city the game gives policy plus 17.9 and 34.2 basis points at two and five years and real coupons of 1.64% and 2.19% at ten and thirty (the study: 18 and 34, 1.61% and 2.17%). The market prices them on what it expects: a floater's coupons on the dial it expects, a linker's index on the inflation it expects (`DebtManager.pricingFlows()`), which `marketValue()`, `yieldOf()` and the buyback's offers read. A rollover in the same structure rolls a floater into a floater and a linker into a linker; none sells abroad or unasked, so the default playtest's trace is byte-identical to main's.

**The books.** A floater's follow the known schedule, today's coupon held flat (IFRS 9 B5.4.5): when the dial moves, the effective rate is solved again at the carrying value. A linker's carrying value catches up each month to the known schedule's present value at its unchanged real rate (B5.4.6), the change being the month's **inflation uplift**, below nothing in a deflation month, read with a month's lag. The budget has it as "Inflation uplift on linked bonds", paid in cash only at maturity and off the bridge's not-cash row; the credit judgement sees all the city owes (`getPricedDebt()` adds the indexation past the face). Over a linker's life interest plus uplift equals the cash coupons plus the face less what the sale raised, to the cent, in a rising index and a falling one (`FloatLinkCheck`, 441 assertions).

### The screens

**The hub, and the position.** COUPON is the cash the paper pays next month and says so, its tooltip putting the budget's own line beside it (*the budget charged D$X of interest last month, the coupons and the discount wearing off*), since the interest on the statement is not the coupon. The balance reads *Owed, as carried*, with *at face · carried · worth today* under the bars; Debt service reads *a month's coupon, in cash*; Home & abroad's WHO HOLDS IT names the households, the bank, the central bank, the businesses and the world.

**The book, as a desk** (`Finances › The book`, 0.8.36; Jerus, D31: *"CD6 keeps the book as a desk, a line's page, Your rate and the manual"*). It was a card a piece, each with its own Buy back. Five figures stand over it: OWED AT FACE, CARRIED, WORTH TODAY on today's curve, the COUPON a month in cash, and A POINT ON THE CURVE, what a point on every rate takes off the book's worth.
