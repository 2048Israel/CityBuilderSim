# RW1 - the world's delay (a study)

Jerus, 2026-11-22 (desk chat): "additionaly research, what about makin it so
that you can find research 10 years early, but if you dont acquire it, the
world would give it to you roughly 20 years after they found it. do you think
that would improve research?" Then: "sure queue it".

## What is true today

A city can find a code from history's date less its field's lag
(`Research.LAG_YEARS_*`: engineering 7, medicine 9, business and law 4). A
code it has not found becomes its own from abroad on history's date. So a city
that never researches loses nothing, and that is the weakness this answers.

## What the study settles

- **The early window:** 10 years for every field, weighed against the lag
  dials' evidence (MANS91, `docs/studies/research-design.md`).
- **The gift's delay:** about 20 years, weighed against research-design.md's
  diffusion evidence ([CM]: technologies from before 1950 reached the average
  country after 44 years, those after 1950 in 7-18).
  - Should the delay shrink with the year?
  - Should it be drawn per code from the seed?
- **Prerequisites:** does a late gift arrive regardless of its prerequisites,
  or in their order?
- **A machine bought abroad against a code known here:** can a sector use
  imported equipment before the city knows the code?
- **What a city that never researches loses:**
  - The 1900 city: the tractor in 1937 rather than 1917, on top of R1g.
  - Today's start.
  - Both measured on the 2,400-month playtest and a short ensemble, with the
    delay and without it.
- **The screens and the save:**
  - On ResearchView, "from abroad on history's date" becomes the arrival
    date.
  - The save should derive the arrival from history's date if it can, with no
    new field.
- **Whether it waits on EDU2's founding faculty:** a city with no research
  posts cannot escape the delay.
