## changelog
### {next} — PW3, wind and solar by place and season, and the power planner — 2026-11-24, ready, see docs/queue/PW3-notes.md
Wind, solar and the rivers now run by calendar month (EIA's 2025 fleet), the sun by latitude and rack (PVGIS 5.3, CC BY 4.0), and a city south of the equator on its own season. A wind or solar farm's firm credit falls with the variable rows' share of the energy, to a tenth at half. The power planner, in the advice and the automatic builder alike, runs the reserve test first: firm supply against the year's peak hour with a 15% margin, the works' own power counted, a plant on site counted from its opening, and each plant at its own posts' fill. Among the firm rows its order is the one cheapest a firm kW-year for the slice of the load the gap lies in: coal short at the base, a peaker short at the peak. A wind or solar farm counts at its falling credit, so wind alone never passes the test; where one farm's credit closes the gap and its year's cost, less the running it saves, is below the firm pick's, the farm is the order. A gap under three tenths of a plant (Jerus's share for oversized works) orders it only when the peak of the year's highest month is short at the plant's own completion, and a gap only the planner's horizon and margin project waits, so a small town is no longer sold, on credit, a plant many times the size of its grid; and the other works wait on power only when that month's peak is short of it. A power site past its deadline is no longer dropped whole: each plant on it counts from its revised opening, so a late coal plant is not ordered twice. When the test passes, the spread orders one more plant where re-dispatching the year shows it saves more than it costs: wind beside diesel, nothing beside coal. A large plant's ground is assembled past the listing, up to 96 offers, priced at the whole offers' own prices before any money moves; an order that cannot be placed buys no ground and sells no bond. Research E25, the single-axis tracker, lifts a solar farm from the fixed rack's yield. PowerPlanCheck is new.
