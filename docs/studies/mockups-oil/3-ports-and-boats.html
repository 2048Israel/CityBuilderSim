Mockup 3 — ports and boats on the map

The port is drawn on the coast the city owns, one terminal per kind. Boats sail in from the map's edge full (imports, blue) and out full (exports, orange). Bulk carriers and tankers carry one way, so they make the other leg empty (grey, riding high). Container ships carry both ways (white). The big city of mockups 1 and 2, same month.

Only what the city has: the number of boats is the number of calls the city's own trade makes, by report §4.3: calls a month = tonnes a month ÷ the cargo of one ship of the class that carries it. Only if you're looking: a call is a schedule entry, not an object. The map draws the boats whose route is on screen at this moment and nothing else (see the inset).

Zoom 1 — district level. About 7.6 km across, so 1 px ≈ 6 m. An Aframax (245 m) is 40 px long.
Zoom 2 — close. About 1.3 km across, so 1 px ≈ 1 m. The tanker terminal: one Aframax unloading crude at T1, an MR coming in with two tugs for T2.
  • Boat count follows trade. 15.8 calls a month in this city: 1.9 Aframax (crude in), 0.9 MR (petrol and diesel in), 0.8 LR1 (fuel oil out), 0.4 MR (jet out), 0.8 gas carriers (LPG out), 0.8 small bitumen tankers, 5.9 Capesize (iron ore out) and 4.3 feeders (containers). A fraction is a rate: 0.4 calls a month is one call every 2½ months.
  • Time on the map. If one game month plays as about 60 s of map time (★ decision Q8), a month's 15.8 calls means about one boat every 4 s across the screen at district zoom. The berth times from report §4.2 decide how long each one stays alongside.
  • Positions are a pure function of time: position = route(call, t). Arrival times are spread through the month by a hash of (good, month, call number), so panning away and back shows the same boat in the same place, and no boat is ever simulated off screen.
  • Close zoom is the same schedule drawn bigger. Tugs, mooring lines and loading arms are decoration that appears only at this zoom.
