Mockup 2 — the oil industry screen

One page for the whole chain: the city's wells by type, the refinery's units, and every product's price and balance. It is the big city of mockup 1 (State B), same month, same figures. It reaches from Sectors, from Oil or Refining, and from the Land office's oil deposits.

The wells chart answers "how long does our own crude last?". Past years are solid; the next ten are pale and show the decline if nothing new is built. The units table answers "why does the refinery build what it builds?". Each unit shows its spread now (report §2.3), and each one not built shows the gate that stops it.

  • Decline: land wells fall about 10% a year as a group once drilling stops (est.). Platforms ramp for a year, hold a 3-year plateau, then fall 8.5% a year (IEA's shallow-offshore rate). Reserves left = what these wells would lift to the end of their fields.
  • The units' spread is the value of what a unit makes minus the value of its feed, per litre of feed, at the city's own prices: the import price for a product the city is short of, the export price for one it has spare. That one rule makes demand pull the refinery.
  • Where it went in the product table lists the same buyers as mockup 1's ribbons, so the two pages can't disagree.
