# Research: the catalogue's years, 1996-2024

*2026-11-16. A design study, R6-G. It sets out the rows the research
catalogue needs between the online shop (B8, 1995) and the near future (FE1,
May 2025), as `research-1900.md` did for 1900-1950. It changes no code and
adds no row to `research.json`. A row enters in the batch that builds what it
gives (R6 ★6), and each row below names that batch. Read it after
`research-near-future.md` (§2 the rules, §6 the gap as R6 saw it) and
`research-design.md` §2. The gatherers' fact sheets are in
`research-1996-2024/`. They give every source and say whether its figure was
seen on the page.*

## 0. In short

1. **Thirty rows stand, out of fifty-seven subjects gathered.** Twelve are
   engineering, twelve medicine, two business and four law. Each has a first
   commercial use with its date, a measured effect and a source that could be
   read (section 3).
2. **Thirteen have something in the game to act on today.** They go to two
   queued batches: R6e takes eight engineering rows, and R6f takes five rows
   from medicine, business and law. The other seventeen wait on a building, a
   cause of death or a lever the game does not have yet. Each is marked with
   the batch that owns that thing (section 8).
3. **Most of this period's medicine acts on deaths the game stopped counting
   in 1980.** `CausesOfDeath` takes typhoid, the summer diarrhoea, the
   childhood outbreaks, TB, pneumonia and childbirth to nothing by 1980. Its
   causes include no cancer, so these rows have nothing to work on:
   - the three vaccines (PCV7, rotavirus, RSV);
   - the TB cure;
   - imatinib, the HPV vaccine and checkpoint immunotherapy.

   MED1, the health study already queued, is the place for both (★2, ★3).
4. **R6b's three rows that lean on these years are called out.** FE5, the
   hundred-hour battery, needs E29, the four-hour grid battery. FM1, the mRNA
   flu vaccine, needs M20, the mRNA vaccine. FM2, the personalised cancer
   vaccine, needs M20 and M17, checkpoint immunotherapy (section 8.3).
5. **Business and law have few rows, and only because the game has few
   handles.** Most of the period's business change is information: the
   smartphone, the cloud and e-invoicing. Measured, it gives a correlation
   and no step. Several of the period's laws were never Canada's: negative
   rates, the congestion charge, a pension age tied to life expectancy
   (section 4.3).
6. **The work rule holds.** A building of 1996 costs 4,303 researcher-years
   (D$0.9 bn); one of 2024 costs 18,271 (D$4.0 bn). This is the same rule
   that gives R6's 2027 building its 21,333 (section 6).

## 1. What was asked, and how it was done

R6's ★5 asked for this study before R6b's rows that need it. The orchestrator
queued it as R6-G on 2026-11-16, with the near-future study's section 6 as its
start: about forty-five subjects named, twenty-nine of them with a figure.

The web research went to four Sonnet agents at xhigh, one per area: energy;
industry and transport; medicine; business and law. Each wrote a fact sheet
with its sources and marked every figure as seen on the page or only in a
search result. Between them the agents covered 57 subjects. The sheets are
committed beside this note as they were gathered (134 KB). A later study
starts from them and does not pay for the search again.

Three dates resting on search snippets were looked up again here (section 9,
H1-H3): PCV7 (CDC gives February 2000 only), Manitoba's smoking ban (1 Oct
2004, on the province's archived page) and ipilimumab (25 Mar 2011, a
society's release on the day).

## 2. The rules a row is held to

The near-future study's rules are kept. The ★ numbers below are R6's, as
taken.

- **★1, the central date.** A row has one date, the one history gives. There
  is no window, because these are past years.
- **★2, the date is the first commercial-scale operation.** For a building or
  a piece of equipment that is the first plant or fleet run for money, not a
  prototype or a demonstration. For a drug it is the first approval, and for
  a law the day it came into force. Tehachapi's battery of 2014 is a
  demonstration, so the battery row is Mira Loma's of 2017. Siguaney's cement
  of 2013 is a trial, so the cement row is Argos's of 2020.
- **★6, a row enters `research.json` only in the batch that builds what it
  gives.** A row of these years is history, so it is built in the "undone"
  pattern R2e used for its recipe changes. Until the row is known, the
  building runs at the figure from before the row. When the row is known it
  runs at the game's figure. The game's figure is today's, so no new number
  enters after the row.
- **★7, codes.** E, M, B and L keep their own series. The next free numbers
  are E21, M11, B9 and L6; R2e holds E19 and E20. Section 5 reserves
  the numbers.
- **★8, the work rule** of `ResearchCatalog.workByRule`, unchanged (section
  6).
- **A law opens on Canada's date**, as in research-1900 §7.2. The world's first
  use is given beside it.
- **A row needs a measured effect.** A builder's or trial's figure counts at
  pilot scale or above. A target does not count, and neither does a
  correlation. A subject without a measured effect is in section 4.2.
- **Every figure has a source that could be read**, marked page, snippet or
  memory, as R6's are. A missing figure is said to be missing. Facts are
  nobody's to own and no wording is copied (`licences.md`). Journals,
  agencies and companies' own releases are cited for their facts only.

### 3.3 Row by row

What the table cannot hold: the date's evidence, the figure's limits, and
what the batch that builds the row must still find. "The sheet" means the
gatherer's sheet for the row's area.

**Engineering**

- **E21, GM crops.** USDA's ERS dates commercial seed to 1996 and gives no
  month (G-I2). Klümper and Qaim's meta-analysis was opened. Its abstract says
  yield +22% and its body says +21%, and the same one-point gap appears in
  profit (+68% and +69%) (G-I1). This is research-design's reserve [T44],
  now with a code. The pesticide figure applies only where the farm buys
  chemicals. Seed and technology fees were not found.
- **E22, the pressure exchanger.** It was first installed in the Canary
  Islands in 1997 (G-E42). Since about 2000 isobaric devices have replaced
  turbines in most new plants (G-E41). The game's Desalination Plant is
  flash distillation (E6, 1957, 13.5-25.5 kWh a m³). It needs reverse osmosis
  first, research-design's reserve [T52] of 1977, which takes E33 (★1). The
  reserve's "2-3 kWh a m³" is today's plant, with the exchanger in it. R6e
  must therefore split it: reverse osmosis takes the plant to the Francis
  figure (6.2-6.7), and E22 takes it to 3.0-5.3.
- **E23, autosteer.** The date, 1999, is AutoFarm's, from a trade timeline;
  a second source says "around 2000" (G-I3). Deere's AutoTrac followed in
  2002 (G-I4). The figures come from a peer-reviewed Finnish field trial with
  a tractor and seeder (G-I5). They are one trial's, in small fields.
- **E24, the large-rotor turbine.** No first installation of the class was
  found. Enercon's 1.5 MW machine dates from 1995 (sheet, energy 2). The row
  therefore takes the date at which LBNL's capacity factor by build year
  steps up, 2004 (G-E9; ★6). This is research-design's reserve [T49]. The
  game's wind farm (E16) runs at 33.8% from 1980 (spec-electricity). Before
  the row it runs at 23%, after it at the game's figure. The 40% of
  2013-2021 is above the game's figure, and PW3, which keeps the factor by
  place and season, owns that.
- **E25, the tracker.** Nellis was dedicated on 17 Dec 2007; the day comes
  from a search summary and the size and tracking from the page (G-E24). The
  first two utility-scale projects reached operation in late 2007 (sheet).
  The range +15-25% is the model's (G-E23); LBNL gives the fleet's share of
  trackers (G-E22). PW2 builds the solar farm at today's figures
  (spec-electricity), so the row is PW2's if it ships first and PW3's if not.
- **E26, the driverless haul truck.** Komatsu calls January 2008 at
  Gabriela Mistral the first commercial deployment, after a trial from 2005
  (G-I12; the builder's own words). The +12% productivity and -13% cost are
  Rio Tinto's (G-I13). The net change in drivers' posts was not found.
  Before R6e changes a post, it must find that figure; the output per post
  can move on +12% alone.
- **E27, endless strip production.** The -45% was measured on the line, but
  against the conventional route: a slab cast, stored, reheated and rolled
  (G-I31, G-I32). The game's mini-mill already has the thin-slab caster
  (E18, reheating 2.0 down to 0.5-0.8 MBtu a tonne). The step against that
  was not found. The builder says the line needs no gas tunnel furnace, only
  a 10 m induction heater (snippet, sheet). R6e builds the row if it finds
  the induction heater's power. If not, the row goes to section 4.2.
- **E28, 60% combined cycle.** Irsching 4 set its record in a test in May
  2011 and was handed to its owner in July 2011. The commercial-operation day
  is not on the page (G-E46). Bouchain reached 62.22% in 2016 (sheet). The
  gas plant is PW2's (the gas part came from PW7).
- **E29, the four-hour battery.** Mira Loma came online in late December
  2016 or January 2017, on unnamed sources (G-E30). The 82% round trip is
  EIA's figure for the US fleet in 2019 (G-E34). Tehachapi, 8 MW / 32 MWh in
  2014, was a demonstration (sheet). The game has no storage:
  spec-electricity leaves it out. So the row waits on a storage batch (★4),
  which FE5 needs too.
- **E30, calcined-clay cement.** Siguaney's 2013 trial put clinker at 50%
  (G-I18). Argos's plant at Rio Claro, opened in February 2020, is the first
  commercial one (G-I19). Its -30% energy is the company's and gives no
  baseline. Ordinary cement's clinker share was not found on a page (about
  95% for pure Portland cement, from memory), and R6e opens it first.
- **E31, the tandem module.** Oxford PV made its first commercial shipment
  on 5 Sep 2024, from the Brandenburg line (G-E35). Fraunhofer gives the
  silicon average of 22.7% (G-E26). FE9, research-speculative's 30% tandem, needs this row.
- **E32, the electric long-haul truck.** The builder says "the end of 2024"
  at Wörth (G-I46); a trade report says November and names Mannheim (sheet).
  The 1.03 kWh a km is a measured run (G-I47). ICCT's diesel baseline, 33.1
  L a 100 km, is from a snippet (G-I51). It matches `Haulage`'s
  `DIESEL_LITRES_PER_100_KM` of 33, so the game's figure needs no change.
  Tesla's Semi of December 2022 had about 36 trucks out by the end of 2023
  (sheet), not a series. FE1, the driverless truck, is at the same depot
  (R6b).

**Medicine**

- **M11, PCV7.** It was licensed in February 2000 (G-M7, H1; the day, 17 Feb,
  is from a snippet only). Efficacy comes from Black et al., the vaccine's
  own trial (G-M8). The fall in admissions comes from Grijalva et al., the
  US national data (G-M9).
- **M12, robot surgery.** The da Vinci system was cleared in July 2000
  (G-M38). The figures are a 2024 meta-analysis of randomised trials (G-M39).
  The share of the stays that are robotic was not found. R6f sets the row in
  the pattern of R2f's stays rows (`STAYS_BEFORE_LATER_ROW`): general care's
  stays, which reach today's in September 1987, run a little longer until
  the row, by the per-operation saving times that share. R6f opens the share
  first.
- **M13, imatinib.** It was approved on 10 May 2001 (G-M18). The 89% is from
  the five-year follow-up (G-M20).
- **M14, the drug-coated stent.** It was approved on 24 Apr 2003 (G-M35).
  SIRIUS gives 4.1% against 16.6% (G-M37). It needs M7,
  angioplasty.
- **M15, rotavirus.** RotaTeq was licensed in February 2006 (G-M12). REST
  gives 98% against severe disease and -75% stays (G-M11). Cortes et al.
  measured the fall in US use (G-M13).
- **M16, HPV.** Gardasil was licensed on 8 Jun 2006 (G-M33). The -87% is
  England's national programme, in girls offered the vaccine at 12-13; it
  is 62% for those offered it at 14-16 (G-M34).
- **M17, checkpoint immunotherapy.** Ipilimumab was approved on 25 Mar 2011
  (H3, a page; the AP report, G-M25, was a snippet). The hazard ratio is
  the phase 3's, Hodi et al. (G-M24). Pembrolizumab followed in 2014
  (sheet).
- **M18, BPaL.** It was approved on 14 Aug 2019 (G-M59). Nix-TB gives 90%
  (G-M60). TB-PRACTECAL gives the comparison (G-M62). WHO's 89% against 52%
  is its review (G-M63).
- **M19, hospital at home.** The first programmes date from about 1995 (a
  snippet), so the row's date is CMS's national waiver of November 2020
  (G-M54). Levine's trial gives cost and readmission (G-M52), and Leff's
  gives the stay (G-M53). The share of admissions that go home was not
  found. R6f sets it as M12 above. The figures are a recipe at the game's
  Home Care Service.
- **M20, mRNA.** The MHRA approved it on 2 Dec 2020 and the FDA allowed its
  emergency use on 11 Dec 2020 (G-M16; full approval came on 23 Aug 2021,
  G-M17). The 95% is Polack et al. (G-M15). In the game, COVID is no cause of
  death and influenza is the 1918 one-off, so the row gives nothing of its
  own (★5).
- **M21, GLP-1.** Wegovy was approved on 4 Jun 2021 (G-M42). SELECT gives
  the heart figure (G-M41). The weight loss is not a cause of death in the
  game and is left out.
- **M22, RSV.** Arexvy was approved on 3 May 2023 (G-M48), with 82.6% from
  AReSVi-006 (G-M47). RSV's share of the pneumonia of the old was not found;
  MED1 opens it.

**Business**

- **B9, decimal share prices.** On 15 Apr 1996 the Toronto Stock Exchange cut
  its minimum tick to five cents (G-B18, a review of four studies). Whether
  Toronto was the first anywhere was not checked. New York followed on 29
  Jan 2001 and Nasdaq on 9 Apr 2001 (sheet). The game's `OrderBook` has no
  tick at all. R6f gives it one, falling at the row. The tick before the row
  is not in the sheet (an eighth of a dollar, from memory), and R6f opens it.
- **B10, the AI assistant.** The measured deployment ran from November 2020
  to February 2021 at a contact centre of about 5,000 agents (G-B1). The row
  takes it, not ChatGPT's release on 30 Nov 2022 (G-B3; ★6). The +14% is
  resolutions an hour (G-B1, G-B2). The game's Contact Centre takes it as
  output a post.

**Law**

- **L6, the smoking ban.** Manitoba's ban on smoking in enclosed public
  places and workplaces came into force on 1 Oct 2004 (H2, the province's
  page, archived). By the compendium's dates it was the first province (G-B66, a
  snippet; Saskatoon's bylaw of 1 Jul 2004 was a city's). Ireland's national
  ban of 29 Mar 2004 came before it (G-B65, a snippet). The
  -12% is Ireland's, from Cronin et al. (G-B61). It waits on heart disease as
  a cause (MED1).
- **L7, the TFSA.** In force from 1 Jan 2009, with $5,000 of room a year
  (G-B67). It gives something only once gains are taxed (HT1).
- **L8, Basel III.** OSFI's guideline came into force on 1 Jan 2013 (G-B33).
  The 4.5% plus 2.5% is from the Basel text (G-B29). Canada's six largest
  banks held 9.7% in 2015 and 13.7% in 2025 (G-B34). The game's
  `Bank.CAPITAL_RATIO` of .08 is "equity a bank must hold against its
  risk-weighted book". The Tier 1 and total-capital minimums before and after
  were not on an opened page (Basel I's 8% total, from memory). R6f decides
  which minimum the .08 stands for and opens the rule before.
- **L9, the stress test.** It applied to insured loans on 17 Oct 2016 (G-B25)
  and, through OSFI's B-20, to uninsured loans on 1 Jan 2018 (G-B24). The 20%

  Canada's (G-B26). It measures the 2018 step with the rate rises of the
  year; the 2016 step alone was not found. HO4's credit levers hold the
  test's add-on, so the row is HO4's.
