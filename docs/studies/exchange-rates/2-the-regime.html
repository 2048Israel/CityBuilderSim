Under the peg it moves nothing: the rate is held, and the sale is US$10.0M more of the vault spent holding it. Floating or in a band, the same sale would make the D$ about 1.5% dearer this month, half of that gone in 6 months.

Buying US$ makes the D$ cheaper. Until this batch the treasury's own purchases moved nothing: measured, buying US$205M of dollars left the D$ 7.7% DEARER after two years, because the bigger vault defended it more.

The dial is still yours. It is 3.25% against the US's 4.50%: money leaves for the US, and the vault pays for it: US$1.5M this month. Match the US's rate to hold the peg for free. A free dial, an open border and a fixed rate cannot all hold at once.

The regimes. Float: the market sets it. Lean is the default, today's behaviour: "a reserve defends a currency; it does not hold one down" (Jerus, 2026-09-21). Band is the Monetary Authority of Singapore's way: a centre, a slope and a width, the vault at the edges. Peg: to the US$; to the euro or the eight once the nations' moneys exist.
How a peg ends. The vault's floor, and a chance that rises with the gap. The break is the shadow rate plus a quarter of the gap again. The prototype's pegs at 2, 5 and 10 points of inflation over the US last a median 7.4, 4.5 and 2.9 years and break by 31%, 37% and 47% (median). History: sterling 1992 about 15%, the baht 1997 about half, Argentina 2002 about 70%.
