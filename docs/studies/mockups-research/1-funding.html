Mockup 1 — Policy › Research › Funding

A sixth Policy area, after Taxes, Wages, Money, Promises and Zoning (study §5.5). Each field has one card with two dials, a grant (D$ a month, buying posts at the field's schools) and a prize (D$ per discovery made here). The rows follow the thumb before → after, as every Policy dial card does (0.7.36), and the tray holds what is staged until Apply. Every figure is a model read: researchers, money, and the expected discoveries here before the world over ten years, which use the size's distribution, never its draw (§6.2).

A young city, June 1955 · one University (1953) · the prize being dragged on Engineering, a medical grant staged
A big city decades later, June 1979 · two Universities, two Institutes of technology, a Medical, a Business and a Law school · funding three fields
  • A post's cost is a researcher from the field's bands, its support staff and the lab's other costs: engineering D$ 18.2k a month, medicine 23.8k, business 12.2k, law 16.0k (study §1.8). A grant past the schools' room buys nothing; the dashed amber line on a dial is the room.
  • "In reach" counts the open problems and names the sectors they touch, not the discoveries (§6.2, Q18). A field with none says so, and its grant dial stays live but warns.
  • The prize shows what it would draw: Research Labs (Business Services, 100 posts each, §5.3) go for the cheapest open problem they can finish before the world. The young city's D$ 140M brings two labs; its chance of an engineering find here goes from none to about 75%. The big city's medical card says where its threshold is (D$ 350M).
  • Illustrative: the young city's 38,400 people, its cash and budget, and "graduates spare". Researchers, posts, money and the expected finds are the model's (claude/research-model/).
