The vault in eight moneys, and an offer across a border

Jerus: "if you buy land you are buying from the nation who has it, so basically there are mutliple currencies... and fx reserves is for each". The vault keeps the eight moneys central banks keep; every nation prices in its own; land is paid to the nation that sells it, in its money. Above, the reserves page; below, the land office's card for a city founded at Windsor, Ontario, across the river from Detroit.

Balances, rates and the city are invented for the board. The world's mix is the IMF's COFER shares for 2026Q2 over the eight. The land prices are today's formula times each seller's land factor against Canada's (spec-nations 6.3): the US's from the USDA's cropland average, Canada's an estimate.

Each part is bought from the nation it lies in, at that nation's land price, in its money. Canada is the reference, its factor 1, so its part costs what any offer costs today. The US part costs 1.7 times as much a hectare: US cropland averages US$14,880 a hectare (USDA, 2026) against about C$12,000 in Canada (est.).

The vault holds both moneys, so both parts come from it. One factor a nation does not see that southwest Ontario's farmland is several times Saskatchewan's (spec-nations 11).
The eight. US dollar, euro, yen, pound, yuan, the Canadian and Australian dollars and the Swiss franc: the moneys the IMF's survey of reserves names, about 94% of what central banks hold (2026Q2). The vault's cover and backing read their sum in US$, as today's do. The mix defaults to All US$, so a city that never opens this card holds what it holds today (spec-nations D20).
The land. Today's formula (US$75,000 a hectare at the world's price level, times the crowding premium) times the seller's land factor: its farmland's price over Canada's, from Eurostat, the USDA and FCC, a fit on output a head and density elsewhere. The border is the world file's raster at 240 cells a degree, so an offer's parts are counted cell by cell, and the river is split at the line (WN5).
