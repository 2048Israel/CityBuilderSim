## What could not be obtained

- **Gross margin for jewelry stores.** ARTS 2022 publishes jewelry stores' sales ($45.7 billion, NAICS 44831; the group 4483 jewelry, luggage and leather goods $50.4 billion) but no gross margin or margin-percent row for 4483 or 44831 in `gm.xlsx` or `gmper.xlsx`. Women's clothing stores are also "S" (not published, 2022). No second Census source was found; the listed-company figure above is the only evidence, and it is secondhand.
- **Income after taxes by quintile.** The current BLS CE tables carry no income-after-taxes row (the table list has only "before taxes" tables). The shares in `cex-quintiles.csv` are therefore of income before taxes (derived) and of expenditures (published by BLS); the user's after-tax denominator cannot be built from this table without an outside tax model.
- **Jewelry and watches by income quintile.** BLS publishes the detail (Table 2500) for all consumer units only; by quintile it publishes only the higher-level lines. Jewelry $88.43 and watches $22.60 a year per consumer unit are all-unit means.
- **Statistics Canada, table 11-10-0223-01 (optional).** Two tries: the CSV zip URL returned a security-check page, and the table page returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch. Not obtained, not guessed.
- **CBP 2024** was not yet posted at the URL tried.

## Cautions for whoever uses the figures

- **The lowest quintile spends more than it earns** (expenditures 209% of income before taxes, 2025). Its share of income is not a share of a budget; use the published share of expenditures (the broad discretionary group is 10.1% to 11.6% of expenditures in the lowest four quintiles and 12.8% in the highest). Income-share figures are meaningful from the second quintile up.
- **The consumer survey under-reports against the national accounts.** Derived, 2025: jewelry 13.9% of BEA PCE jewelry ($12.2 billion vs $87.8 billion), watches 16.9%, cosmetics 46.7%, apparel and services (clothing, footwear, jewelry and watches, clothing services) 40.2%. The CE's "Apparel and services" includes jewelry and watches (under "Other apparel products and services"); household surveys miss gifts, purchases for others, and high-end purchases. A budget set against CE means undershoots the national accounts by this much.
- **CE "apparel and services" is not goods only,** and "entertainment" includes admissions; `cex-quintiles.csv` gives three groupings (broad, goods-ish, narrow), all derived.
- **Two department-store counts, different populations.** CBP 2023 (NAICS 2017, 452210): 2,434 establishments, 195,673 employees. BLS CES 2025 (NAICS 2022, 4551 "Department stores"): 934 thousand employees on average. A five-fold gap; the likely cause (the CES category holding discount department stores that CBP files under supercenters) was not verified. ARTS department stores (4522, $43.7 billion sales in 2022) sits with CBP's smaller group.
- **NAICS vintages differ across sources:** BEA by product; ARTS and CBP use NAICS 2017 (jewelry stores 448310 or 44831; department stores 4522 or 452210); CES uses NAICS 2022 (jewelry, luggage and leather goods retailers 4583; department stores 4551). 4583 includes luggage; 44831 does not.
- **Annual averages for CES are derived** from monthly not-seasonally-adjusted values; BLS's own annual averages could differ in the last digit.
- **BEA 2025 values are the latest vintage** (dataset revised 2026-10-29), so 2023 and 2024 differ from earlier-published values.
