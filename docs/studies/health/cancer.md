# Gathered: cancer, 1900 to today

*Gathered 2026-11-17 for MED1 by a Sonnet agent; marks: page = seen on the opened page, snippet = search summary only, memory = not verified.*

Two extra marks are used where a figure is not printed anywhere but follows from printed ones: **page (calc)** = I computed it from page figures (the arithmetic is stated), and **derived** = a rougher estimate, with its assumption said. The Statistics Canada tables were read as the downloaded CSV of the table itself (the same numbers the table page shows), so those count as "page". Rates are per 100,000 people. "Cancer" = malignant neoplasms (ICD-10 C00-C97) unless said otherwise. M = males, F = females.

*How "both sexes (derived)" was made:* both = 0.40 x M + 0.60 x F. The 0.40 was fitted to Statistics Canada's own both-sexes values for 2000-2012 (table 1c), where the implied male weight is 0.40-0.41 (women outnumber men at the old ages where most cancer deaths fall). It is calibrated on 2000+ only, and the chart counts "cancers" a little more widely than the C00-C97 table (2000: M 317.1 against 306.5), so treat the column as good to about +-3%.

**Peak.** 1988, both sexes (CA-17, page: "peaked in 1988 in both sexes"; CA-16, page: M 344.5 and F 209.5 that year, 2011 standard, against 349.4 / 211.9 in CA-5). F's rate has no sharp peak: it stayed between 199.3 and 224.9 from 1950 to 2000, its chart maximum is 1953 (224.9), and CA-16 chose 1988 as "the practical peak" to match the men.

## 2. Before 1921 (the US)

**The 1900 figure is confirmed.** NCHS's table "Leading causes of death, 1900-1998" (CA-11), the page for 1900 in the registration states: cancer and other malignant tumours, **12,769 deaths, 64.0 per 100,000, rank 8** (page). Above it: pneumonia/influenza 202.2, tuberculosis 194.4, diarrhoea/enteritis 142.7, heart disease 137.4, stroke 106.9, nephritis 88.6, accidents 72.3; below: senility 50.2, diphtheria 40.3. All causes 1,719.1. The 1999 MMWR article (CA-14) has no cancer row in its text (its figures are images); the NCHS table is the confirmed source.

**Crude US rates, registration states (CA-11, page).** 1900 64.0 | 1901 66.4 | 1902 66.3 | 1903 70.0 | 1905 73.4 | 1908 71.5 | 1912 77.0 | 1914 78.7 | 1920 83.4 | 1921 85.5 | 1926 94.6 | 1930 97.4 | 1940 120.3 | 1946 130.0 | 1950 139.8. (1920 and 1930 were read from a garbled column of the same PDF; the values fit their neighbours.) The registration area grew, so the series mixes a rising rate with new states joining: 10 states plus Washington DC in 1900, all 48 states by 1933 (CA-13, page).

**Age-adjusted US series, NCHS (CA-12, page).** Age-adjusted death rates for cancer, both sexes: 1900 114.8 | 1910 143.1 | 1920 159.0 | 1921 163.5 | 1930 174.5 | 1940 187.8 | 1950 193.9 | 1960 193.9 | 1970 198.6 | 1980 207.9 | **1990 216.0 (the series' maximum)** | 2000 199.6 | 2010 172.8 | 2018 149.1. The dataset description names the 2000 US standard population for rates after 1998 and calls earlier rates "historical data"; the early values look to be on the same scale (1990 = 216.0), but the page does not name the early standard. For females alone (2000 standard, CA-13, page): 1900 138.6, 1910 169.5, 1920 180.0, 1930 188.1, 1940 189.6 (the peak), 1950 182.3. The CDC article adds that 1900-1940 cancer figures leave out leukaemia.

**Was 1900-1930 understated?** Partly, and the sources say so in general terms, not with a measured percentage for cancer:
- 1900-1930 US figures are from registration states, not the nation; "early rates may not be representative"; incomplete reporting "leads to underestimates of death rates"; ill-defined causes were 4.0% of deaths in 1900 (CA-13, page);
- a paper on the ten original registration states, 1900-1920 (JSTOR 4577747), came up in search with a machine-written summary saying about 30% of the rise was better reporting; not opened, unverified (snippet);
- for Canada, no source found says outright that 1921-1930 cancer rates were understated; the 1921-44 report's crude-versus-adjusted comparison (section 1a) shows ageing explains most of the crude rise (snippet, CA-8).

The game's bands, from CA-2 (page (calc), same nearest-class rule as 1d): babies 0-5 (classes 0-4) **2.2%** (41 of 1,906 deaths); children 6-12 (classes 5-14) **21.1%** (92 of 436); teens 13-17 (class 15-19) **7.1%** (56 of 794); adults 18-69 (classes 20-69) **32.2%** (26,851 of 83,428); seniors 70-84 **32.6%** (39,886 of 122,220); elders 85+ **15.8%** (18,663 of 117,995). The same shares in 2000 / 2010: babies 2.1% / 2.0%; children 15.7% / 20.9%; teens 7.8% / 6.8%; adults 40.7% / 41.5%; seniors 31.0% / 35.0%; elders 14.1% / 15.8%.

Why the adult share fell from 41.5% (2010) to 32.2% (2024): cancer deaths per adult fell (rate table above) and the all-cause denominator grew (adult deaths 66,947 to 83,428; the 25-34 and 35-44 classes went from 2,721 and 4,940 in 2010 to 5,539 and 8,004 in 2023, non-cancer causes whose nature I did not look up).

Lifetime: the Canadian Cancer Statistics 2025 report says 42% of people in Canada are expected to be diagnosed and 22% expected to die of cancer (page, CA-18).

**Children's cancer, and leukaemia.**
- Rate for under-15s: 23 deaths per million a year in 2006-2010, about 129 child deaths a year (page, CA-10); my calc for 2024 is 133 cancer deaths under age 15 and a rate near 2.1 per 100,000 (page (calc), CA-4, CA-2).
- Longer view: 430 cancer deaths, 7 per 100,000 children, in 1971, down to 3 per 100,000 in 1996 (a Public Health Agency of Canada page surfaced in search; snippet only). 1992-2010: childhood cancer deaths fell 2.0% a year and leukaemia deaths 3.5% a year, while leukaemia incidence rose 0.6% a year (page, CA-10).
- Leukaemia deaths under 15 in Canada: 38 (2000), 37 (2005), 26 (2010), 28 (2015), 22 (2020), 26 (2023), 20 (2024); it is about 15% of the 133 childhood cancer deaths in 2024 (page (calc), CA-4). Leukaemia was "almost one in four" childhood cancer deaths in 2006-2010 (page, CA-10).
- Survival (US, as no Canadian leukaemia-specific figure was found): children 0-14 with acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, five-year relative survival 74.8% (diagnosed 1981-90), 84.5% (1991-2000), 88.6% (2001-2010) (page, CA-25); the NCI says 57% in 1975 and 92.3% in 2014-2020 (page, CA-24). The "under 10% in the 1960s" figure comes from reviews surfaced by search (snippet only; a Canadian or SEER source for the 1960s was not found).

## 4. What moved it

- **Smoking, rise and fall (lung cancer).** Dates: men's smoking has fallen since the 1960s and women's since the 1980s; 61% of men smoked in 1965 against 10% in 2020, and 37% of women in 1979 against 12% in 2020 (as printed by CA-15, page; no figures for the earlier rise were found). The 20-year gap between the sexes shows as a 20-year gap between their lung-cancer peaks (CA-15). Effect: lung cancer's age-standardized rate peaked in M at 107.0 in 1989 and in F at 48.3 in 2008 (2011 standard, CA-15, page); since the peaks it is down 34% in M and 9% in F to 2022 (CA-15, page); Canadian Cancer Statistics 2025 projects 2025 as 60% below the male 1988 peak and about 30% below the female 2006 peak, with -4.1% a year for males in 2014-2022 and -3.1% for females in 2015-2022 (page, CA-18); 186,553 deaths avoided since the peaks (CA-15, page). The US models: 795,851 lung-cancer deaths averted in 1975-2000 by the change in smoking, 70,218 in 2000 alone, which is 32% of what perfect tobacco control after 1964 could have averted (page, CA-27). **Share of Canadian cancer deaths: lung 25.7% (2000), 26.9% (2010), 22.6% (2024; 19,379 of 85,589)** (page (calc), CA-2).
- **Stomach cancer's fall (diet, food storage, then H. pylori).** Date: the peak year in the data is its first year (M 51.9 in 1950, F 27.4 in 1951; the authors warn the true peak may be earlier) (CA-15, page). Effect: 65% or more down in both sexes, nearly 90% of expected deaths avoided by 2022, 417,561 deaths avoided, the most of any site (CA-15, page). The paper credits less salting and pickling of food, and later H. pylori treatment; the female rate fell from 11.3 (1970) to 2.9 (2007), 1991 standard (CA-17, page). **Share: stomach about 21% of M's and 12% of F's all-cancer rate in 1950-51 (page (calc), rough: two sources); 3.2% of cancer deaths in 2000, 2.6% in 2010, 2.3% in 2024 (1,928 deaths)** (page (calc), CA-2).
- **The Pap test and cervical cancer.** Dates: the test dated from 1928; British Columbia offered it from 1948 and started the first provincial screening programme in 1960; it became free in 1984 (CA-15, page). Effect: the rate fell from 13.2 in 1950 to 2.2 in 2006, -83% (a BMC Public Health paper, snippet), and nearly 90% of expected deaths had been avoided by 2022; 66,281 deaths avoided (CA-15, page). On the 1991 standard, 7.3 to 1.9 for 1970-2007 (CA-17, page). **Share: cervical cancer was 0.63% of cancer deaths in 2000, 0.52% in 2010, 0.50% in 2024 (424 of 85,589; about 1.05% of the 40,283 female cancer deaths)** (page (calc), CA-2). In 1950 it was about 6% of the female all-cancer rate (13.2 of 222.8; page (calc)).
- **Mammography screening (breast).** Dates: the first provincial programme in British Columbia in 1988; all provinces and territories but Nunavut had one by 2004 (CA-15, page). Effect: breast cancer's rate peaked at 42.2 in 1987; since then the standardized mortality ratio is 0.73 (27% fewer deaths than the peak rate would give), 64,347 avoided deaths (CA-15, page). US models credit screening with 28-65% of the fall in breast deaths in 1975-2000 (median 46%) and treatment with the rest (page, CA-26); the US breast death rate fell 44% in 1989-2022 (page, CA-28). **Share in Canada: breast 7.8% of cancer deaths (2000), 7.0% (2010), 6.4% (2024; 5,449)** (page (calc), CA-2).
- **Childhood leukaemia chemotherapy.** Dates: combination chemotherapy took shape in the 1960s-1970s (the NCI says improvements "beginning in the 1960s and 1970s"; snippet). Effect: five-year survival of ALL in children from under 10% (1960s, snippet) to 57% (1975) to about 90% (page, CA-24, CA-25); Canadian child leukaemia deaths fell 3.5% a year 1992-2010 (page, CA-10). **Share: all leukaemia 3.3% of Canadian cancer deaths in 2000 and 3.4% in 2024 (2,947 deaths); deaths under 15 from leukaemia are 20 a year (2024), 0.02% of cancer deaths** (page (calc), CA-4). So a step on all-cancer deaths from childhood leukaemia is tiny in deaths (large only in life-years).
- **Catalogue row 1: imatinib (Gleevec), CML.** Date: FDA accelerated approval 10 May 2001 for CML (snippet, CA-23). Effect: in the IRIS trial (553 patients on imatinib, median follow-up 60 months) the estimated overall survival at five years was 89%, and only 7% progressed (page, CA-20). In Canada CML deaths: 203 (2000), 163 (2001), 133 (2002), 118 (2005), 87 (2008), 106 (2010), 125 (2015), 123 (2024); aged under 50 they went 34 (2000), 7 (2010), 4 (2024) (page (calc), CA-4; ICD-10 code C92.1). **Share of Canadian cancer deaths: CML 0.32% in 2000, 0.15% in 2010, 0.14% in 2024 (123 of 85,589)** (page (calc), CA-4). So the fall coinciding with the drug was about 85 fewer CML deaths a year by 2005, 0.14% of the cancer deaths of 2000 (the timing fits; that it is all the drug's doing is my inference).
- **Catalogue row 2: HPV vaccine (Gardasil).** Dates: FDA licensed it 8 June 2006 for females 9-26 (page, CA-23); Canada's school programmes for girls began between the 2007/08 and 2010/11 school years, province by province, boys added by 2017 (snippet, CA-29). Effect: in England, women offered the (bivalent, Cervarix) vaccine at 12-13 had 87% less cervical cancer (95% CI 72-94) at ages 20-29 than the unvaccinated cohorts, 97% less CIN3 (page, CA-21). Caveats the game should know: the 87% is incidence, not deaths; it is the bivalent vaccine, not Gardasil; it is England; and it covers women only up to 29. **Share: cervical cancer 0.50% of Canadian cancer deaths (424 of 85,589 in 2024); only 18 of 424 were under 35**, so girls vaccinated from 2007 (age 12 then, 29-30 in 2024) cannot yet show in the deaths (page (calc), CA-4; the "cannot yet show" is my reading).
- **Catalogue row 3: checkpoint immunotherapy.** Date: ipilimumab, FDA 25 March 2011 for unresectable or metastatic melanoma (snippet, FDA notification, CA-23). Effect: in the pivotal trial (676 patients, previously treated) the hazard ratio for death was 0.68 for ipilimumab plus a vaccine against gp100 alone, 0.66 for ipilimumab alone; median survival 10.0 against 6.4 months (page, CA-22). **Share of Canadian cancer deaths in 2024: melanoma 1.53% (1,313; 1.13% in 2000, 1.36% in 2010)**; with the other sites where checkpoint drugs are used widely, lung 22.6%, kidney 2.2% and bladder 2.8%, about **29.1%** (24,899 of 85,589) (page (calc), CA-2). Observed so far: no clear drop in melanoma deaths in Canada, which rose from 981 (2010) to 1,313 (2024); the standardized melanoma rate peaked in 2011 in M (4.2) and 2009 in F (2.0), and the standardized mortality ratio since the peak was 1.00 (M) and 0.88 (F) to 2022 (page, CA-15). Lung: male rate -4.1% a year in 2014-2022, with the report naming targeted therapy and immunotherapy among the changes in lung cancer care (page, CA-18); it does not measure their share.
- **How much of the fall since 1990 came from smoking, screening and treatment?** No split for Canada was found. What exists: the American Cancer Society reports (CA-28, page) that US cancer mortality fell through 2022, averting nearly 4.5 million deaths since 1991 (over 4 million in the 2024 edition), "because of smoking reductions, earlier detection for some cancers, and improved treatment", with no shares; the breast-only model split above (CA-26); the lung-only smoking model (CA-27). Canada's avoided-deaths counts (section 1c, CA-15, CA-16) are measured against peak rates, not split by driver: 256,347 deaths avoided for cervical, breast and colorectal cancer combined since their screening programmes began (1960, 1988, 2007), and over a million for tobacco-associated cancers; the authors warn the site totals overlap (CA-15, page).

## 5. Notes

**Gaps (said missing, not filled).**
- No both-sexes age-standardized Canadian rate for 1921-1930, and none printed for 1950-1999; the sex-specific series (CA-5) and my 0.40/0.60 mix are what exists. The Historical Statistics of Canada section B (11-516-X) could not be opened or found by search, so nothing here comes from it.
- The 1921-44 report's PDF would not download (an "archived" notice page); its figures are search-summary level.
- No Canadian age-specific cancer rates or shares by age before 2000; no cancer share of all deaths for Canada before 2000 (1921 total deaths not found). US age-specific tables HIST290 could not be read (the archived copies returned HTML).
- No Canadian split of the fall since 1990 into smoking, screening and treatment; no Canadian childhood leukaemia survival figure; no Health Canada approval dates (the dates above are the FDA's).

**Contradictions and cautions.**
- Same-year values differ between sources: 1988 M 349.4 (CA-5) against 344.5 (CA-16); 2000 M 317.1 (CA-5 chart, "cancers") against 306.5 (CA-1, C00-C97). Probably a wider cause group in the chart, not an error (not verified).
- CA-5's own text says the male rate fell 27.5% from 1988 to 2012, while its chart values (349.4 to 258.8) give 25.9%.
- Standard populations differ (1991, 2011, 2021 and the US 2000); do not splice. The game should pick one (2011 is the one on Statistics Canada's tables; the factor to the 2021 standard is about 1.18).
- 1921: 75 (registration area, CA-7) against 70.1 (all Canada, CA-8, snippet); probably area coverage (Quebec joined the registration area in 1926).
- 1900 US coverage: CA-13 says the original registration area held 40.5% of the US population; backing the population out of the NCHS 1900 row gives about 20 million people, which I remember as about a quarter of the nation (memory). Unresolved; the rate is unaffected.
- The page does not name the standard of NCHS's pre-1999 rates; a SEER table (search summary) gives 195.4 for 1950 on the 2000 standard against 193.9 here.
- A falling cancer share of deaths is partly other causes rising (section 3), not only falling cancer risk.
- CA-15 and CA-16 built their pre-2000 values from GLOBOCAN (non-commercial); only their findings are used. Statistics Canada's own chart (CA-5) is the open-licence route for 1950-1999.
- The Canadian Cancer Statistics data portal could not be reached from this machine, so its 182.6 figure for 2020 (snippet) is unchecked.
