# Gathered: diabetes and kidney failure, 1900 to today

*Gathered 2026-11-17 for MED1 by a Sonnet agent; marks: page = seen on the opened page, snippet = search summary only, memory = not verified.*

`page` also covers a file I downloaded and read myself (a Statistics Canada CSV, an NCHS or NIDDK PDF turned to text); the key says which. `arith` is my own arithmetic on `page` figures. All wording is mine. Gaps are in section 7; each source's licence is in the Sources table.

A "crude" rate is deaths per 100,000 people of all ages; an "age-standardized" rate fixes the age mix, so it does not fall merely because the population ages. Cause-of-death coding changed several times (US revisions 1900, 1910, 1921, 1930, 1939, 1949; Canada ICD-10 from 2000), so a jump at a revision year is partly coding.

Who died before insulin. Weighting the 1921 rates by 1920 census age counts (memory: about 9.3 M aged 1-4, 22 M 5-14, 19 M 15-24, 17 M 25-34, 14 M 35-44, 10 M 45-54, 6.5 M 55-64, 3.4 M 65-74, 1.3 M 75-84, 0.2 M 85+) gives, as an order of magnitude (arith + memory): about 6 % of diabetes deaths under 15, 11-12 % under 25, 17 % under 35, 24 % under 45, and 60 % at 55 or over. ### 2c. The effect in the US registration-state rates (page, DK-9; arith)

Between 1922 and 1925 the rates fell: ages 5-14 from 3.9 to 2.1 (-46 %); 15-24 from 5.0 to 2.9 (-42 %); 25-34 from 6.3 to 3.4 (-46 %); 1-4 from 2.1 to 1.3 (-38 %); the drop already shows in 1923 (5-14: 2.5). By 1939 the 5-14 rate was 1.5 and the 15-24 rate 2.3. The all-ages rate did not fall (18.3 to 16.8) because the old did not improve (65-74: 130 to 131; 75-84: 151 to 162) and they made up most deaths.

### 4d. Why it fell (what the sources say)

- Page: the old "nephritis (all forms)" bin ran 84-103 per 100,000 in the US registration area in 1900-1926 (DK-11) and 32-55 in Canada in 1921-26 (DK-6); Canada is 6.6-7.2 in the early 1960s (DK-7). Today's ICD-10 group (4b) is a different bin that also holds acute kidney failure.
- Snippet: a 1971 US study (New Haven and national data) found that the 1950-60 fall of combined chronic nephritis and kidney-infection rates came mainly from doctors changing how they recorded the cause; a 1967 Lancet analysis found chronic nephritis increasingly written as a secondary cause beside heart disease, and diabetics' kidney deaths increasingly written as diabetes. Neither paper was opened. A history article says the old Bright's disease label covered several syndromes later regrouped under glomerulonephritis.
- Memory only: much old "Bright's disease" would be called hypertensive or diabetic kidney disease today, and antibiotics for strep infections cut post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis. Not verified here.

An older CORR table gives a mean age of 64.9 on haemodialysis and 62.4 on peritoneal dialysis (snippet, year not shown). Diabetes was the leading cause of kidney failure, 39 % in 2019 (snippet, DK-37). The CIHI pages I opened give no patient age.

Waiting and grafts (page, DK-18, DK-19): 2,813 on the kidney wait list at 31 December 2022; median wait for a deceased-donor kidney 3.4 years in 2023; 303 days for a living-donor kidney. Graft survival, deceased donor: 95 % at 1 year, 88 % at 3, 79 % at 5; living donor 98 %, 94 %, 90 %.

### 5c. Survival

- Untreated kidney failure (no dialysis, no transplant): days to a few weeks in the usual lay statements (snippet, low-quality sites; no NIDDK or Kidney Foundation page I opened gives a figure). Older patients who choose conservative care and keep some function: median survival 1 to 45 months across studies (snippet, a narrative review); 21.2 months against 67.1 on dialysis in one cohort of 844 (snippet); 1.4 against 2.1 years at age 80 and over (snippet). Shields was dying of uraemia when the shunt went in (snippet).
- Survival on dialysis at 5 years (all snippets): about 40 % of incident patients in Ontario 2005-2014; about 34 % in one hospital programme; about 42 % of US haemodialysis patients in USRDS 2017 as cited; 56.8 % unadjusted for a 2012-2015 haemodialysis start cohort in a single-centre paper. No Canadian CORR figure found; the USRDS pages are scripted and returned nothing. USRDS dialysis mortality was 168.4 per 1,000 person-years in 2023 (snippet).
- Transplant survival: see 5b.
