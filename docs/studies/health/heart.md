## 1. Canada's rate, 1921 to today

No single Canadian heart-disease-only age-standardized series from 1921 to now was found. The long run has to be stitched from the pieces in 1A to 1F, each on its own basis. Three standard populations are in use (1991, 2011 and 2021 Canadian population) and their levels differ a lot (1A). The heart-only gap 1931-1999 is in Notes.

Heart-disease deaths in 85+ have grown (18,693 in 2000, 25,151 in 2024) even as the rate fell 41%, because that group grew about 2.3 times (derived population 404,000 to 921,000).

Heart disease and stroke: one cause or two? Statistics Canada counts them as two causes everywhere (diseases of heart; cerebrovascular diseases, I60-I69), ranks them separately (heart 2nd, stroke 4th in 2023 and 2024; stroke 3rd in 2008), and its own 2026 article on them keeps them as two conditions that share one risk factor, high blood pressure (page, HD-17). It also publishes a combined group "major cardiovascular diseases" (I00-I78) in the same tables. The two have fallen at different speeds (stroke faster, see 1A) and have different age shapes (stroke is 4.3% of deaths at 70-84, heart 17.3%). Prevalence: heart disease 6.3% of adults 18+ in 2023 (men 7.9%, women 4.7%); stroke 1.2% overall, 3.4% at 65+ (page, HD-17).

## 4. What moved it

Each line: date, measured effect, mark, key. Scale: Canada's heart rate fell about 47% from 2000 to 2023 (age-standardized), and all circulatory disease about 76-82% from 1950 to 2012.

- **Coronary care unit, 1960s.** First US unit May 1962, Bethany Hospital, Kansas City (Hughes Day); concept published 1961 by Wilburne and Julian. Early hospital death after a heart attack fell from about 30% to about 15% by the late 1960s, per an ACC editorial that names no study for it. Page (HD-38). Killip and Kimball 1967 (250 patients, two years in a unit): snippet.
- **Treating high blood pressure, 1967 and 1970.** VA Cooperative Study, diastolic 115-129: 143 men (70 placebo, 73 on three drugs); 27 serious complications on placebo against 2 treated (one summary says 1); 4 deaths on placebo, none treated; stopped early as clearly beneficial. 1970 report, diastolic 90-114, 380 men: 19 deaths on control against 8 treated. Snippet; the JAMA records (1967;202:1028, 1970;213:1143) are real but carry no abstract (HD-33).
- **Bypass surgery, 1967-68.** Favaloro, Cleveland Clinic, first documented saphenous-vein bypass 9 May 1967; aorta-to-coronary graft series from Oct 1967. No population effect found. Snippet (HD-41).
- **Beta blockers.** Norwegian timolol trial, NEJM 1981: 1,884 survivors of a heart attack, 12-33 months; deaths 98 vs 152 (about 36% fewer, derived); sudden death 7.7% vs 13.9%; reinfarction 14.4% vs 20.1%. Page (HD-30). BHAT 1982 (about 26% fewer deaths): memory.
- **Clot-dissolving drugs and aspirin.** ISIS-2, Lancet 1988: 17,187 suspected heart attacks; 5-week vascular death streptokinase 9.2% vs 12.0%, aspirin 9.4% vs 11.8%, both 8.0% vs 13.2% (odds cut 25%, 23%, 42%). Page (HD-29). GISSI-1 1986 (about 18% fewer hospital deaths): memory.
- **Balloon angioplasty, 16 Sep 1977.** Gruentzig, Zurich, first coronary balloon dilation of an awake patient (Adolph Bachmann); one editorial says 15 Sep. Snippet (HD-41). Primary angioplasty against clot-busting drugs, 23 trials, 7,739 patients, Lancet 2003: short-term death 7% vs 9%; death, reinfarction or stroke 8% vs 14%. Page (HD-31).
- **Statins.** Lovastatin FDA-approved 31 Aug 1987 (some sources say September): snippet (HD-42). 4S, Lancet 1994: 4,444 patients, median 5.4 years; deaths 12% placebo (256) vs 8% simvastatin (182), RR 0.70; coronary deaths 189 vs 111, RR 0.58. Page (HD-26). CTT 2010: each 1.0 mmol/L lower LDL cut major vascular events about a fifth (rate ratio 0.78) and coronary deaths a fifth (0.80); 129,526 people in 21 trials. Page (HD-32).
- **Drug-coated stent, 24 Apr 2003.** FDA approved the Cypher sirolimus stent (record P020026), the first in the US: snippet (HD-42). SIRIUS, NEJM 2003: 1,058 patients; repeat procedure on the treated lesion 4.1% vs 16.6%; target-vessel failure at 270 days 8.6% vs 21.0%. Page (HD-27).
- **GLP-1 drugs, 4 Jun 2021.** FDA approved Wegovy for weight management that day: snippet (HD-42). SELECT, NEJM 2023: 17,604 adults with overweight or obesity and no diabetes (established heart disease was required: memory), mean 39.8 months; cardiovascular death, heart attack or stroke 6.5% (569) vs 8.0% (701); hazard ratio 0.80 (0.72-0.90). Page (HD-28). Canada's heart rate is flat 2019-2023 (121.9 to 118.4, 2011 standard), so no population effect shows yet (derived).
- **Smoking's fall (Canada, adults 15+).** 49.5% (1965; men 61%, women 38%); 39.5% (1981); 34% (1985); 31% (1990); 24% (2000); 17% (2010); 13% (2015); 10% (2020). Page (HD-39). The 1993 report links the post-1965 fall in IHD to less smoking and dietary fat, better blood pressure control and better care (page, HD-11).
- **Smoke-free laws, effect.** Ireland, workplace ban 29 Mar 2004: acute coronary admissions -12% in the next year (177.9 vs 205.9 per 100,000), a further -13% from Mar 2006 to Mar 2007 (page, HD-34). Helena, Montana, six months: heart attack admissions 24 vs a usual 40 (-40%, derived) (page, HD-35). Pooled, 12 months after a law: heart attack admissions rate ratio 0.83 (0.80-0.87) (page, HD-36). Toronto 1996-2006: cardiovascular admissions -39% crude in the restaurant-ban phase, no consistent fall after other phases, no separate heart attack figure (page, HD-37). Prince Edward Island (law 1 Jun 2003) and Saskatoon (about 32 fewer heart attack admissions in a year) also reported falls (snippet, HD-35).
- **Smoke-free laws, Canadian dates** (workplaces or enclosed public places; a secondary reference, unverified against the statutes, HD-40): Manitoba 1 Oct 2004; New Brunswick Oct 2004; Prince Edward Island 2003; Northwest Territories 1 May 2004; Nunavut 1 May 2004; Saskatchewan 1 Jan 2005 (public places; the page also gives 31 May 2009 for workplaces); Newfoundland and Labrador 2005; Ontario 31 May 2006 (workplace law with exceptions since 1990); Quebec 31 May 2006; Nova Scotia 1 Dec 2006 (an earlier 2003 law is in my memory, unverified); Alberta 1 Jan 2008; Yukon 15 May 2008; British Columbia no main date on the page. Page.
- **Decomposition, US 1980-2000 (Ford et al., NEJM 2007).** Age-adjusted CHD death rate men 542.9 to 266.8, women 263.3 to 134.4; 341,745 fewer deaths in 2000. About 47% from treatments (secondary prevention 11%, initial treatment of heart attack or unstable angina 10%, heart failure 9%, chronic angina revascularization 5%, other 12%) and about 44% from risk factors (cholesterol 24%, systolic blood pressure 20%, smoking 12%, inactivity 5%), with diabetes (10%) and body-mass index (8%) pushing the other way. Page (HD-23). A replication abstract found about two thirds from medical therapy once drug use was corrected (snippet).
- **Decomposition, Ontario 1994-2005 (Wijeysundera et al., JAMA 2010).** Ages 25-84, CHD death rate 191 to 125 (-35%), 7,585 fewer deaths in 2005; 43% from treatments (range 11-124%: stable coronary disease 17%, heart attack 8%, community heart failure 10%), 48% from risk factors (cholesterol 23%, blood pressure 20%; diabetes -6% and body-mass index -2%). The rest, about 9%, is unexplained (derived). Page (HD-24).
- **Older split (quoted in the 1995 Canadian report).** In North America about 54% of the fall in IHD death from lifestyle change, 39.5% from medical care, 6.5% unexplained; the source is probably Goldman and Cook (inferred). Page as quoted (HD-11).
- **Canadian hospital outcomes.** In-hospital death after a heart attack 15.2% (1994/95) to 11.7% (2001/02) (page, HD-13). 1994 to 2004: AMI death rate -38.1%, heart failure -23.5%, stroke -28.2%, AMI admissions -9.2%, in-hospital AMI fatality -33.1% (page, HD-25).

## 5. Notes

- **Biggest gap.** Heart-disease-only age-standardized rates for Canada 1931-1999 were not found as numbers. Two charts exist (Health Reports 2000, 1950-1997 by sex, 1991 standard, HD-12; the 1993 report's 1951-90 figure, HD-11) with no printed values. Statistics Canada's older cause tables (Canada Year Book pages 1938, 1943-44, 1959-60) are scans whose columns did not extract in order; the annual Vital Statistics volumes (84-206) were not opened and may hold the series. Heart share of "major cardiovascular" in 2000 (72%) could scale 1B backwards, but that share was not found for earlier years.
- **1950 and the peak.** For circulatory disease the age-standardized peak is women 1951 and men 1957 (HD-5); IHD peaked in the mid-1960s (HD-11); crude cardiovascular-renal peaked in the 1951-55 average (HD-6). A heart-only peak year for Canada is therefore between the 1950s and mid-1960s, not pinned down. The US age-adjusted peak is 1950 (588.8, HD-19) and the CHD peak 1963 (HD-20).
- **Standards differ a lot.** Heart disease 2000: 152.0 (1991), 222.7 (2011), 271.1 (2021). Link ratios 2011 to 1991 standard: 1.47 (2000), 1.48 (2010), 1.49 (2011). A game curve that joins a 1991-standard history to today's data needs one standard throughout. The 1950-1997 charts (1991) and 1950-2012 numbers (2011) are different bases.
- **Revisions and classification breaks.** ICD-9 covers 1979-1999 and ICD-10 from 2000 (page, HD-14). The Historical Statistics notes (search summary only) say ICD-8 applies from 1969 and warn against comparing 1967-68 with 1969 on; earlier ICD-7 (1958-67) is memory. Counts get revised: 2023 heart deaths 57,890 then 58,098; 2000 55,070 then 54,959. Crude and standardized are easy to mix: 2008 heart disease is 152.2 crude but 159.8 standardized (2011 standard) (page, HD-1).
- **The 1920s rise is partly paperwork.** The 1934 report says physicians began naming more than one cause, and the Bureau's choice rules send such deaths to heart, arteries and kidney, not stroke. The standardized rise 1921-31 was 9% against 25.5% crude (HD-8, HD-9).
- **US 1900-1932 is not national.** Only the registration states count (mostly the northeast: memory); the 1900 crude rate 137.4 is a registration-area rate. The registration area grows each year, so 1900 to 1926 level changes mix real change with coverage.
- **Disagreements to settle before use.** Nova Scotia's smoke-free date (1 Dec 2006 on the page against a 2003 law in memory) and Saskatchewan's (2005 vs 2009) need the statutes; Gruentzig's first angioplasty is dated 15 or 16 Sep 1977; VA trial complications in the treated arm 2 or 1; FDA's Wegovy and Cypher dates came from news and device-record snippets; lovastatin 31 Aug or September 1987.
- **Age bands.** The tables have no 0-5 or 6-12 split; the closest are under 1, 1-4, 5-9, 10-14 (heart deaths 2024: 6, 9, 5, 10) and 15-19 (16). Adults 18-69 are read as 20-69; seniors 70-84 and elders 85+ map directly. Before 2000, age-specific Canadian heart figures were found only for ages 35 and over, IHD, in 1990 (1F).
- **Both sexes only.** All figures above are both sexes unless noted; men's heart-disease rate is about 1.7 times women's (2023, 2011 standard: 153.4 vs 89.2).
- **Not used.** WHO and IHME/GBD data (not allowed), Our World in Data (not fetched; its inputs keep their own licences), Heart and Stroke Foundation and CIHI figures (terms refuse commercial reuse without permission). The Statistics Canada tables were read from CSV downloaded to a temporary folder outside the repository; nothing from them is stored in the tree.
