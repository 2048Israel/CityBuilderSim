## 1. Polio in Canada, by year

Two series run side by side, and they disagree after 1948. They must not be mixed in one column of the game's data.

- **PHAC series** (PO-1, `page`): the Public Health Agency of Canada's notifiable-disease file, 1924-2016, one count per year, rate per 100,000 given. Open Government Licence - Canada, stated on the dataset page. The file does not say whether a count is all cases or paralytic cases only.
- **Rutty's series** (PO-2, `page`, read from a chart in his 2005 paper): all notified cases 1927-1956, paralytic cases only 1957-1962. It agrees with the PHAC file in the years I could compare before 1948 (1927: 609; 1931: 1,342; 1937: 3,905; 1941: 1,881; 1946: 2,527) and in 1959 (1,886 against 1,887). From 1952 it is about twice the PHAC count (1952: 4,755 against 2,334; 1953: 8,878 against 5,384). My reading, **not stated by any source** (`memory`/inference): from 1949, when paralytic and non-paralytic cases began to be reported apart, the PHAC file carries the paralytic cases only. The Canada Year Book of 1956 (PO-3, `page`) supports the order of size: 1953 had 8,736 cases in all, of which 4,963 were paralytic, and 481 deaths. That is neither 5,384 nor 8,878, so the three 1953 figures are three counts of different things or different revisions. Pick one and name it.

## 2. Who, when, and the breathing paralysis

**Ages, and the shift.** All `page` unless marked.

- The 1928 Manitoba epidemic hit mostly children under five; in Winnipeg over half of the stricken were ten or younger and 3.1% were over 25 (PO-8). Each later epidemic reached older patients, and 1953 struck young adults hard (PO-8).
- Deaths moved up in age: in 1926, 70% of deaths were in children under fifteen; in 1953 the share was 30%. In 1953, 31.6% of reported cases, 31.4% of paralytic cases and 60.9% of deaths were in people over twenty (PO-3).
- In 1957 the paralytic attack rate was highest in children under five, but adults of 20-39 were as high as, or higher than, school-age children; that led to adult campaigns in 1958-60 (PO-2).
- The Canadian bulbar (breathing and swallowing) cases of 1937 and 1953 were many, and in 1953 they were especially among young adults (PO-2).
- A Canadian health agency page says infection is more common under age five (PO-1, guide file, `page`). A 1959 news story names a 13-year-old who died and a 28-year-old mother who died (PO-10, `page`).
- No Canada-wide table of cases by age band was found for 1927-1965. The Year Book gives only the shares above. A count by game age band cannot be built from what I found (gap).

**Season.** Epidemic summers and falls. Manitoba 1928: cases from July, over 100 by end of August, the provincial report covering July to October; 1953: clear by July, about 100 a week at the peak, about 2,200 cases July to November (PO-8, `page`). Rutty places the 1953 national epidemic in "that summer and fall" (PO-2, `page`). The current US manual says infection peaks in summer in temperate climates and records that early-twentieth-century epidemics came each summer and fall (PO-11, `page`). No month-by-month Canadian count was found (gap).

**Share of infections that paralyse.** About 70% of infections in children show nothing; about 24% are a minor illness; 1-5% are meningitis without paralysis; under 1% bring paralysis (PO-11, `page`). Paralysis starts usually 7-21 days after infection (PO-11, `page`).

**Share of paralytic cases with breathing paralysis.**
- Copenhagen 1952: about 345 of the roughly 1,250 patients with any paralysis (about 28%, `computed`) had bulbar polio with breathing trouble (PO-12, `page`).
- US 1969-79: bulbar polio was about 2% of cases (PO-11, `page`). That is a later era and all cases, not paralytic only.
- Canada: no national share found (gap). Peaks of respirator need: 92 Winnipeg patients on respirators at one moment in 1953 (PO-2, `page`); 99 in iron lungs on one night at a Winnipeg hospital during a power cut (PO-8, `page`). In 1937 Canada had one iron lung at the start of the Ontario epidemic and 27 were built in six weeks in a Toronto hospital basement (PO-2, `page`).

**Death rate with breathing paralysis: iron lung against positive-pressure ventilation.**
- US manual: with bulbar involvement the death rate of paralytic polio rises to 25-75% (PO-11, `page`).
- Copenhagen 1952, the first three weeks, before the change to hand ventilation through a tracheostomy: 27 of the first 31 patients with bulbar polio died, which is 87% (`computed` from PO-12, `page`; the paper by Lassen, PO-13, has no abstract in Europe PMC so I could not read his own numbers).
- After the change: the review says the death rate is "said to have dropped" from about 90% to about 25% (PO-12, `page`). Popular accounts give other end points: 11%, 17%, 20%, 40% and 50% (`snippet`, five different web articles that disagree). The brief's "about 40%" is therefore **not confirmed by anything I could open**; it is `memory`. The 87% start is confirmed as 27 of 31. The end point is the weak figure. Seventy patients needed hand ventilation round the clock at the peak, staffed by about 200 medical students in relays (PO-12, `page`).
- Canadian iron-lung death rates: not found (gap).

**Survivors left disabled for life.**
- The Canadian Public Health Association's history estimates 11,000 people were left paralysed between 1949 and 1954 (PO-9, `page`; a 2017 column, an estimate, not a count).
- Today's survivors with late effects: about 31,000 (March of Dimes Canada, PO-15, `snippet`) or about 16,000 (a CBC news item, PO-20, `snippet`, third-party copy); a peer-reviewed paper says the number is unknown (`snippet`). So the figure is soft. Up to 70% of survivors may have post-polio syndrome (March of Dimes, `snippet`); the US manual says 25-40% of those paralysed as children get new or worse muscle problems after 15-40 years (PO-11, `page`).
- A Canadian fact for the game: after the 1937 Ontario epidemic the province paid for free standard treatment and three weeks' hospital stay for every paralytic case (PO-2, `page`).

### PCV7 (pneumococcal conjugate vaccine for infants)
- FDA: Feb 2000 (`memory`, brief).
- Canada: the first conjugate vaccine for children was approved around June 2001 (`snippet`), though another source puts the infant authorization in 2002 (`snippet`); **unsettled**. The advisory committee's statement ACS-2 is dated 15 January 2002 and recommends infants at 2, 4, 6 and 12-15 months, plus a catch-up for ages 24-59 months (`snippet`, archived PHAC pages).
- Provincial program starts: Alberta 2002 (`snippet`); northern Quebec Apr 2002, Nunavut Sep 2002, northern Labrador Jul 2003, Yukon Jun 2005 (`snippet`, a CDC table); **BC Sep 2003** (`page`, PO-25); Quebec Jan 2005 (`snippet`, one source; another gave 2004); Ontario 2005 (`snippet`). Newfoundland, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, PEI, Manitoba and Saskatchewan: **not found**.
- Measured effect, children under 5, **BC**: 54.4 down to 13.6 invasive cases per 100,000, 2002 to 2006 (PO-25, `page`). In children aged one year the fall was from 135 to 15 per 100,000 (`snippet`).
- **National sentinel network** (the IMPACT hospitals), 2000-2007: reported invasive cases fell 48% in all children and 56% in children 0-4; cases of vaccine serotypes fell 87.5% overall and 92% in 0-4 (PO-26, `page`, Europe PMC abstract). A 2011 conference item gives under-5 rates of 35 per 100,000 before and 9.6 in 2006 (`snippet`). **Quebec** under-5: 69 per 100,000 in 2003 to 12 in 2016, an 83% fall across three vaccine eras (`snippet`).
- Vaccine effectiveness in Canadian children 2002-2018: PCV7, 96% after one dose and 95% after two or more; PCV13, 55% after two or more (PO-27, `page`).
- All-ages invasive pneumococcal counts in PO-1 (`page`): 2000: 1,357 (7.14 per 100,000); 2005: 2,857 (8.86); 2010: 3,344 (9.83); 2015: 3,212 (8.96); 2016: 3,290 (9.07). Reporting widened after 2000 and the old carry most cases; do not read the rise as program failure.
- Child pneumonia deaths: tiny already in 2000 (under 1: 13; 1-4: 5, Section 4a). No Canadian before-and-after figure for pneumonia deaths in children found.

### Rotavirus vaccine
- FDA: Feb 2006 (`memory`, brief). Health Canada licensing and NACI statement dates: **not found**.
- Canada: provincial programs came one by one from December 2010 (PEI) to December 2015 (Newfoundland and Labrador); Nova Scotia funded its program in 2019 (`page`, PO-28). Alberta began in June 2015 (`snippet`); Ontario's post-program study period starts August 2011 (`snippet`). Other provinces' month: **not found**.
- Measured effect (PO-28, `page` via fetch summary; the table's population base, under five or all ages, was not clear to me): hospitalization rates per 100,000, before to after, PEI 241.7 to 70.8 (2009-10 against 2012-13, down 70.7%); Ontario 96.9 to 45.3 (down 53.2%); BC 31.7 to 7.9 (down 75.1%); Saskatchewan 288.7 to 71.2 (down 75.3%); Alberta 59.0 to 19.5 (down 67.0%); Nova Scotia 94.3 to 34.1. In the four earliest provinces reductions passed 85% in babies and one-year-olds. National lab-confirmed community cases fell 54% from 2010 to 2017 (828 to 381). Nationwide hospital discharge rate down by 48.2 per 100,000. The paper's results text and its abstract disagree on which provinces dropped; treat the table as primary.
- Deaths: no figure found. Canadian child diarrhoea deaths were already 1-7 a year (Section 4b).

### Six-month all-oral treatment for drug-resistant TB (bedaquiline, pretomanid, linezolid)
- FDA: Aug 2019 (`memory`, brief). Health Canada: **not found**. A 2023 Canadian case report says the drugs were not all licensed in Canada and describes the first Canadian patient to finish the regimen, treatment ending Nov 2021 (`snippet`).
- Cases a year: see Section 4c, about 17 multidrug-resistant a year in 2009-2020 (`computed`).
- Canadian measured effect: none found (too new, too few cases). A trial of the four-drug version (Nyang'wa 2022) found less death, failure and dropout than usual care (`snippet`; international, not Canadian).

### RSV vaccine for older adults
- FDA: May 2023 (`memory`, brief). **Health Canada: Arexvy authorised 4 Aug 2023 for 60 and over; Abrysvo 21 Dec 2023** (PO-29, `page`). Arexvy extended to ages 50-59 at higher risk in Nov 2024 (`snippet`).
- **NACI, 12 July 2024** (PO-29, `page`): programs recommended for adults 75 and over, and for residents of nursing and chronic-care homes aged 60 and over; for 60-74 an individual choice with a doctor. Provinces choose their own funding.
- Burden in Canada (PO-29, `page`): hospitalization rates per 100,000 from a prospective study, 13.9 (50-59), 43.7 (60-69), 88.6 (70-79), 282.5 (80 and over); from hospital discharge records averaged over 12 seasons, 4, 10, 22 and 63 per 100,000. In-hospital case fatality about 6% in the discharge data, about 5-10% for older or high-risk patients. RSV is not a notifiable disease in Canada; no national count of RSV deaths was given.
- Canadian measured effect: none found.

## 6. Notes

1. **Game age bands against the tables.** The agency's bands are under 1, 1-4, 5-9, 10-14, 15-19, then 5-year steps. Babies 0-5 are under 1 plus 1-4 plus part of 5-9; children 6-12 and teens 13-17 cut across 5-9, 10-14 and 15-19, so a clean fit needs an assumption (spread a band's rate over its years). Adults 18-69 are 20-24 to 65-69 plus part of 15-19; seniors 70-84 and elders 85+ fit 13-10-0394-01's 75-84 and 85+ bands once 70-74 is added.
2. **Coding breaks.** ICD-9 to 1999, ICD-10 from 2000 (notes of 13-10-0014-01 and the 2001 Mortality summary, `page`); the 1950s tables use earlier revisions. The 2003-04 step in intestinal infections is not children (4b).
3. **Two age-standard levels.** The 1991 standard (to 2013) and the 2021 standard (to 2024) give different numbers for the same year (influenza and pneumonia 2000: 13.2 against 26.2). Use one and name it; for a game with its own age mix, the age-specific rates in 4a are cleaner.
4. **Polio deaths before 1950** are not found nationally (Alberta 1927 and Ontario 1937 only). A game reaching back to 1927 would estimate them from cases times about 5-10% (`computed`: 1953's 9.7%; 1950-52 ran 4-13%).
5. **Children's diarrhoea** deaths in Canada were already a handful a year in 2000, and I found no source for 1980-1999: the row has almost nothing to act on. TB has a 1980 rate (0.8 per 100,000) and a falling line, but nearly all deaths are in adults over 55.
6. **Paralytic against all cases in the 1950s.** See Section 1. For deaths per case use the Year Book's 1953 pair (481 of 4,963) or the 9-10% computed ratio, not PHAC counts against Rutty's all-case counts.
7. **Excluded.** A PAHO profile (the WHO's regional office) gave 1997 age-standardized pneumonia and influenza rates of 32 (men) and 19 (women) per 100,000 (`snippet`); WHO family, not shipped. No WHO or IHME series used.
8. **Could not read.** The Canadian Encyclopedia polio article (site frame only); Historical Statistics of Canada section B (index not found; try other table numbers); the printed Causes of Death volumes 1980-1999 (refused); Lassen's own paper (no abstract).
9. **Statistics Canada licence caveat.** The grant allows use, copying, sale and sub-licensing with a source line ("Source: Statistics Canada, product name, reference date"; "Adapted from ..." for changed data). A clause on merging or linking with other databases exists; my fetch summary quoted it without its qualifier. Read it in full at PO-30 before shipping (I believe it is aimed at identifying individuals, `memory`).
