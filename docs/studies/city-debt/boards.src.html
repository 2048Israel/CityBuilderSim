Finances › The book › 3½% Jan 2211 › Buy back

Closing early: the buyback as a reverse auction, and the call

The city names how much face it wants back and the most it will pay. Every holder offers what it holds, each slice at the price where its own buying rule stops wanting it, and never under fair. The city takes the cheapest offers and pays each its own price.

AOne rule, both directions. A household bought because the line paid more than a deposit; it sells only for what that spread is worth to it. The bank bought at fair; it sells at its desk's ask. Nobody sells under fair.

BMultiple price. Each accepted offer is paid its own price, as treasury buybacks are run; the stop is the dearest one taken.

CThree comparisons, one booked. Against carrying value is the statement's line. Against face is what the debt-to-GDP measure sees. Over fair is what the auction cost.

DThe call is a right the city paid for. Its coupon carried the option at the sale; past the first call date the line can be retired at 100 whatever the market says.

AFive kinds of paper. Bills at a discount, bonds at par near the market, the amortizing bond on one coupon solved on the curve, the floating note on the policy rate, the linked bond on the price index.

BThe coupon is the market's. The clearing yield rounded down to an eighth of a point, so a new line sells just under 100. A reopening keeps its line's coupon and sells at whatever price that makes.

CThe bidders are the game's own. Households first, by their own rule at the auction's yield and out of their savings; the firms with idle cash, as they send it abroad; the bank at fair while it has room; the world as hot money. The central bank never bids, except to roll its own paper.

DCapped, or filled. With a highest yield, bids that do not reach the issue there sell only what was bid. With Fill it, the yield rises until the bidders' own rules cover the issue, and the card says what that costs.

The curve is where the market expects the central bank to go

The short end is the dial. The long end is what the market expects the dial to be, month by month, plus a premium for time and for the city's debt, less what the central bank's holdings take off. A hike the market expects to undo inverts the curve.
