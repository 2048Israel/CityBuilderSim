# Gathered: medicine, 1996-2024

*Gathered 2026-11-16 for R6-G by a Sonnet agent; marks: page = seen on the opened page, snippet = search summary only. Kept as gathered for [the study](../research-1996-2024.md), which chooses and writes; its section 9 lists the keys it cites.*

Reading notes. PubMed pages would not open (cookie wall), so each journal abstract was read as its Europe PMC record (`ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:<PMID>`, or a title query); the PMID is in the source row. A Europe PMC abstract is marked `page`. Confidence intervals not printed in an abstract are written "CI not on page". Health Canada and EMA dates were looked up only where easy; elsewhere "not found" means not searched or not seen.

### 1. Combination antiretroviral therapy for HIV
- **First approval:** saquinavir (Invirase, Roche), the first protease inhibitor: FDA announcement 7 Dec 1995 (FDA's own timeline says 6 Dec) (snippet) [G-M4]. Indinavir (Crixivan, Merck): 13 Mar 1996, accelerated approval (snippet) [G-M4]. Ritonavir (Norvir, Abbott): March 1996, day unclear (snippet) [G-M4]. The XI International AIDS Conference, Vancouver, ran 7-12 Jul 1996 (snippet) [G-M5]. EMA, MHRA, Health Canada: not found.
- **Effect:** (a) Palella et al., HIV Outpatient Study, 1,255 patients with a CD4 count below 100, nine US clinics: deaths fell from 29.4 to 8.8 per 100 person-years between Q1 1995 and Q2 1997 (page); three major opportunistic infections fell from 21.9 per 100 person-years in 1994 to 3.7 by mid-1997 (page); the more intense the regimen, the lower the mortality (page) [G-M1]. An observational cohort, not a trial. (b) CDC, national estimate: AIDS deaths Jan-Jun 1996 were 22,000 against 24,900 in Jan-Jun 1995, down 13% (page) [G-M3]; across 1996-1997 estimated deaths among people with AIDS fell 42%, which CDC ties mainly to wide use of combination therapy (page) [G-M2].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no HIV).
- **Notes:** The 13% (half-year, 1996) and the 42% (1996-1997) are different periods; a "47%" figure seen in one search was a rise in cases, not a fall in deaths, and is not used. The 42% is in a 2001 MMWR (the "United States, 2000" update), not the 1997 report. The 1996 report itself says protease inhibitors "may further improve survival" (page) [G-M3].

### 2. Pneumococcal conjugate vaccine for children (PCV7, Prevnar)
- **First approval:** FDA licensed Prevnar (Wyeth) on 17 Feb 2000 (snippet, FDA product page per search summary) [G-M6]; the year 2000 and Wyeth as maker are confirmed on an opened page [G-M7]. Described as the first pneumococcal conjugate vaccine licensed in the US (page) [G-M7]. Health Canada, EMA: not found.
- **Effect:** (a) Black et al., Kaiser Northern California, double-blind, 37,868 children randomised to PCV7 or a meningococcal C control: efficacy against invasive disease from vaccine serotypes 97.4% (95% CI 82.7-99.9) in fully vaccinated children, 93.9% (79.6-98.5) by intention to treat; no rise in non-vaccine-serotype disease (page) [G-M8]. (b) Grijalva et al., US time-series from the Nationwide Inpatient Sample: all-cause pneumonia admissions in children under 2 were 39% lower (95% CI 22-52) by end 2004 than expected from 1997-99; about 41,000 admissions prevented in 2004; pneumococcal-coded pneumonia admissions fell 65% (47-77); dehydration admissions (the comparison) did not change (page) [G-M9]. (c) Griffin et al., 1997-99 against 2007-09: pneumonia admissions per 100,000 fell by 551.1 (95% CI 445.1-657.1) under age 2 (about 47,000 a year), 85.3 (7.0-163.6) at 65-74, 359.8 (199.6-520.0) at 75-84, 1,300.8 (984.0-1,617.6) at 85 and over (about 73,000 a year); age-adjusted all ages 54.8 (41.0-68.5) per 100,000, about 168,000 admissions a year (page) [G-M10]. The adult fall is the herd effect of the child programme.
- **Game:** pneumonia (a cause of death; children, and the old by the herd effect); general care's stays.
- **Notes:** The task's PMID 17618415 for Grijalva is wrong; the paper is PMID 17416262. Time-series estimates, not randomised.

### 3. Rotavirus vaccine (RotaTeq, Rotarix)
- **First approval:** RotaTeq (Merck) licensed by FDA in February 2006; Rotarix (GSK) in April 2008 (page, CDC ACIP report) [G-M12]. The day in February: not confirmed (one trade article said 1 Feb; 3 Feb not seen). EMA, MHRA, Health Canada: not found. An earlier rotavirus vaccine is not checked here.
- **Effect:** (a) REST trial (Vesikari et al., 2006): 34,035 infants on vaccine and 34,003 on placebo watched for safety; in the efficacy substudy, 98.0% (95% CI 88.3-100) against severe G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis; 74.0% (66.8-79.9) against any; hospitalisations and emergency visits for G1-G4 cut 94.5% (91.2-96.6); intussusception 12 vaccine v 15 placebo cases, relative risk 1.6 (0.4-6.4) (page) [G-M11]. Rotarix, per the CDC report: 84.7% (CI 71.7-92.4) in a Latin American trial and 95.8% (89.6-98.7) in a European one, against severe disease (page) [G-M12]. (b) Cortes et al., MarketScan claims, US children under 5, 2007-09 against 2001-06: rotavirus-coded hospitalisations down 75% (95% CI 72-77) in 2007-08 and 60% (58-63) in 2008-09; all diarrhoea hospitalisations down 33% (31-35) and 25% (23-27); about 64,855 fewer hospitalisations over the two years; also a fall among unvaccinated children in 2007-08 (page) [G-M13]. (c) CDC, 2000-2022: against the pre-vaccine period, rotavirus-coded hospitalisations are 65% lower and all gastroenteritis hospitalisations 34% lower, more so under age 2 (page; a conference abstract) [G-M14]. Before the vaccine rotavirus caused 55,000-70,000 US hospitalisations a year (page) [G-M12].
- **Game:** summer diarrhoea (children).
- **Notes:** The all-diarrhoea fall is smaller than the rotavirus-coded fall because rotavirus is only part of it; use the all-diarrhoea figure (25-34%) for a dial on the game's whole cause.

### 4. mRNA vaccines (COVID-19)
- **First approval:** UK MHRA, 2 Dec 2020, Pfizer/BioNTech vaccine, temporary authorisation under Regulation 174, the first COVID-19 vaccine approved in the UK (page) [G-M16]. FDA emergency use authorisation 11 Dec 2020; full approval as Comirnaty, age 16 and over, 23 Aug 2021 (page) [G-M17]. Health Canada, EMA: not found. Moderna's first approval: not found.
- **Effect:** Polack et al., NEJM 2020, placebo-controlled: 43,448 people received injections (21,720 vaccine, 21,728 placebo); 8 COVID-19 cases on vaccine against 162 on placebo; efficacy 95% (95% credible interval 90.3-97.6) (page) [G-M15]. The FDA's 2021 page says 91% on later data (page) [G-M17].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no COVID); the platform two later rows (an mRNA flu vaccine, a personalised mRNA cancer vaccine) depend on. Closest causes: childhood outbreaks, pneumonia.
- **Notes:** The task gives 43,548 for the trial; the abstract record shows 43,448 injected (the larger randomised figure was not on the record I read). The 95% is a credible interval (Bayesian), not a confidence interval.

### 5. Targeted cancer drugs (imatinib, CML)
- **First approval:** FDA, 10 May 2001, Gleevec (Novartis), for CML in blast crisis, accelerated phase, and chronic phase after interferon failure; reviewed in under three months; based on three studies of about 1,000 patients (page) [G-M18]. Health Canada: conditional authorisation (notice of compliance with conditions) September 2001, later than FDA (snippet) [G-M22]. EMA: not found.
- **Effect:** IRIS trial, 1,106 patients (553 imatinib, 553 interferon alfa plus low-dose cytarabine): at 18 months complete cytogenetic response 76.2% against 14.5%; freedom from progression to accelerated or blast phase 96.7% against 91.5% (page) [G-M19]. Overall survival with imatinib first-line: 89% at 60 months (page; CI not on page) [G-M20]; 83.3% at 10 years, median follow-up 10.9 years; 65.6% of the interferon arm crossed over, so the later analysis follows the imatinib arm only (page) [G-M21].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no cancer).
- **Notes:** No head-to-head survival figure against the interferon arm was on the pages read, because of the crossover; the effect is a before/after against the era's interferon regimen. 5-year and 10-year survival counts are overall (all causes).

### 6. Checkpoint immunotherapy (ipilimumab, pembrolizumab)
- **First approval:** ipilimumab (Yervoy, Bristol-Myers Squibb), FDA, 25 Mar 2011, unresectable or metastatic melanoma, the first drug shown to lengthen survival in the disease (snippet; several outlets agree) [G-M25]. Pembrolizumab (Keytruda), FDA accelerated approval 4 Sep 2014, advanced melanoma after ipilimumab (snippet) [G-M26]. EMA, Health Canada: not found.
- **Effect:** Hodi et al., NEJM 2010, 676 previously treated patients, randomised 3:1:1 to ipilimumab plus gp100 vaccine (403), ipilimumab alone (137) or gp100 alone (136): median overall survival 10.0 and 10.1 months against 6.4 months; hazard ratio for death 0.68 (95% CI 0.55-0.85, P<0.001) for ipilimumab plus gp100 and 0.66 (0.51-0.87, P=0.003) for ipilimumab alone, against gp100 alone (page) [G-M23, G-M24]. One-year survival 43.6% and 45.6% against 25.3%; two-year 21.6% and 23.5% against 13.7% (page) [G-M24].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no cancer).
- **Notes:** The comparator (gp100 vaccine) was an active control with little effect of its own; the figure is a survival gain of 3.6-3.7 months at the median.

### 7. The hepatitis C cure (sofosbuvir)
- **First approval:** Sovaldi (Gilead), FDA, 6 Dec 2013 (page, Gilead release dated that day; a government blog item is dated 9 Dec, the blog's date) [G-M27]. EMA: the committee (CHMP) gave a positive opinion on 22 Nov 2013 (snippet) [G-M32]; the European Commission's authorisation date: not found. Health Canada: not found.
- **Effect:** Against the interferon era: peginterferon alfa-2a plus ribavirin for 48 weeks gave a sustained response in 56% of 1,121 patients overall and 46% of those with genotype 1 (page) [G-M30]. Sofosbuvir: NEUTRINO, 327 untreated patients (genotypes 1, 4, 5, 6) on sofosbuvir plus peginterferon and ribavirin for 12 weeks, SVR12 90% (95% CI 87-93) (page) [G-M28]; FISSION, 499 genotype 2-3 patients, sofosbuvir plus ribavirin 12 weeks against peginterferon plus ribavirin 24 weeks, 67% in both arms (genotype 3 only 56% against genotype 2 at 97% on sofosbuvir) (page) [G-M28]; POSITRON, 207 patients who could not take interferon, SVR12 78% (72-83) against 0% on placebo (page) [G-M29]. Later all-oral combination ION-2, 440 genotype 1 patients who had failed interferon-based treatment: SVR12 94-99% over four arms (page) [G-M31]. Gilead's own summary: 50-90% across sofosbuvir-based regimens (page) [G-M27].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no hepatitis C); at most the long-term sick.
- **Notes:** Sofosbuvir's 2013 label combined it with interferon for genotype 1 and 4; the interferon-free 94-99% figures come from the later ledipasvir combination (ION-2), whose own approval date was not searched. Where interferon-free sofosbuvir was tested head-to-head (FISSION) it only matched interferon (67% v 67%) but with fewer side effects (page) [G-M28].

### 8. The HPV vaccine (Gardasil)
- **First approval:** Gardasil (Merck), FDA, 8 Jun 2006, females 9-26 (page, CDC ACIP report) [G-M33]. Health Canada, EMA: not found.
- **Effect:** Trials (combined protocols), HPV 16/18-related CIN 2/3 or adenocarcinoma in situ: efficacy 100% (CI 92.9-100) per protocol, and 39.0% (CI 23.3-51.7) by intention to treat, which counts women already infected (page) [G-M33]. Population: Falcaro et al., England's national programme (Cervarix, the bivalent vaccine, from 1 Sep 2008; girls 12-13 offered it routinely, 14-18 a catch-up), register data on women aged 20 to under 30: cervical cancer incidence 87% lower (95% CI 72-94) for girls offered the vaccine at 12-13, 62% (52-71) at 14-16, 34% (25-41) at 16-18; CIN3 97% (96-98), 75% (72-77), 39% (36-41); about 448 cancers (339-556) and 17,235 CIN3 cases (15,919-18,552) prevented by 30 Jun 2019 (page) [G-M34].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no cervical cancer or HPV).
- **Notes:** The measured population effect is for the bivalent vaccine, not Gardasil; a Gardasil-based population figure was not found. The 87% is for the youngest cohort, who were vaccinated before sexual activity.

### 9. The drug-coated coronary stent (Cypher)
- **First approval:** Cypher sirolimus-eluting stent (Cordis, Johnson & Johnson), FDA, 24 Apr 2003 (page, company release dated that day, which calls it the first US-approved drug-device combination against restenosis) [G-M35]. CE mark (Europe) earlier, announced 15 Apr 2002 (snippet) [G-M36]. Health Canada: not found.
- **Effect:** SIRIUS trial (Moses et al.), double-blind, 1,058 patients, 53 US centres: target-vessel failure within 270 days 8.6% with the sirolimus stent against 21.0% with a standard stent (P<0.001); target-lesion revascularisation 4.1% against 16.6% (page) [G-M37]. The abstract gives no restenosis percentage; in-stent restenosis rate: not found on a page. The company's release says re-blockage was cut by "more than 90 percent" across two trials of about 1,400 patients (page) [G-M35].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no heart disease).
- **Notes:** Both firm figures are repeat-procedure and failure rates, not deaths; the abstract does not give death or heart-attack differences separately.

### 10. Robot-assisted surgery (da Vinci)
- **First approval:** da Vinci Surgical System (Intuitive Surgical), FDA announcement 17 Jul 2000, cleared for laparoscopic gallbladder surgery and reflux surgery, described by FDA as the first system of its type (page, FDA announcement as reproduced by ScienceDaily) [G-M38]. The 11 Jul 2000 clearance date: not found. Health Canada, EMA: not found.
- **Effect:** (a) At clearance: 113 robotic patients against 132 standard laparoscopy; the robot matched laparoscopy on safety and effectiveness and took 40-50 minutes longer (page) [G-M38]. (b) Choi et al., Surgical Endoscopy 2024, meta-analysis of randomised trials: length of stay shorter by 0.23 days against laparoscopic surgery (P=0.01) and by 1.69 days against open surgery (P=0.001); operating time longer by 27.98 and 44.05 minutes; overall complications equal against laparoscopy (page; CIs and number of trials not on page) [G-M39].
- **Game:** general care's stays (hospital stay length) is the only possible handle; effect is small against keyhole surgery.
- **Notes:** The larger stay gain is against open surgery (a keyhole procedure would share much of it: likely, not shown on the pages read); against the keyhole comparator it is about a quarter of a day.

### 11. GLP-1 drugs for obesity (semaglutide 2.4 mg, Wegovy)
- **First approval:** Wegovy (Novo Nordisk), FDA, 4 Jun 2021, adults with obesity or overweight with a related condition (page, trade-press report of the FDA announcement; FDA's own page did not open) [G-M42]. Health Canada: 25 Nov 2021, Novo announcement (snippet) [G-M43]. EMA, MHRA: not found.
- **Effect:** (a) STEP 1 (Wilding et al.), 1,961 adults with overweight or obesity, no diabetes, 68 weeks: mean weight change -14.9% on semaglutide against -2.4% on placebo; difference -12.4 percentage points (95% CI -13.4 to -11.5); 86.4% lost at least 5% against 31.5% (page) [G-M40]. (b) SELECT (Lincoff et al.), 17,604 adults 45 or older with heart disease and BMI 27 or more, no diabetes, mean follow-up 39.8 months: heart death, nonfatal heart attack or nonfatal stroke in 569 of 8,803 (6.5%) on semaglutide against 701 of 8,801 (8.0%) on placebo; hazard ratio 0.80 (95% CI 0.72-0.90); stopping for side effects 16.6% against 8.2% (page) [G-M41].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no obesity, diabetes or heart disease).
- **Notes:** The "20% lower" is confirmed as hazard ratio 0.80 for the combined heart end point, not for deaths alone.

### 12. Alzheimer's antibodies (lecanemab, Leqembi)
- **First approval:** FDA accelerated approval 6 Jan 2023 (page, Alzheimer's Association; FDA press title seen in a search summary) [G-M45]; traditional approval 6 Jul 2023, Eisai (page) [G-M46]. EMA, MHRA, Health Canada: not found.
- **Effect:** CLARITY AD (van Dyck et al.), 1,795 early-stage patients (898 lecanemab, 897 placebo), 18 months: change on the dementia sum-of-boxes score 1.21 against 1.66, difference -0.45 (95% CI -0.67 to -0.23, P<0.001); brain swelling or fluid (ARIA with edema or effusions) in 12.6%; infusion reactions 26.4% (page) [G-M44]. The 27% slowing is the sponsors' ratio (0.45/1.66); the Alzheimer's Association page states "27%" (page) [G-M45]; the abstract and FDA's page give no percentage [G-M44, G-M46].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no dementia); a lever on Nursing Home or Assisted Living demand only if dementia were added.
- **Notes:** 1,795 confirmed. A 2026 Cochrane view that the benefit is not clinically meaningful appears in the earlier sheet (research-near-future/medicine.md), not rechecked here.

### 13. RSV vaccines for adults 60 and over (Arexvy)
- **First approval:** Arexvy (GSK), FDA, 3 May 2023, adults 60 and over, the first RSV vaccine approved in the US (page) [G-M48]. EMA, MHRA, Health Canada: not found.
- **Effect:** AReSVi-006 (Papi et al.), 24,966 adults 60 and over (12,467 vaccine, 12,499 placebo), median follow-up 6.7 months: efficacy against RSV lower-respiratory-tract disease 82.6% (96.95% CI 57.9-94.1), 7 cases against 40; against severe lower-respiratory disease 94.1% (95% CI 62.4-99.9); against RSV acute respiratory infection 71.7% (56.2-82.3) (page) [G-M47]; FDA's page repeats 82.6% and 94.1% (page) [G-M48].
- **Game:** pneumonia among the old, possibly (RSV lower-respiratory disease is the path); general care's stays.
- **Notes:** A single RSV season of follow-up; the waning figures in the earlier sheet were not rechecked.

### 14. Gene editing as a treatment (Casgevy)
- **First approval:** UK MHRA authorised 15 Nov 2023 (announced 16 Nov), sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent beta-thalassaemia, age 12 and over, the first licensed CRISPR medicine (page) [G-M50]. FDA, 8 Dec 2023, sickle cell disease age 12 and over (page) [G-M51]. Health Canada, EMA: not found.
- **Effect:** Frangoul et al., NEJM 2024, 44 patients treated, 30 with enough follow-up: 29 of 30 (97%, 95% CI 83-100) had no severe pain crisis (vaso-occlusive crisis) for at least 12 months in a row; 30 of 30 (100%, 88-100) no crisis hospitalisation for 12 months; median follow-up 19.3 months (page) [G-M49]. FDA: 29 of 31 evaluable patients (93.5%) (page) [G-M51]. MHRA: 28 of 29 (97%) (page) [G-M50].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no sickle cell).
- **Notes:** The three denominators (29, 30, 31) are different data cuts of the same trial; use the journal's 29 of 30. Single-arm trial, no placebo arm.

### 15. Hospital care at home
- **First approval:** CMS Acute Hospital Care at Home initiative announced 25 Nov 2020, hospital-by-hospital waiver, covering more than 60 acute conditions such as pneumonia and heart failure (page) [G-M54]; six health systems with prior experience (Brigham and Women's, Massachusetts General, Huntsman Cancer Institute, Mount Sinai, Presbyterian Healthcare Services, UnityPoint) were approved at once (page) [G-M55]. Earlier: the Johns Hopkins model dates from about 1995 (snippet) [G-M58]; Leff's study ran at two Medicare managed-care systems and a VA hospital (page) [G-M53]; Presbyterian (Albuquerque) began its programme in October 2008 (snippet) [G-M58]; about 20 programmes existed before the waiver, mostly in Medicare Advantage and VA settings (snippet) [G-M56]. First commercial or insurer-paid programme's exact date: not found.
- **Effect:** (a) Levine et al., Annals of Internal Medicine 2020, randomised, 91 adults admitted through the emergency department (43 home, 48 hospital): cost of the episode 38% lower (95% CI 24-49%); readmission within 30 days 7% against 23% (page; length of stay and deaths not in the abstract) [G-M52]. (b) Leff et al. 2005, quasi-experiment, 455 older patients (pneumonia, heart failure, COPD, cellulitis): stay 3.2 days at home against 4.9 in hospital (P=0.004); mean cost $5,081 against $7,480 (P<0.001) (page) [G-M53]. (c) Cochrane review of admission-avoidance hospital at home: six-month mortality risk ratio 0.77 (95% CI 0.60-0.99), 912 participants (snippet; moderate certainty; the 2024 update's figures not seen) [G-M57].
- **Game:** the Home Care Service; general care's stays.
- **Notes:** Levine's 38% and 7% against 23% are confirmed. Small trial (91). The shorter stay in (b) is a self-selected comparison (69% took the home option at two sites).

### 16. The six-month all-oral cure for drug-resistant TB (BPaL, BPaLM)
- **First approval:** pretomanid (TB Alliance), FDA, 14 Aug 2019, with bedaquiline and linezolid (BPaL), adults with extensively drug-resistant TB or MDR-TB not responding to or not tolerating other treatment (page) [G-M59]. WHO: the 2022 update puts the six-month BPaLM regimen for rifampicin-resistant TB first, the WHO item dated 15 Dec 2022 (page; the date taken from the item's URL) [G-M63]. The May 2022 BPaL recommendation in the earlier sheet was not rechecked. EMA, Health Canada: not found.
- **Effect:** (a) Nix-TB (Conradie et al., NEJM 2020), single-arm, 109 patients with highly resistant TB: 90% (95% CI 83-95) favourable outcome 6 months after treatment; 7 deaths (page) [G-M60]; TB Alliance's page gives 95 of the first 107 (page) [G-M59]. (b) ZeNix (Conradie et al., NEJM 2022), 181 patients, 88% with XDR or pre-XDR TB: favourable outcome 84% to 93% across four linezolid dose-and-length arms (page) [G-M61]. (c) TB-PRACTECAL (Nyang'wa et al., NEJM 2022), randomised, 301 patients in stage 2: unfavourable outcome at 72 weeks 11% on BPaLM against 48% on standard care (modified intention to treat), difference -37 percentage points (96.6% CI -53 to -22); per protocol 4% against 12%; serious or grade 3+ adverse events 19% against 59% (page) [G-M62]; WHO states success 89% against 52% (page) [G-M63]. The comparator regimens last 9-24 months (page; trial protocol abstract) and the old ones leave success near 60% worldwide (snippet, MSF) [G-M64].
- **Game:** tuberculosis.
- **Notes:** The 52% is the trial's control arm, not a world figure; the 60% world figure is a secondary source with no year seen.

### 17. The rapid molecular TB test (Xpert MTB/RIF)
- **First approval:** WHO endorsed it on 8 Dec 2010 (page): fully automated, a result in about 100 minutes where older methods could take up to three months; the endorsement followed 18 months of assessment [G-M65]. FDA market authorisation: 25 Jul 2013, announced by Cepheid, a de novo clearance (snippet) [G-M68]. Health Canada, EMA: not found.
- **Effect:** (a) TB-NEAT (Theron et al., Lancet 2014), randomised, primary-care clinics in Africa, 758 patients on smear microscopy and 744 on Xpert: sensitivity 83% against 50% in culture-positive patients; same-day diagnosis 24% against 13%; same-day treatment start 23% against 15% (P=0.0002); the patients' illness-score and performance-score outcomes at 2 and 6 months did not differ (page; the abstract gives no mortality or numeric time to treatment) [G-M66]. (b) Di Tanna et al., Lancet Global Health 2019, individual-patient meta-analysis of five randomised trials, 8,567 adults: six-month mortality odds ratio 0.88 (95% CI 0.68-1.14, P=0.34), 182 of 4,050 deaths on Xpert against 217 of 4,093 on smear; in HIV-positive patients a secondary analysis hazard ratio 0.76 (0.60-0.97) (page) [G-M67].
- **Game:** tuberculosis.
- **Notes:** No mortality difference overall is a finding; a time-to-treatment gain exists (more same-day starts) but its size in days was not found.
