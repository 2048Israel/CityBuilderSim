# Gathered: energy, 1996-2024

*Gathered 2026-11-16 for R6-G by a Sonnet agent; marks: page = seen on the opened page, snippet = search summary only. Kept as gathered for [the study](../research-1996-2024.md), which chooses and writes; its section 9 lists the keys it cites.*

"Derived" = my arithmetic on a marked figure, not a published value. Heat-rate conversions use 3,412 Btu/kWh. Makers quote LHV; EIA heat rates are HHV on net output, so the two are not one basis.

### 1. Horizontal wells with slickwater fracturing
- **First commercial:** The two parts are five years apart. Slickwater: Mitchell Energy (Barnett Shale, north Texas) pumped its first slickwater treatment May 1997, a design from UPR's Cotton Valley work; general Barnett use as "light sand fracs" from September 1998 (page) [G-E1]. Mitchell's own horizontals failed commercially (1991 GRI/DOE test; two wells in 1998, treatments too small) (page) [G-E1]. Horizontals with large fracs became commercial under Devon, whose first came on production December 2002 and January 2003 (snippet) [G-E3a]; its program launched "in the past year" as of 19 Jan 2004, 65-70 of 315-330 wells in 2003 horizontal, about 95 of about 150 planned for 2004 (page) [G-E3].
- **Effect:** Slickwater against gel: fracturing cost down about $140,000 a well (page) [G-E2]; an early estimate put slickwater at as little as 20% of gel cost (page, estimate) [G-E1]. Late-1980s gel fracs gave initial rates of about 900-1,100 Mcf/D, and a 1989 model needed 1.0 Bcf over 25 years to pay; with light sand fracs and the upper Barnett the core-area average EUR reached about 1.25 Bcf a well (page, undated) [G-E1]. Horizontal against vertical: Devon expected 2-2.5 times the rate and total recovery of a vertical; core horizontals averaged about 2.5 MMscfd initially (1.5 outside), EUR 2.5-3.5 Bcf (1.5-2.5 outside) (page; company estimates) [G-E3]. EIA shares: shale gas 8% of US gas in 2007 (page) [G-E5]; shale gas and tight oil 16% of gas and about 12% of crude in December 2008, 70% and 60% in December 2018 (page) [G-E4]; tight oil about 64% of 2023 crude, 8.32 million b/d (page) [G-E6]; shale about 77% of 2023 dry gas (snippet) [G-E7].
- **Cost (not an effect):** Horizontal well about $1.6 million against $680,000-800,000 for a core vertical (page, 2003-04) [G-E3]. A secondary account says slickwater saved $75,000-100,000 a well (snippet; conflicts with $140,000) [G-E8].
- **Game:** The Oil Well (a natural-gas good only with a later power batch).
- **Notes:** The design notes' 1997 is the slickwater date, not the horizontal one; the pairing dates from 2002-03. EIA re-based shale shares in 2019 (about +9 points for gas) (snippet) [G-E7], and the 2007 page's 8% sits oddly with its own volumes (5 of 46 Bcf/d is about 11%) [G-E5]. No 2000 share found.

### 2. The multi-megawatt wind turbine
- **First commercial:** Not pinned down. Enercon's timeline lists the E-66/1.5 MW as its 1995 "move into the megawatt class", "the most powerful wind turbine available at the time", over 2,700 sold (page; first-installation date not given) [G-E10]. A 1996 trade-journal piece has an E-66 1.5 MW prototype starting early August (year garbled) (snippet) [G-E10a]. Zond (later Enron Wind, then GE) began a 1.5 MW in 1996 and claims first of its class commercially (snippet, brochure) [G-E11]. US average crossed 1.5 MW about 2006: 1.60 MW in 2006, 1.65 in 2007, 1.67 in 2008; turbines above 1.5 MW were 24% of 2004-05 installs, 34% of 2006, 40% of 2007 (snippet, LBNL slides) [G-E12].
- **Effect:** 2022 installs, LBNL: average 3.2 MW, up 350% since 1998-99 (derived 1998-99: about 0.7 MW); rotor 131.6 m, up 173% (derived about 48 m); hub 98.1 m, up 73% (derived about 57 m). Capacity factor in 2022 by project vintage: 23% (built 1998-2003), 31% (2004-2012), 40% (2013-2021); fleet 36% (page) [G-E9]. The game notes' 0.7 to 3.2 MW and 23% against 40% are confirmed.
- **Cost (not an effect):** not gathered.
- **Game:** The Wind Farm (from 1980, 20 MW, 33.8% today).
- **Notes:** LBNL ties the gain to lower specific power (bigger rotors per MW) and taller hubs together. Editions differ (2024 edition: rotor 133.8 m, hub 103.4 m, snippet); do not mix.

### 3. Offshore wind fixed to the seabed at scale
- **First commercial:** Vindeby (Lolland, Denmark), 1991, a "demonstration project" (page) [G-E17]; 11 Bonus 450 kW units, 4.95 MW (snippet) [G-E18]. Horns Rev 1 (North Sea, 14-20 km off Denmark; Vattenfall 60%, Ørsted 40%; 80 Vestas V80 of 2.0 MW, 160 MW): construction permit 29 Mar 2001; production licence 25 Jul 2002; turbines installed and commissioned in 2002 (page) [G-E15]; last turbine on 11 Dec 2002 (page) [G-E13]; inauguration 2003, 24 August (page) [G-E14]. "First large-scale offshore wind farm", 160 MW being four times the largest before it (Middelgrunden 40 MW), first on monopiles, first with an offshore transformer platform (page) [G-E13] [G-E15].
- **Effect:** Annual net output 600 GWh stated for Horns Rev 1 (page, Wikipedia infobox, probably a design figure) [G-E13]; derived about 43%. Turbine no. 6 reached 100 GWh in about 13 years (May 2015) (page) [G-E16]; derived about 44% of 2 MW. Vindeby: 243 GWh over about 25 years (snippet) [G-E18]; derived about 22% of 4.95 MW. A measured farm capacity factor for Horns Rev 1 from the operator or regulator: not found (the 47.7% and 52.0% found belong to Horns Rev 2).
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** nothing yet (the power study leaves offshore out).
- **Notes:** 2002 (licence, commissioning) against 2003 (inauguration): 2002 is better supported, by the regulator's own filing. Vattenfall's plant page lists 158 MW (snippet).

### 4. Floating wind farm
- **First commercial:** Hywind Scotland, 25 km off Peterhead; Equinor; five Siemens 6 MW units, 30 MW; commissioned October 2017 (page, Wikipedia) [G-E20]; Equinor counts November 2017 to January 2018 as its first three full months (page) [G-E19]. (The 2009 2.3 MW Hywind demonstrator is a pilot.)
- **Effect:** About 65% capacity factor over November-January against 45-60% quoted for a typical bottom-fixed farm in winter (page, operator, winter only) [G-E19]. Average 54% over the first five years (page, Wikipedia; other reports attach 54% to the first two years) [G-E20]. 57.1% for twelve months to March 2021 (one outlet says 2020), a UK record; UK offshore average about 40% (snippet) [G-E21].
- **Cost (not an effect):** GBP 264 million, about GBP 8,800 a kW (page, Wikipedia; compiler's division) [G-E20].
- **Game:** nothing in the game.
- **Notes:** The winter figure is not annual; use 54-57%. The near-future sheet's NREL $7,349/kW is a model, not a measured cost.

### 5. Utility-scale solar: trackers, module efficiency, PERC, bifacial
- **First commercial:** Single-axis trackers: first utility-scale tracker order 250 kW for BP Solar in 2001 (Array Technologies) (page, sponsored) [G-E25]; Nellis Air Force Base, 14 MW, SunPower single-axis trackers, inaugurated 17 Dec 2007 (page for size and single axis; date from search summary) [G-E24]; LBNL notes the first two utility-scale projects reached operation in late 2007 (snippet) [G-E22a]. PERC: reported by UNSW in 1989 (22-23% lab); rapid commercial adoption from 2013 (page) [G-E27]; another source says 2012 (snippet) [G-E27a]. Bifacial: first date not found.
- **Effect:** Trackers: roughly 15-25% more energy than fixed tilt (page, an abstract's background statement, NREL and Univ. of Arizona, IEEE JPV 2019) [G-E23]; SunPower claimed up to 30% for Nellis (page, maker) [G-E24]; an LBNL study found tracking added 4.05 to capacity factor at equal sunlight and inverter loading, unit unstated (snippet) [G-E22b]. 96% of new US utility PV capacity in 2023 is single-axis tracking (page) [G-E22]. Fleet median capacity factor 24% AC, plants 7-37% (page), matching the game's 24% [G-E22]. Module efficiency: commercial monocrystalline modules rose from about 17% to just under 25% over the past decade; shipment-weighted average of crystalline silicon 22.7% in Q4 2024 against 21.6% in Q4 2023 (page, Fraunhofer ISE, 14 Jul 2026) [G-E26]. PERC took about 90% of 2022 cell output (page) [G-E28]; one review puts it about 1.2 points absolute above Al-BSF (snippet) [G-E27b]; Fraunhofer notes TOPCon and heterojunction now replace PERC (page) [G-E26]. Bifacial on trackers: 4-15% more yield, global average 9% (page, modelled and validated) [G-E23].
- **Cost (not an effect):** LBNL installed cost $1.43/WAC ($1.08/WDC) for 2023, down 75% since 2010 (page) [G-E22]; the game's $1,484/kW is not on it. Trackers cost slightly more than fixed tilt ($1.4 against $1.2/WAC) (snippet) [G-E22c].
- **Game:** The Solar Farm, from 1982 (Lugo) at today's figures (24%, $1,484/kW).
- **Notes:** Best rows with a date and a figure: single-axis trackers (2007, 15-25%) and module efficiency (about 17% to about 25% over ten years; no earlier series found). Fraunhofer's module figures are the compilers' tally of data sheets.

### 6. The four-hour lithium-ion grid battery
- **First commercial:** An earlier four-hour unit exists: SCE Tehachapi, 8 MW / 32 MWh, LG Chem, operational 24 Sep 2014 (snippet) [G-E32]. Of the 2017 group (after the Aliso Canyon leak): Tesla at SCE Mira Loma, 20 MW / 80 MWh, completed late December 2016 and brought online, per unnamed sources (page, Electrek 23 Jan 2017) [G-E30]; inaugurated on a Monday, article 1 Feb 2017, commissioning date undisclosed; Greensmith claimed it put the first of three projects online (page) [G-E31]. AES at SDG&E Escondido, 30 MW / 120 MWh, showcased 24 Feb 2017 (page; operating date not given) [G-E29]. Hornsdale (Neoen, Tesla): 100 MW discharge, 80 MW charge, 129 MWh, about 75 minutes at full discharge (page) [G-E33]; official start 1 Dec 2017 (snippet) [G-E33a].
- **Effect:** US utility battery fleet, 2019: round-trip efficiency 82% against 79% for pumped storage; average duration a little over 46 minutes in 2015, 1.5 hours in 2019 (page, EIA) [G-E34]. Hornsdale's response to a generator trip on 18 Dec 2017 was fully consistent with the expected response (page, AEMO; no efficiency figure) [G-E33].
- **Cost (not an effect):** The power study's $1,521/kW and 85% from EIA: not found on any page opened. NREL 2025 four-hour range about $147-243/kWh (snippet) [G-E34a]. Tehachapi about $50 million (snippet) [G-E32].
- **Game:** a later row; the power study's battery.
- **Notes:** A four-hour lithium-ion plant ran in 2014 at 8 MW, so 2017 is first at tens of MW, not first at four hours. Mira Loma's date rests on reporters' sources, Escondido's on a press showcase: use December 2016 to February 2017.

### 7. Tandem perovskite-silicon solar module
- **First commercial:** Oxford PV, first commercial shipment 5 Sep 2024, to an undisclosed US customer for a utility-scale project, from its pilot line in Brandenburg an der Havel, Germany (page) [G-E35] [G-E36].
- **Effect:** 24.5% module efficiency as shipped, 72 cells (Oxford PV's page); lab module record 26.9% (60-cell) [G-E35]. PV Tech's page calls 24.5% the cell figure [G-E36]. Claimed up to 20% more energy than a standard silicon panel (page, company claim, no silicon figure) [G-E35]. For scale: silicon module average 22.7% in Q4 2024 (page) [G-E26]. Wattage and volume: not stated.
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** a later row.
- **Notes:** The two pages disagree on module against cell; the company's says module. A pilot line, so a shipment, not a factory.

### 8. Vanadium flow battery at grid scale; sodium-ion
- **First commercial:** Flow: Rongke Power, Dalian, first phase 100 MW / 400 MWh (four hours, derived), connected by 29 Sep 2022 (article date; operation expected mid-October); 200 MW / 800 MWh planned (page) [G-E37]. Sodium-ion: CATL unveiled its first-generation cell 29 Jul 2021 (160 Wh/kg, 80% charge in 15 minutes at room temperature, above 90% retention at -20 C) (page) [G-E40]: an unveiling, not commercial use. First grid use found: China Southern Power Grid Fulin, Nanning, 10 MWh (page, undated; its link points to a 15 May 2024 report); Datang Hubei, Qianjiang, 50 MW / 100 MWh first phase, connected 30 Jun 2024, 185 Ah HiNa cells, over 300 cycles a year, about 12,000 households' daily need (page) [G-E39].
- **Effect:** Round-trip efficiency, flow or sodium: not found. CATL's figures are the maker's, at unveiling.
- **Cost (not an effect):** Dalian first phase RMB 1.9 billion, about US$298 million (snippet, CNESA) [G-E38].
- **Game:** later rows.
- **Notes:** Sodium-ion belongs at 2024, not 2021. The near-future sheet's 1 GWh flow and 60 GWh CATL items are 2026.

### 9. Seawater reverse osmosis with energy recovery
- **First commercial:** Isobaric pressure exchanger (PX), Energy Recovery Inc.: first installed in the Canary Islands in 1997, first sales to SWRO that year (page) [G-E42]. Large plant: Ashkelon, Israel (IDE; mid and end 2005), 40 DWEER work exchangers (page) [G-E43]; whole-plant kWh/m3 not on the page. Since about 2000 isobaric devices have replaced centrifugal ones in most new SWRO plants (page) [G-E41].
- **Effect:** Device efficiency: turbine 75%, turbocharger 80%, Pelton 85%, isobaric about 95-97%; recovery saves generally 25-40% (page) [G-E41]. Plant specific energy by device: Francis turbine 6.2-6.7 kWh/m3, Pelton 3.5-5.9, PX 3.0-5.3, piston 3.5-4.6 (page, table of ranges); some plants with the latest isobaric devices about 3 [G-E41]. Maker: PX cuts SWRO energy cost by up to 60% (page) [G-E42]; Stover (ERI), Desalination 203, 2007: up to 60% against no recovery (snippet) [G-E45]. Sorek (mid-2013): 2014 average use below the 3.414 kWh/m3 contract ceiling (page; no exact figure) [G-E44]. The earlier 2.3 kWh/m3 best: not reconfirmed.
- **Cost (not an effect):** not gathered.
- **Game:** The Desalination Plant (from 1957, flash distillation).
- **Notes:** No one-plant before and after found; the clean before and after is the table (about 6.2-6.7 with a turbine, 3.0-5.3 with PX). A news snippet's 3.5 kWh/m3 for Ashkelon is secondary; not used.

### 10. Combined-cycle gas plant at 60% efficiency
- **First commercial:** Irsching 4 (Bavaria), Siemens SGT5-8000H, E.ON: record test May 2011, 60.75% net, over 578 MW from one gas turbine, generator and steam turbine; handed to E.ON July 2011 (page; commercial-operation date not on it) [G-E46]. Later: Bouchain (EDF, France, GE 9HA) 62.22% net, Guinness June 2016; Nishi-Nagoya 1 (Chubu, Japan, three 7HA.01, over 1,188 MW) 63.08% gross, article 27 Mar 2018 (page) [G-E47].
- **Effect:** 60.75% net against US plants: average tested heat rate of gas combined cycle 7,549 Btu/kWh in 2023 (derived 45.2%, HHV) and 7,655 in 2015 (44.6%) (page) [G-E52]; all gas plants' operating average 7,721 in 2023 (44.2%) (page) [G-E51]. About one-third less gas a kWh than the world average of installed combined cycle (page, Siemens claim as reported) [G-E46].
- **Cost (not an effect):** not stated.
- **Game:** a later batch adds a combined-cycle plant on imported gas.
- **Notes:** LHV against HHV: 60.75% against 45% overstates the gap. The fleet barely moved in eight years; the step is in new plants.

### 11. Ultra-supercritical coal
- **First commercial:** Nordjyllandsværket unit 3 (Denmark): 411 MW, inlet 285 bar and 580 C, double reheat; start-up scheduled October 1998, commercial operation due 1 Oct 1998 (page, pre-start article) [G-E48]. Isogo (Yokohama, J-Power) unit 1: 600 MW, April 2002; unit 2: 600 MW, July 2009, 25 MPa, 600 C main, 620 C reheat, replacing two 265 MW units of 1967 and 1969 (page) [G-E50]. Japan's 1,000 MW Haramachi and Shinchi were "recently commissioned" in a 1999 article (page, no year) [G-E49].
- **Effect:** NJV3 planned net 47% LHV; Avedøre 2 just over 48%; large SC and USC plants 43-45% LHV, Shinchi measured above 43% LHV (page, Oct 1999, mostly planned values) [G-E49]. Isogo 2 about 45% gross LHV (page) [G-E50]. Subcritical baseline: US coal operating heat rate 10,745 Btu/kWh in 2023 (derived 31.8%, HHV net); tested coal steam units 10,020 (34.1%) (page, EIA) [G-E51] [G-E52]. A snippet gives NJV3 47% LHV and 44.9% HHV, "most efficient coal unit", as Vattenfall's own claim [G-E48a].
- **Cost (not an effect):** not stated.
- **Game:** the coal plant (40.2% today; the supercritical reserve of 1957 at about 40% against 29.9%).
- **Notes:** The game's 29.9% and 40% are on another basis than EIA's 32-34% HHV fleet figures. A forum post disputes "ultra-supercritical" for 1998 (supercritical dates from the 1960s) (snippet, informal). No pre-2000 Japanese 600 C unit dated; Isogo 1 (2002) is the earliest here.

### 12. LED street lighting
- **First commercial:** Los Angeles, the largest city retrofit: launched 2009; 141,089 LED luminaires, completion announced June 2012 per the city case study (page) [G-E53]; CleanTechnica (Sept 2013) says end of June 2013 (page) [G-E54]. The case study, which says 2012 twice, is better supported.
- **Effect:** Before: 209,000 lights, about 197 GWh a year, $15 million (2008). After: 63.15% cut in the network's yearly energy (2012 measurements, against a 35% plan); annual saving 110.45 GWh and $9.8 million in 2017-18, over 80% of about 220,000 lights LED (page, city figures via ICLEI; derived 110.45 of 197 GWh is 56%, network grew) [G-E53]. About 63% less electricity than the sodium units replaced (page, analyst, basis unstated) [G-E54].
- **Cost (not an effect):** Seven-year US$40 million loan at 5.25% (page) [G-E53]; city estimate $7 million a year electricity plus $2.5 million maintenance (page, estimate) [G-E54].
- **Game:** nothing in the game.
- **Notes:** The 63.15% and 110.45 GWh are on different bases. Other cities not pursued.
