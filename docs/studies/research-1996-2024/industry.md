# Gathered: industry, mining, farming and transport, 1996-2024

*Gathered 2026-11-16 for R6-G by a Sonnet agent; marks: page = seen on the opened page, snippet = search summary only. Kept as gathered for [the study](../research-1996-2024.md), which chooses and writes; its section 9 lists the keys it cites.*

Conventions: every figure ends with its mark and key. "calc" = my arithmetic on a marked figure (unit conversion only). Wikipedia pages are marked `page` (opened) but are encyclopedia type: use for orientation, not as the effect source. Dates and figures are facts; wording is paraphrased.

### 1. GM crops (Bt maize, herbicide-tolerant soybeans)
- **First commercial:** USDA ERS: genetically engineered seed was first commercially introduced for the major field crops in 1996 (page) [G-I2]. Klümper and Qaim place the start of commercial adoption in the mid-1990s (page) [G-I1]. Month, place and seed company: not found.
- **Effect:** The game's cited meta-analysis was opened and confirmed. Against conventional crops: yield +21% in the body text, +22% in the abstract (page) [G-I1]; chemical pesticide quantity -37% (page) [G-I1]; pesticide cost -39% (page) [G-I1]; farmer profit +69% in the body text, +68% in the abstract (page) [G-I1]. Adoption in the US, 2025: herbicide-tolerant soybeans 96% of acres (17% in 1997), Bt corn 87% (about 8% in 1997), herbicide-tolerant upland cotton 93% (about 10% in 1997) (page) [G-I2].
- **Cost (not an effect):** seed and technology fees: not found.
- **Game:** the Grain Farm and the Mixed Farm.
- **Notes:** Klümper and Qaim: PLOS ONE 9(11): e111629, 3 Nov 2014; the body and abstract differ by one point on yield and profit, so quote either with its source. Results are averages across crops and countries; developing-country yield gains were not read on the page. ERS gives no 1996 adoption figure: 1997 is its earliest year.

### 2. GPS autosteer and precision guidance on farm machines
- **First commercial:** 1999, the AutoFarm division of Novariant, described as the first commercially available GPS automated steering system for farm equipment (page) [G-I3]. A second search summary says AutoFarm's RTK AutoSteer shipped to farmers in California, Arizona and Australia, and that a video history says "around 2000" (snippet) [G-I8]: 1999 is a trade-press timeline date, not confirmed by a second source. John Deere's AutoTrac launched as a commercial product in 2002 (page) [G-I4]. Trimble's AutoPilot date: not found.
- **Effect:** Best measured figure: a peer-reviewed field trial of a tractor-seeder in small irregular Finnish fields, GPS-guided autosteer against manual driving: overlap and unnecessary movement -21%, total work time -9.7%, fuel use -20%, operator mean heart rate -10%, all p < 0.01 (Haapala et al., Agronomy Research 23(1), published 16 May 2025) (page) [G-I5]. A North Dakota survey (survey-based, not a controlled trial) estimated machine time a further -5.75% and fuel -5.33% from autosteering (snippet) [G-I9]. USDA ERS: guidance reduces operator fatigue (page) [G-I6]; half of the farms using autosteer named saving labour time as a reason (snippet) [G-I8]; ERS gives no figure for labour, overlap or inputs (page) [G-I6][G-I7]. Industry-funded study (AEM, ASA, CropLife, TFI, 2021): about 15,000 tons of herbicide avoided and 10.2 million acres of cropland avoided through auto guidance, variable rate and section control combined; not peer-reviewed, not autosteer alone (page, as reported by trade press) [G-I3].
- **Adoption (ERS):** guidance on 34% of US soybean farms in 2012 (page) [G-I6]; in the largest farm class 73% of corn farms (2016), 82% of winter-wheat farms (2017), 68% of soybean farms (2018), 67% of cotton farms (2019) against 10%, 7%, 11% and 50% in the smallest class (page) [G-I7].
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** the Grain Farm and the Mixed Farm (tractor and combine operator posts).
- **Notes:** the Finnish trial is a Nordic small-field result; the Italian trial summary (overlap -88.9% and fuel -11.5% for semi-automatic guidance, not full autosteer) is snippet only and was not opened. No US government measurement of fuel or overlap saving was found. Posts: no source gives operator posts saved; autosteer cuts fatigue and heart rate, not the number of drivers.

### 3. Gene-edited crops
- **First commercial:** Calyxt announced its first commercial sale of Calyno high-oleic soybean oil in a release dated 26 Feb 2019; the release gives no separate sale date (page) [G-I10]. Buyer: US foodservice (frying, dressings, sauces) (page) [G-I10]. A second search summary dates the first sale 1 Mar 2019 in secondary coverage, and Calyxt's quarterly filing says only "first quarter 2019" (snippet) [G-I10]. Builder: Calyxt.
- **Effect:** quality trait, not yield: oleic acid about 80%, zero trans fat per serving, up to 20% less saturated fat than commodity soybean oil, up to three times the fry life (company claims) (page) [G-I10]. Yield: the company's filings say the edited variety performs comparably to its unedited counterpart in multi-location trials in Minnesota and South Dakota, no number given (snippet) [G-I11]. Independent measured yield for a commercial gene-edited crop: not found.
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** not specified in the brief; the same farms as subject 1 if used.
- **Notes:** the earlier gathering also found no measured yield; confirmed. Calyxt's own claim of "first gene-edited food to reach US commercialisation" follows an FDA consultation (snippet) [G-I10].

### 4. Driverless haul trucks in mines
- **First commercial:** January 2008, Codelco's Gabriela Mistral copper mine, Chile, Komatsu FrontRunner; Komatsu says it began the trial in 2005 and calls January 2008 the world's first commercial AHS deployment (page, builder's own statement) [G-I12]. Fleet: 20 Komatsu 930E-class trucks, described as a fully autonomous fleet (snippet) [G-I16]. Rio Tinto: five-truck trial at West Angelas, Pilbara, with trials said to begin December 2008 (snippet) [G-I15]; the trial trucks dumped waste only (snippet) [G-I15]; the first production use was Yandicoogina, 10 trucks (5 moved from West Angelas, 5 new), reported 16 Apr 2012, with 150 more Komatsu trucks ordered over four years (page) [G-I14]. Rio's trucks at West Angelas had logged about 897 days by 19 Feb 2012 (page) [G-I14]. Better supported as "first commercial anywhere": Gabriela Mistral (2008, builder's statement, only Komatsu's word for "first"); Rio's 2008 start was a trial.
- **Effect:** Komatsu: customers' load-and-haul unit costs fell by more than 15% against conventional haulage; tyre life +40% (builder's claim, page) [G-I12]. Rio Tinto, reported 3 Sep 2015: autonomous trucks 12% more productive than the manned fleet, load-and-haul costs cut 13% (page) [G-I13]. Rio Tinto 2016: each autonomous truck ran an extra 1,000 hours a year at 15% lower unit cost; 2017: an extra 700 hours at 15% lower (snippet) [G-I15]. Caterpillar claims up to 30% productivity over operator-driven trucks (vendor, snippet) [G-I17]. Cumulative: Komatsu 1.5 billion tons by end 2017 and over 100 AHS trucks by Jan 2018 (page) [G-I12]; Rio over one billion tonnes since 2008 across five Pilbara sites (snippet) [G-I15].
- **Drivers removed:** one in-cab driver per truck is removed by definition; trucks are supervised remotely (Rio from Perth, about 1,500 km away) (snippet) [G-I15]. Operators or controllers per truck, or net posts: not found. Rio says the roles shift (central controller, pit controller) rather than vanish (company statement, snippet) [G-I15].
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** the Iron Mine, Large Open-Pit Mine, Copper Mine & Mill, Large Copper Mine and Surface Coal Mine (truck driver posts).
- **Notes:** Rio's primary release failed to open (page not found), so Rio's 2016-17 figures are snippet and the 2015 figures come from a trade report of Rio's statement. Komatsu's 15% and Rio's 13-15% agree in size.

### 5. Calcined-clay cement (LC3)
- **First commercial:** disputed by definition. (a) Industrial trial at the Siguaney cement plant, Cuba, 2013: clay calcined March 2013, cement ground August 2013, about 130 t of cement from about 110-130 t of clay (snippet for the months [G-I22]; the trial itself and its 130 t of clay fired near 800 C confirmed on the abstract page [G-I18]). Builders: Cuban cement industry with UCLV (Martirena) and EPFL (Scrivener). (b) Cementos Argos, Rio Claro, Colombia: a 0.45 Mt/yr calcined-clay line completed, reported week of 19 Feb 2020 and called the first major cement plant to install such a line (page) [G-I19]. (c) The LC3 project's own press release of 30 Oct 2015 says Cuba "will be" the first country producing LC3 at commercial scale (page) [G-I21], so the 2013 batch was a trial, not routine production. Best supported for "first commercial-scale plant": Argos Rio Claro, Feb 2020; "first industrial production run": Siguaney, 2013. First LC3 as a retail product in India (JK Cement, Aug 2025) and Ghana (2026) are after the window (snippet) [G-I22].
- **Effect:** clinker share of the Siguaney trial cement: clinker 50%, gypsum 8.9%, calcined clay 27.4%, limestone 13.7%; strength as good as plain Portland and met Cuban standards (page) [G-I18]. Clinker share of ordinary Portland (the before figure): not found on a page I opened. CO2: Argos Rio Claro blends expected 38% below ordinary Portland; energy use stated as cut by 30% (trade-press reports of company statements; the baseline is not given) (page) [G-I19]; the LC3 project: potential 20-30% CO2 cut (page) [G-I21]. Ghana (2026, outside the window): LC3-35 emits 310 kg CO2/t against a 780 kg/t Portland reference (about 40% less), clay calcining about 80 kg CO2/t, clay line energy cost 50% of a clinker line's (vendor, page) [G-I20]; GS PAS 5:2024 permits as little as 35% clinker (page) [G-I20]. Calcination temperature about 800 C (page) [G-I18][G-I19]. Kiln fuel (MJ or kWh per tonne) against clinker burning: not found.
- **Cost (not an effect):** Argos invested over USD 78 million for the 0.45 Mt/yr line (snippet) [G-I22]; plants can make LC3 on existing lines with minor capital (page) [G-I21].
- **Game:** the Cement Plant.
- **Notes:** the earlier gathering's "35% clinker, 310 vs 780 kg" is confirmed as Ghana's LC3-35 product (2026), a 35% floor in a standard, not the product mix of the 2013 or 2020 plants. Clinker 50% is the first-generation LC3-50 blend.

### 6. Cross-laminated timber and mass-timber buildings
- **First commercial:** not pinned to a year. Graz University of Technology research on CLT began 1990 and Schickhofer's thesis is 1994 (snippet) [G-I30]; Austrian and EU approval December 1998 (page; the page's own source is a secondary one) [G-I24]; the producer KLH developed it with TU Graz "in 1996" (page; wording does not say production began) [G-I23]; the article says Schickhofer worked with three small sawmills and the sawmillers' association, first named plant not given (page) [G-I24]. Stora Enso's Bad St. Leonhard started CLT in 2008 but was not the first (snippet) [G-I30]. Best supported: Austria, mid-to-late 1990s, KLH among the first; not found: the first commercial plant and month.
- **First tall buildings:** Stadthaus, Murray Grove, London, completed 2009: 8-9 storeys (the page is inconsistent), 901 m3 of timber, four carpenters assembled the frame in 27 days, whole build 49 weeks, about five months faster than a notional concrete frame; the page flags several of these as "citation needed" (page, encyclopedia) [G-I29]. Brock Commons Tallwood House, UBC, Vancouver, 2017: 18 storeys (17 timber on a concrete podium); 54 m (builder's association) or 53 m (page) [G-I26][G-I28]. Mjøstårnet, Brumunddal, Norway, 2019, 85.4 m; overtaken by Ascent, Milwaukee, 2022, 86.6 m (snippet) [G-I30].
- **Effect:** Brock Commons: the whole mass-timber superstructure assembled in 70 days by nine wood installers, two months faster than planned (industry association, page) [G-I26]; the wood structure complete less than 70 days after the prefabricated parts arrived on site, about four months faster than a typical project of this size (page) [G-I27]; Wikipedia gives 57 days and a nine-person crew, citing a newspaper, which disagrees with the other two (page) [G-I28]. One floor of prefabricated envelope panels a day (UBC Living Labs, page: no figure for the timber frame itself) [G-I62]. Weight, tonnes of concrete or steel avoided and CO2: not found as measured figures.
- **Code:** the 2021 International Building Code (approved by the ICC, reported 3 Jan 2019): Type IV-A up to 18 storeys with gypsum on all mass timber, IV-B 12 storeys, IV-C 9 storeys (trade press, not the code text) (page) [G-I25].
- **Cost (not an effect):** Brock Commons total cost USD 51.5 million (Architect magazine via Wikipedia) (page) [G-I28]; whole-building cost against concrete: not found.
- **Game:** construction: the Builders' Yard, the Construction Materials Plant and the Panel Mill.
- **Notes:** the two Brock Commons durations (70 days with site start counted from first delivery; "two months faster" against "four months faster") are different baselines; use the 70 days and nine crew, which two pages agree on in substance.

### 7. Endless strip production (Arvedi ESP)
- **First commercial:** Cremona, Italy, Arvedi; industrial endless-mode production began June 2009 (page) [G-I32]; "earlier this summer" in an article dated 13 Aug 2009 (page) [G-I31]; construction completed autumn 2008, start-up in the first half of 2009 (snippet) [G-I34]. Equipment: Siemens VAI (now Primetals). Month: June 2009.
- **Effect:** online measurements showed energy consumption 45% lower than conventional casting and rolling (page) [G-I31][G-I32]; Primetals repeats the 45% (vendor, page) [G-I33] and its ESP page claims 50% (vendor, snippet) [G-I34]. The Arvedi technical paper had projected 50-70% lower depending on the product (a projection, snippet) [G-I34]. Line length 190 m, under half that of a conventional hot-strip mill without casting and storage (page) [G-I31]; strip as thin as 0.8 mm, widths to 1,600 mm, capacity above 2 million t of hot coil a year (page) [G-I32]; no head-and-tail cropping, so yield is higher (no number) (page) [G-I31][G-I32]. Absolute energy a tonne (GJ, MBtu, kWh), and the saving against a thin-slab caster with a reheating furnace specifically: not found. The 45% is against the conventional route, not against thin-slab.
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** the Steel Mini-Mill.
- **Notes:** the game's thin-slab step (reheating from 2.0 to 0.5-0.8 MBtu a tonne) cannot be cross-checked here; Primetals says ESP needs no long gas-fired tunnel furnace and uses a 10 m induction heater (snippet) [G-I34]. All 45% figures are from the builder or trade press quoting it.

### 8. Automated container terminals and the large container ship
- **First commercial (terminal):** CTA, Hamburg-Altenwerder: HHLA says developed in 2002 as a greenfield project (page) [G-I35]; first operation June 2002 and official opening 25 Oct 2002 are both given by different sources (snippet) [G-I37]. Driverless AGVs between quay cranes and 26 automated storage blocks, each with two rail-mounted stacking cranes (page) [G-I35]; about 100 AGVs now (page) [G-I36]. ECT Delta, Rotterdam, 1993 had 58 AGVs (page) [G-I36]: before the window. First terminal with remote-controlled quay cranes plus automated yard and AGVs in Asia: Qingdao Qianwan, first deep-sea call 11 May 2017 (page) [G-I38][G-I39].
- **Effect:** CTA: moves an hour and staff a TEU: not found. Qingdao: average 33 moves an hour per crane against 21 at a manual terminal (Navis case study, vendor), crane productivity 20% above manual terminals, overall 30%; for the first two berths 9 remote operators do the work of 60-80; labour cost 70% lower than at manual terminals (all vendor-linked claims, page) [G-I38]. First call, 11 May 2017: 26.1 moves an hour across seven cranes (page) [G-I39]. ITF/OECD (2021): 53 terminals automated to some degree, about 4% of global capacity, and productivity is not guaranteed by automation (summary of the report, snippet) [G-I40].
- **Large ship:** Emma Maersk in service 31 Aug 2006 (page, encyclopedia) [G-I41]; delivered 1 Sep 2006 per another report (snippet) [G-I42]. Length 397 m; main engine about 80-81 MW; 14,000 L of heavy fuel an hour at full output (page) [G-I41]. Capacity: Maersk's brochure 14,770 TEU, the shipyard's release about 11,000 TEU "depending on definition", independent estimates 13,500-15,200 TEU (page, secondary) [G-I41]: "15,000 TEU" is an estimate, not the official size. Fuel a container: not found.
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** the Container Terminal.
- **Notes:** Qingdao figures are 2017-2018 and vendor-linked; no regulator or peer-reviewed measurement of moves an hour was found.

### 9. Battery-electric city bus at fleet scale
- **First commercial:** BYD supplied 200 K9 pure-electric buses (lithium iron phosphate, range 250 km) for the 2011 Shenzhen Universiade, report dated 3 Aug 2011 (page) [G-I44]; Shenzhen Bus Group says trials started 2010 and its first electric bus fleet ran at the 2011 Universiade (snippet) [G-I45]. Whole fleet electric: 16,359 buses by end 2017 (page) [G-I43]. Shenzhen was a national pilot city for new-energy vehicles from 2009 (page) [G-I43].
- **Effect:** Shenzhen 2016 operating data: electric buses use 106.3 kWh per 100 km (1.063 kWh/km, calc) and 72.9% less energy than diesel buses (page) [G-I43]. CO2 a year: 67.02 kg per 100 km against 129.91 kg, about 48% less (snippet) [G-I45]. Diesel bus fuel a km in Shenzhen: not found; a national study cited by ITDP uses 38 L per 100 km for a 12 m diesel bus (page) [G-I43]. BYD: K9 energy cost under a third of a diesel bus's fuel cost (builder claim, page) [G-I44]. City totals: 345,000 tons of fuel and 1.35 million tons of CO2 saved a year (Shenzhen Daily via Green Car Reports, snippet) [G-I45].
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** the Bus Network.
- **Notes:** average daily mileage of an electric bus is 174.4 km against 228.8 km for a diesel bus (snippet) [G-I45]: fewer km a day, so more buses a route. The 72.9% is a government-supplied figure on 2016 data.

### 10. Battery-electric long-haul truck
- **First commercial:** Mercedes-Benz eActros 600: series launch at the Wörth am Rhein plant "at the end of 2024" (builder, page) [G-I46]; a trade report dated 10 Dec 2024 says series production began in Nov 2024 and the first trucks went to German customers by then, but names Mannheim (disagrees with the builder) (page) [G-I48]. Tesla Semi: first deliveries to PepsiCo began early Dec 2022; about 36 of the 100 ordered were estimated delivered by mid-2023 (page) [G-I50]; volume production announced for 2026 (page) [G-I49]. So: pilot deliveries Dec 2022 (Tesla), first series production end 2024 (Mercedes).
- **Effect:** eActros 600: battery 621 kWh (3 x 207 kWh); range 500 km at 40 t gross, 20 C (internal measurement) (page) [G-I46]. Measured: 103 kWh per 100 km (1.03 kWh/km, calc) over 15,269 km in 45 days and 22 countries at 40 t gross, daily 85 to 140 kWh per 100 km, recuperation about 25% of drive energy (builder's test tour, page) [G-I47]. Customer routes: 100 (Seifert, Wörth-Bielefeld), 92 (FERCAM, South Tyrol) kWh per 100 km (page) [G-I46]. Tesla Semi at PepsiCo, Aug 2023: average 1.7 kWh/mile (about 1.1 kWh/km as the page states; 1.06 calc), 21 trucks at the Sacramento plant, 18 doing multi-stop work within 100 miles (page) [G-I50]; Tesla's final spec 1.7 kWh/mile, 325 mile and 500 mile trims (spec, not measured) (page) [G-I49]. Diesel before: a typical 2015 European 40 t tractor-trailer uses 33.1 L per 100 km on the long-haul cycle; Euro VI typical 32.6 (ICCT) (snippet) [G-I51]. Hours of driving a day, payload loss: not found.
- **Cost (not an effect):** Tesla Semi price figures conflict between sources; not used.
- **Game:** the Truck Depot.
- **Notes:** the earlier "1.06 kWh/km" matches the Tesla Semi at PepsiCo; Mercedes's measured figure is 1.03 kWh/km. The two are within 3%.

### 11. Robotaxi
- **First commercial:** Waymo, Phoenix area, 8 Oct 2020: fully driverless rides opened to the public, starting with existing Waymo One riders, sign-ups to follow (page) [G-I53]. Earlier in 2020, 5-10% of rides were driverless for early riders under NDA (page) [G-I53]. Waymo's own post failed to open (snippet) [G-I54].
- **Effect:** Waymo, through June 2026: 271.3 million rider-only miles; any injury reported 0.67 against a human benchmark of 3.77 per million miles (-82.12%); serious injury or worse 0.01 against 0.21 (-94.82%); airbag deployment 0.29 against 1.61 (-81.68%) (operator's own data, page) [G-I52]. In 2023, over 7 million rider-only miles: injury-causing crashes -85% (snippet) [G-I54].
- **Cost (not an effect):** not found.
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no taxis).
- **Notes:** operator-published; the benchmark is an average human driver in the same cities, and human crash data under-report (snippet) [G-I54].

### 12. Warehouse robots and drone delivery
- **First commercial (robots):** Kiva Systems founded 2003 as Distrobot, renamed 2005 (page, encyclopedia) [G-I55]; the earliest dated installation named on that page is Walgreens, Mount Vernon, Illinois, 2007 [G-I55]. The "first deployment 2004" in the brief: not found. Amazon announced the Kiva purchase 19 Mar 2012 for about USD 775 million (page) [G-I55].
- **Effect (robots):** Amazon said Kiva-type robots let it store 40% more material in the same space and reportedly cut costs by up to 20% (page) [G-I56]. Pick rate: an analyst's estimate of 600 lines an hour with Kiva against a 160-an-hour manual labour goal (an estimate, not a measurement) (snippet) [G-I57]; Kiva's own claim of 2-4 times the productivity of other picking methods (vendor, snippet) [G-I57]. Staff a unit of output: not found. Amazon had more than 200,000 robots by June 2019 (page) [G-I55].
- **First commercial (drones):** Zipline, Muhanga, Rwanda, launched 14 Oct 2016 (page, article of that date) [G-I58]: blood to 21 clinics in the western half of the country. Wing, Canberra, Australia, April 2019 (snippet) [G-I61]: a first in Australia, not the first anywhere.
- **Effect (drones):** Zipline Rwanda: 50 to 150 deliveries a day at launch; blood arrives about 30 minutes after a text request (page) [G-I58]. A 2022 Lancet Global Health study of 20 facilities, 2015-2019: expired blood units -67% at 12 months (snippet) [G-I60]; median drone delivery 41 minutes or under against two hours or more by road (snippet) [G-I60]; a "-61% delivery time" figure on the Wikipedia page for Zipline (page) [G-I59] was not confirmed by the Lancet summary.
- **Cost (not an effect):** Kiva cost USD 1-2 million for a starter installation and USD 15-20 million for 1,000 robots (CNN 2012 via Wikipedia) (page) [G-I55].
- **Game:** nothing yet (no online shop's Fulfilment Centre).
- **Notes:** Kiva's original deployment and pick-rate measurements are not on any page I opened; Amazon's figures are company statements.
