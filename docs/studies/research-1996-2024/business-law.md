# Gathered: business and law, 1996-2024

*Gathered 2026-11-16 for R6-G by a Sonnet agent; marks: page = seen on the opened page, snippet = search summary only. Kept as gathered for [the study](../research-1996-2024.md), which chooses and writes; its section 9 lists the keys it cites.*

Effects below are measured before/after figures unless the line says "model" or "estimate". A figure with no mark is not on this sheet. "Not found" means two tries or more.

### 1. AI assistants at work (generative AI for customer-support agents)
- **Date:** First commercial LLM chat assistant for the public: ChatGPT, 30 Nov 2022 (snippet) [G-B5]. The studied firm's tool was older: a GPT-based assistant rolled out after a seven-week randomised pilot of 50 agents, the bulk of adoption between Nov 2020 and Feb 2021 (page) [G-B2]. Canada: no Canadian date sought (not a law).
- **Effect:** Issues resolved per hour +14% on average, +34% for novice and low-skilled agents (NBER abstract, page) [G-B1]; 5,179 agents there. The later arXiv version (revised 6 Nov 2024) says about 15% and 5,172 agents (page) [G-B3]; the QJE listing also says 5,172 (snippet) [G-B4]. Inside the paper: +0.30 chats an hour = +13.8% with agent and tenure fixed effects; +0.37 on a baseline of 2.6 chats an hour is about +14%; average chat time -3.8 minutes (-9%); agents under one month of tenure +46% (all page) [G-B2]. About 3 million chats in the data (page) [G-B2].
- **Game:** Contact Centre (output per agent; fits a "tool raises output of less-skilled staff more" effect).
- **Notes:** 14% (working paper, NBER) against 15% (final version); 5,179 against 5,172 agents. One Fortune 500 business-software firm, not a random sample of firms (page) [G-B2]. The study's deployment (2020-21) came BEFORE ChatGPT; the "commercial LLM assistant" date and the study's date differ by two years.

### 2. The smartphone and mobile banking (iPhone 2007)
- **Date:** iPhone announced 9 Jan 2007; Apple's release said US sales in June 2007 (page) [G-B6]. The exact 29 June day was not seen. First mobile banking app: not found.
- **Effect:** Canada, bank branches: 6,205 (2012) to 5,656 (2022), -9%; per 100,000 adults 24.3 (2012) to 20.7 (2021), -15% (page) [G-B7]. US BLS: the tellers page says branches "have been in decline due to technological change"; tellers 339,200 (2025), projected 294,500 (2035), -13% (page; the 2035 figure is a projection, not an effect) [G-B8]. A matched-county paper: where banks had mobile apps, small-business lending fell about 18.8% less after branch closures (snippet) [G-B9]. A tellers count for 2007 was not found.
- **Game:** Commercial Bank (branch count or staff per deposit; a research could cut the staff a branch needs).
- **Notes:** No figure isolates the phone from ATMs, online banking and the 2008 crisis. The Canadian branch series starts at 2012, so it does not show the 2007-2012 change. "Not found" for a clean before/after.

### 3. Rented computing (cloud)
- **Date:** Amazon S3 launched 14 Mar 2006, one-paragraph announcement (page) [G-B10]. EC2 first beta instance 25 Aug 2006 (snippet) [G-B11].
- **Effect:** UK firm data: adopting cloud goes with IT investment per employee 49% lower over the sample (page; the paper calls it a correlation; -50% for incumbents, -36% for young firms) [G-B12]. Cloud leads to growth of employment and revenue in young firms (page, no figure in the abstract) [G-B12]. IT staff: not found. A survey: 14% of companies cut IT headcount after a cloud strategy (snippet, survey, not a before/after on payrolls) [G-B13].
- **Game:** Shared Services Centre (cost of running back-office IT; fixed cost moves to a per-use cost).
- **Notes:** A German study finds no effect of cloud on ICT investment (snippet) [G-B13]; the two papers disagree.

### 4. Precision scheduled railroading
- **Date:** Harrison ran Illinois Central from 1989 (snippet) [G-B17]; CN chief operating officer March 1998 to Dec 2002, CEO from 2003 (snippet) [G-B16]. CN's own 2003 Fact Book names "precision railroading" in Harrison's letter (page) [G-B15]. The "CN from 1999" date was not confirmed; 1998 is the date in the sources.
- **Effect:** Illinois Central operating ratio 67.7% (1994) to 63.8% (1998); average employees 3,250 (1994) to 3,192 (1998); revenue ton-miles 20,582 million to 23,359 million (page, SEC filing) [G-B14]. The 1989 ratio "high 90s" and 62.3% at the 1998 acquisition (snippet) [G-B17]. CN adjusted operating ratio 75.1% (1998) to 69.4% (2002); average employees 24,993 to 23,190; revenue ton-miles 138,669 million to 159,876 million (page) [G-B15]. My arithmetic from those: about 180 employees per billion revenue ton-miles in 1998, 145 in 2002.
- **Game:** Freight Line, Rail Terminal (cost per ton-mile and staff per train).
- **Notes:** CN's 1998 ratio differs by source: 75.1 (page), 78.6, 89 (snippets) [G-B16]; the page figure is "adjusted" (excludes special items). Illinois Central filings only show 1994-1998.

### 5. Decimal share prices
- **Date:** Toronto Stock Exchange 15 Apr 1996, minimum tick cut to five cents (page) [G-B18]. First anywhere: not checked. NYSE all stocks 29 Jan 2001 (snippet) [G-B23]; Nasdaq completed 9 Apr 2001 (page) [G-B22].
- **Effect:** TSE: quoted spreads -17% to -27%, quoted depth -27% to -52%, no significant rise in volume (page, a review of four studies) [G-B18]. Another TSE study: spreads narrowed and depth fell, more for low-priced high-volume stocks (page, no figures) [G-B19]. TSE stocks cross-listed in the US: effective spreads -16% to -27% on the TSE; about -8% for Nasdaq-traded ones (snippet) [G-B20]. US, SEC staff, 8 Jun 2001: quoted spreads NYSE -37%, Nasdaq -50%; effective spreads NYSE -15%, Nasdaq about -50%; quote sizes NYSE -60%, Nasdaq -68% (preliminary) (page) [G-B21]. SEC acting chairman, 18 Jul 2001: effective spread NYSE about -28% (SEC economists), Nasdaq about -50% (Nasdaq's own report) (page) [G-B22].
- **Game:** the exchange's order book (the price step and the book's depth; a finer step narrows the spread, thins what sits at each price).
- **Notes:** NYSE effective spread is -15% in the June speech and -28% in the July speech (different samples). The Bacidore 1997 paper (the main TSE study) was not opened; its figures were not found.

### 6. The mortgage stress test
- **Date:** Canada, insured: high-ratio mortgages 17 Oct 2016, low-ratio insured 30 Nov 2016 (page) [G-B25]. Uninsured, OSFI B-20: letter 17 Oct 2017, in force 1 Jan 2018 (page) [G-B24]. First anywhere: not checked.
- **Terms:** Insured: qualify at the greater of the contract rate or the Bank of Canada posted five-year rate (4.64% on 28 Sep 2016); gross debt service at most 39%, total debt service at most 44% (page) [G-B25]. Uninsured, 2018: the greater of the contract rate plus 2 points or the Bank of Canada five-year benchmark rate (page) [G-B24]. Since 1 Jun 2021 the floor is a fixed 5.25% instead of the benchmark (snippet) [G-B28]. Renewals with the same lender were not re-tested (snippet) [G-B28].
- **Effect:** Bank of Canada staff: low-ratio mortgages with loan-to-income above 450% fell from 20% to 14% between Q2 2017 and Q2 2018 (page; "a year earlier" to Q2 2018) [G-B26]. Bank of Canada staff 2024: about 29% of low-ratio mortgages issued in 2017 would have failed a 44% debt-service test under the new rule; of those issued after Jan 2018 about 13% had such a ratio; the rule also slowed mortgage counts, mortgage size and local house price growth (page) [G-B27]. OSFI, Oct 2024: the test did not stop mortgages above 450% of income from building up; a cap of 15% of new loans followed on 31 Jan 2025 (snippet) [G-B28].
- **Game:** the planned credit levers (a qualifying-rate stress on new mortgages; the figures give the size of the cut: the share of loans above 450% of income fell 6 points in a year).
- **Notes:** The 14% and 13% figures come from different notes with different measures (loan-to-income against debt service). OSFI's Oct 2024 memo says the test did not stop a build-up of such loans, so the first-year fall did not last (snippet) [G-B28].

### 7. Basel III capital rules
- **Date:** Basel Committee: published Dec 2010, revised June 2011 (page) [G-B30]. Canada: OSFI's final capital guideline in force 1 Jan 2013 (page) [G-B33].
- **Terms:** Common equity minimum 4.5% of risk-weighted assets, capital conservation buffer 2.5%, so 7% in all (page) [G-B29]. The Tier 1 and total-capital minimums and OSFI's own targets were not on an opened page. Canada also keeps a limit of total assets at about 20 times capital (snippet) [G-B33].
- **Effect:** Large international banks (Group 1): average common equity ratio 7.7% at 31 Dec 2011 on Basel III definitions (page) [G-B31]; 13.1% at 31 Dec 2023 (page) [G-B32]. Canada: the six largest banks' aggregate common equity ratio 9.7% (Q1 2015) to 13.7% (Q4 2025) (page) [G-B34]. A pre-2013 Canadian figure was not found.
- **Game:** the Commercial Bank (a minimum capital-to-loans ratio; a bank under it cannot lend).
- **Notes:** The two samples (BCBS, OSFI) and the definitions differ; the rise is not all Basel III (supervisors also added a domestic buffer later (page) [G-B34]).

### 8. Quantitative easing
- **Date:** Bank of Japan 19 Mar 2001 (page) [G-B39]; Federal Reserve 25 Nov 2008, up to $100 bn of agency debt and $500 bn of mortgage-backed securities (page) [G-B35]; Bank of England 5 Mar 2009, GBP 75 bn (page) [G-B37]; Bank of Canada first announced bond purchases 27 Mar 2020 (page) [G-B40].
- **Effect:** US: the ten-year Treasury yield fell 91 basis points summed over eight announcement days; mainly the term premium, with the ten-year term premium down 50 to 100 basis points (page) [G-B36]. UK: gilt yields about 100 basis points lower than without QE (page; an estimate from announcement windows plus econometrics) [G-B38]. Canada: the 27 Mar 2020 announcement cut Government of Canada yields by about 10 to 15 basis points (page; one-hour event study) [G-B40]; a later event study over many announcements gave about 20 basis points on the ten-year (page, as cited) [G-B41]. Japan: studies reviewed by the San Francisco Fed: a 10 trillion yen rise in the reserve target went with about 19 basis points lower three-year and 17 lower five-year yields; no significant effect of bond buying on medium and long yields in one study (page) [G-B39].
- **Game:** the central bank and the bond auction (a purchase lowers the term yield; the effect on the long rate is a percent of the stock bought).
- **Notes:** Model-based totals exist (Canada's peak ten-year effect of at least 84 basis points, page [G-B41]) but they are a model, not a measurement, and are left out of the effects. The 91 basis point sum is a small-sample event study that later work disputes (snippet) [G-B36]. Sizes of the programmes were not gathered beyond the announcements.

### 9. Negative policy rates
- **Date and level:** Danmarks Nationalbank: certificates-of-deposit rate -0.20% in early July 2012 (page) [G-B42]; 6 July 2012 as the day it took effect (snippet) [G-B42]; -0.75% by early Feb 2015, then -0.65% in early 2016 (page) [G-B43]. ECB deposit rate -0.10% on 11 Jun 2014, -0.20% Sep 2014, -0.30% Dec 2015 (page) [G-B43]. Swiss National Bank: -0.25% announced 18 Dec 2014 (effective 22 Jan 2015), -0.75% announced 15 Jan 2015 (page) [G-B43]. Bank of Japan: -0.10% on part of current-account balances, announced 29 Jan 2016 (page) [G-B43]. Canada: 27 Mar 2020 the rate went to 0.25%, which the Bank called its effective lower bound (page) [G-B45]; the Bank did not go negative, and in Dec 2020 said it could consider a lower but still positive rate (snippet) [G-B74].
- **Effect:** Money market rates followed the policy rate below zero in all four places; retail deposit rates stayed largely insulated, banks reluctant to charge households (page, no figures) [G-B43]. Sweden: virtually full pass-through to money market rates; deposit rates kept responding until the policy rate reached -0.25%; mortgage rate pass-through about 80% within 30 days in normal times, but slightly negative (a small rise in lending rates) once the policy rate was well below zero (page) [G-B44].
- **Game:** the policy rate's zero floor (the floor reflects that households would hold cash instead; the measured deposit floor near zero agrees).
- **Notes:** The Swedish figures are one country; the Danish rate is tied to its currency peg (snippet) [G-B42]. Denmark's first negative spell (2012) ended in April 2014; the rate went to -0.05% on 5 Sep 2014 and on to -0.75% by early Feb 2015 (page) [G-B43].

### 10. Instant payments
- **Date:** UK Faster Payments 27 May 2008 (page; encyclopedia) [G-B46]; Brazil's Pix in full operation 16 Nov 2020 (page; restricted start 3 Nov 2020 per the central bank's rule, snippet) [G-B48].
- **Volume:** UK 967.6 million payments worth GBP 771.4 bn in 2013 (page) [G-B46]; 4.5 bn in 2023 and 5.1 bn in 2024 (page) [G-B47]. Pix: record of over 227 million payments in a day, 6 Sep 2024; 168.15 million users by end of Aug 2024 (page) [G-B49].
- **Effect:** Brazil, before Pix: cash was 77% of financial transactions in 2019 (page) [G-B48]. Cost to a payee: Pix 0.33% a payment, debit card 1.13%, credit card 2.34% (page) [G-B48]. ATM and bank withdrawals down 25% since 2020, with Pix, the pandemic and card growth all named (snippet; a trend, not a causal figure) [G-B50]. A clean before/after on cash or card use: not found.
- **Game:** probably nothing.
- **Notes:** No UK study of cash use was gathered.

### 11. The congestion charge
- **Date:** London 17 Feb 2003 (snippet: TfL monitoring reports) [G-B52]. Stockholm trial 3 Jan to 31 Jul 2006, permanent from 1 Aug 2007 (snippet) [G-B54]. Canada: no road charge of this kind (not searched).
- **Effect:** London, first year: traffic entering the zone in charging hours -18%, cars -30% (snippet) [G-B52]. Five years on: traffic into the original zone still 21% below pre-charge, about 70,000 fewer cars a day; the western extension -14% (about 30,000); but congestion back to pre-charge levels because roadworks cut road space (page) [G-B51]. Stockholm: August 2007 passages across the cordon 21% below August 2005; 2008-09 on average 18% below 2005; 2010 -19%, 2011 -20%; adjusted for fuel prices, jobs and other factors: -21.4% (2006) and -22.3% (2011) (page) [G-B53]. Habit effect: in the year the charge was off (Aug 2006 to Aug 2007) traffic stayed 5-10% below 2005 (page) [G-B53].
- **Game:** roads and the Bus Network (a charge on road use; the effect: fewer car trips, mostly not a fall in total congestion).
- **Notes:** London's congestion came back (page) [G-B51]; Stockholm's reduction held. Baselines differ (2002 for London, 2005 for Stockholm).

### 12. Upzoning
- **Date:** Auckland Unitary Plan operative 15 Nov 2016 (page) [G-B55]. About three quarters of residential land upzoned (page) [G-B55]. Canada: not gathered.
- **Effect:** 21,808 additional dwellings consented over the five years after the reform, about 4.11% of Auckland's dwelling stock (page; an estimate from a difference-in-differences against an extrapolated trend, not a count) [G-B55]. Rents, six years after: three-bedroom dwellings 22% to 35% below a synthetic control; two-bedroom 14% to 22% below, significant only at 10% in some specifications (page; May 2023 working paper) [G-B56].
- **Game:** zoning (how much housing a lot may carry; more permitted density means more homes built, lower rent than otherwise).
- **Notes:** A secondary source quotes 26-33% lower for two-bedroom rents, which does not match the May 2023 paper; it may come from the revised 2024 version (snippet) [G-B57]. A blog counts 26,903 homes (snippet) [G-B57], not the paper's 21,808. Critics dispute the paper (snippet) [G-B57].

### 13. Pension age tied to life expectancy
- **Date:** Denmark: the Welfare Agreement of 2006 and the Retirement Reform of 2011 set the indexation (page) [G-B59]. First rise in force: 2019, from 65 to 65.5 (page) [G-B58]. Canada: no such link found (not searched).
- **Ages set:** 65.5 in 2019, 66 in 2020, 68 in 2030, 69 in 2035, 70 in 2040 (page, 2021 table) [G-B59]. The rule: the age is set so that the average person spends 14.5 years on pension; changes are decided 15 years ahead; a rise is capped at 1 year every 5 years (page) [G-B58, G-B59]. A 2025 law sets 70 for people born after 1970 (page) [G-B60]; "70 from 2040" (snippet) [G-B60].
- **Game:** the old-age pension (retirement fixed at 70; a link would move the age with the model's life expectancy, and the Danish rule gives the formula). First anywhere: not checked.
- **Notes:** News summaries disagree on the early steps ("65 in 2004 to 67 in 2019"; "67 by 2022") (snippet) [G-B60]; the opened table shows 65.5 in 2019 and 66 in 2020, and the text gives 67 in 2029 (page) [G-B59]. The effect on older workers' employment was not looked up.

### 14. Workplace smoking ban
- **Date:** Ireland 29 Mar 2004 (snippet) [G-B65]. Canada: Manitoba 1 Oct 2004, Saskatchewan 1 Jan 2005 (public places), Alberta 1 Jan 2006, Ontario and Quebec 31 May 2006 (snippet) [G-B66]; Saskatoon's own bylaw 1 Jul 2004 (snippet) [G-B63].
- **Effect:** Ireland (Cork and Kerry heart-attack register): acute coronary admissions 205.9 to 177.9 per 100,000 in the first year, -12%; a further -13% to 149.2 in March 2006 to March 2007 (page) [G-B61]. Toronto: crude heart-attack admissions about -17% after the restaurant phase (snippet) [G-B64]. Saskatoon: heart-attack rate 165.4 to 148.2 per 100,000 (snippet; quoted by a critical blog, not the paper) [G-B63]. Pooled across studies: relative risk 0.848 for hospital admissions for coronary events (snippet) [G-B62]; a second pooling 0.88 (snippet, source not identified).
- **Game:** nothing in the game (no smoking, no heart disease).
- **Notes:** The Irish authors say 12%; the rates they give work out to about 13.6% (page). Canada's provinces came six months to two years after Ireland.

### 15. The Tax-Free Savings Account
- **Date:** Bill C-50 royal assent 18 Jun 2008; accounts opened 1 Jan 2009 (page) [G-B67]. First anywhere: not checked.
- **Rule:** Room $5,000 a year; $5,500 from 2013; $10,000 from 2015 (page) [G-B67]; back to $5,500 from 2016 (snippet) [G-B68]. Income and gains inside are tax-free and withdrawals are not taxed; a 100% tax on income from deliberate over-contributions from 17 Oct 2009 (page) [G-B67].
- **Effect (take-up):** Holders: 4,839,870 in 2009, cumulative 6,861,900 (2010), 8,366,100 (2011), 9,596,830 (2012), 10,714,620 (end 2013) (page) [G-B67]. Later: 13.5 million holders in 2016 and 17.8 million Canadians by end 2022 (snippet) [G-B68]. Share of holders who filled the room in 2019: 9% (snippet) [G-B68].
- **Game:** the planned tax on realised gains and dividends (an account type that is exempt up to a yearly room).
- **Notes:** Holders and accounts are different counts (4.8 million holders against 5.3 million accounts in 2009, snippet [G-B68]).

### 16. Real-time e-invoicing reported to the tax office; driverless vehicles allowed on public roads
- **Date:** Italy: e-invoicing mandatory for business-to-business and business-to-consumer from 1 Jan 2019, after public bodies in 2014-15 (snippet) [G-B71]. Germany: the Act on Autonomous Driving in force 28 Jul 2021, level-4 vehicles without a driver in defined areas, with a "technical supervisor" (page) [G-B72]; the ordinance followed 1 Jul 2022 (snippet) [G-B73].
- **Effect (e-invoicing):** Cross-border VAT fraud, from trade-data gaps: Italy's revenue loss lower by EUR 0.6 bn to 1 bn in 2019 (page, working paper, Jan 2023) [G-B69]; the published version gives EUR 2.2 bn to 2.6 bn against 2018 (snippet) [G-B70]. Italy's VAT gap fell about EUR 4.1 bn in 2019 and 4.5 bn in 2020; Rödl says the part due to e-invoicing cannot be isolated (snippet) [G-B70].
- **Game:** nothing in the game (the sales tax has no evasion; no driver in the model).
- **Notes:** The two Heinemann-Stiller figures differ by a factor of about 3; use the working-paper page figure or call the range 0.6 to 2.6 bn. No effect figure for driverless-vehicle law (dates only, as asked).
