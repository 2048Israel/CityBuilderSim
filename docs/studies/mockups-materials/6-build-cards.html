Mockup 6 — build cards: six of the new buildings

The game's one card for every building (0.7.25), row by row, as in the oil mockups: head, tags, hero, price, bar 1 (money), bar 2 (the scarce thing), the investors' line, needs, the order bar, the quote. The big city of mockups 1–5, same month; every figure is that city's.

Six states: a processing building the investors rank second (Blast Furnace), one that would lose money on imported feed (Copper Smelter), the player's own order against the investors' advice (Large Sawmill, pink border), and three investors' orders on site (Underground Coal Mine, Large Quarry, and a Harvesting Operation whose card says what it costs the forest).

Build › Industry — the processing sectors. Bar 2 is the feed the building needs: the city's own feed is what makes a processing building pay, because imported feed carries its freight.
  • The player can always overrule the investors. The Large Sawmill card says what the rule thinks (no spare logs) and what that means in money (26 years on imported logs), then builds it anyway if asked. That is today's behaviour for player orders.
  • The investors' line names the rival. When a building would be built but something else ranks first, the line says what and why, in the same terms as the sector page: what each would earn a month on its cost.
Build › Industry — the extraction sectors. Bar 2 is the ground: free sites in the right kind of field, or years of forest.
  • Bar 1 for a quarry is short because aggregate is imported. At the city's own price (D$ 40, the import price) a Large Quarry pays for itself in 10 months; at the world's D$ 10 it would take 5 years. The price rule does this on its own.
  • Forest has no sites, so bar 2 is where the forest settles. There is no limit on the cut (report Q14): a seventh crew pays, so the investors build it, and the card says what that costs, 17% of the natural woods left standing in the long run instead of 29%. Forestry plants to make up for it when the city has bare land (report Q15).
