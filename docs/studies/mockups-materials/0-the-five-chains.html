Every new and changed building, by chain and industry. Each chain has at most three industries: one that digs or cuts, one that processes (and decides for itself which of its buildings to build: whichever earns the most), and for iron and copper the existing Manufacturing. Industries that buy from another chain are not counted in it: Iron & Steel buys coal, but it is iron's industry, not coal's.

Figures per building are a month at nameplate, from the report's model (§2–§7). Costs are all-in game costs, priced like today's buildings (one to two and a half years of what they sell abroad).

The extraction rule (as today's Mining). A sector builds a mine on a free site of a field the city owns, choosing the mine type that fits the field's kind and the free sites (a Large Open-Pit Mine needs 20 in one field). Its product always sells, at the export price if nobody here wants it, so a mine is worth building before anything downstream exists. Forest has no sites and no limit: Forestry builds crews while cutting pays, and its page shows where the forest settles at that cut. Whenever its cut exceeds regrowth and the city has bare dry land, it plants a plantation as a cost of cutting, outside the interest test (report Q14, Q15).
The processing rule (the oil study's §2.3, for all five).
  1. Price every good at the city's own price: the import price if it imported any this month, the export price if it exported any, the cleared price otherwise. A new building's own output counts at the import price up to what the city imports, and at the export price beyond.
  2. What it earns is what it makes less what it uses, at those prices, less its running cost. Power it makes beyond its own needs sells to the grid at the tariff.
  3. Build the one that earns the most on its cost, if it passes land, staff and money (1.25× its interest), as today. Nothing else holds it back: freight in the price bands keeps a building that imports its feed to export its output under the interest test (report Q7).
  4. One order at a time per sector. A building whose margin stays below zero for 6 months idles, then sheds, by the existing distress rule.
