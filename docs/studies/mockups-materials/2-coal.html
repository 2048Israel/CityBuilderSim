Mockup 2 — coal: Coal Mining

Coal is one industry. Coal Mining owns the mines and the wash plant; there is no coal-refining sector, because what coal becomes is made by the sectors that burn it: coke in Iron & Steel's coke ovens, heat in the cement and lime kilns, and power in the city's coal power plants. A field is either thermal (sub-bituminous, 20 GJ a tonne, sold as it is dug) or coking (bituminous, 29 GJ, washed into coking coal and a thermal by-product).

Two states: a small town with one surface mine, and the big city of the other mockups. Ribbons are to scale in tonnes. The power plants are drawn as they will be once they burn coal, which is the electricity run, not this one (report Q10).

State A — a small town with one surface mine. 41,300 people, April 2061. The town owns one thermal field; its one mine ships every tonne abroad.
  • A mine is worth building with no buyer in the city: coal at the world's D$ 50 against a cost of D$ 10.70 a tonne. That is the existing rule for iron ore, applied to coal.
  • The power plant is the town's natural buyer, but at today's tariff (D$ 10 a kW a month, about D$ 0.014 a kWh) its coal alone would cost twice what its power sells for. That is why burning coal belongs to the electricity run (report Q10).
State B — the big city. 2.61M people, June 2183: two underground longwall mines on a coking field feed one preparation plant; a large strip mine and two small ones work a thermal field.
  • Washing is where coking coal is made. Unwashed, coking-field coal sells only as thermal coal (D$ 50); washed, 55% of it becomes coking coal at D$ 180 and 20% thermal by-product. So the sector builds a preparation plant as soon as a coking mine stands. The pair returns about 2.4% a month.
  • The buyers are the other industries. Iron & Steel takes coking coal for its coke ovens and thermal coal for the blast furnace and the pelletising plant; Cement & Concrete burns it in its kilns. Each is priced at the city's own price, so coal mined here makes their spreads wider.
