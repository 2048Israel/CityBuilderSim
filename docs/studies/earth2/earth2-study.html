Earth 2 Study: slope, snow, shallow sea and bridges on the real Earth

Five boards for the Earth 2 study: the map at each zoom it draws, the legend and the hover words, the land office and the founding card, a sector's land line, and Build's bridges. Every map here is drawn from earth-2.bin itself: its 30" slope shares, its 15" shallow-sea share and landmass ids, and earth-1's sea, lakes, rivers and land cover inside it, by figures.py's rule with seeded detail at 30 m plots (mockups.py beside this page). The screens' wording follows the code (EarthSite, FoundingScreen, LandScreen, LandMap, SectorStatements, StatementView, BuildScreen); the new lines are written in the same voice and marked.

Board 1 · the map

The ground at every zoom the map draws

Land gets a second plane beside its cover: flat (under 10% grade) keeps its cover's colour; foothill (10-30%, buildable, dearer to prepare), steep (30-60%) and very steep (60% and more) are three greys, lightest to darkest. Snow and ice is earth-1's ICE, white, and wins over slope. The sea gets a second blue: shallow sea, 30 m or less (GEBCO), lighter than the deep. ★ D5

Forest on a slope: drawn grey (A), or forest shaded toward its slope's grey (B)

Medellín's valley, 18 km square round its centre, a pixel a 30 m plot, drawn as if the city owned it all (the map dims ground it does not own 40% toward its background, as today). Measured on this render: 25% flat, 50% foothill, 24% steep, 1% very steep of the land; forest covers 46% of it, and 37% of the land is forest on a slope. The white box is the close-up below.

A · greys over everything

One colour a class, nothing mixed; the slope reads at a glance. It hides the forest on every hillside, which the forest law and Forestry count, and makes wooded hills (most of them) look like bare rock.

B · forest kept, shaded by its slope (recommended)

Three more colours, one per slope class on forest, each a fixed mix of the two (a dither of the two at plot level would read the same at L0 and average to the mix farther out). The forest and the slope both read, and a forested foothill is visibly buildable ground and visibly forest.

Finding · the 14-plot cap does not fit Halifax

Measured on earth-2: along the bridges' own lines the 15" sea share passes a half for 820 m (Macdonald) and 805 m (MacKay). With the seed's fray a run meets 555-1,000 m under the Macdonald and 295-915 m under the MacKay over eight seeds; seven seeds in eight leave the MacKay over 14 plots, and all eight the Macdonald.

Halifax and Dartmouth are one landmass (id 2, joined round the head of Bedford Basin), so the long-bridge order refuses the crossing as well. On most seeds neither of Halifax's bridges could be built. The study's D12 ("Halifax's two bridges are this kind", §6.2) rests on the BIO's "300 to 400 metres wide", which is the sill's width under water, not shore to shore.

The study has to widen one rule: a short crossing of about 34 plots (1 km) for highway and rail est., or the long order allowed within one landmass where the water is wider than a run's bridge.

Depths along the lines (GEBCO 2026, 15"): Macdonald 6-21 m, MacKay 7-14 m; the depth layer's 1' cells 4-11 m and 13-19 m. Bedford Basin's middle cell 51 m. Every sea plot within 5 km of downtown is shallow on this render once the shallow share is weighted by the sea share (read plain, the land cells' 0 drew a fringe of deep sea along every shore).

Greys and snow

The Khumbu: Namche Bazaar to Everest

Slope, not height, decides: Medellín at 1,500 m is mostly buildable, and so is the Tibetan plateau at 4,500 m (the globe tier below); the Himalaya's flanks are not. The few pale flats here are real valley floors at 4,300-4,900 m.

Two things look odd here, both earth-1's land cover, not earth-2's slope: the ice bit covers 77% of a 20 km box round the summit, bare rock faces included; and a few tan patches are the desert bit (bare ground on half a cell's land) on moraine. Both draw as they do in the game today.

The noise here is box-relative (figures.py's): another crop of the same place draws the same shares with different detail. The game's noise is the world's, so a place draws the same in every view.

Long crossings

The Confederation Bridge, as one order on Build

Measured on the bridge's line (Borden-Carleton to Cape Jourimain): 12.8 km of water by earth-1's 15" sea; every water cell 15 of 15 shallow; GEBCO 1-24 m, the depth layer's 1' cells 1-21 m. The two shores are landmass 103, Prince Edward Island, 5,596.50 km² in the landmasses table (Charlottetown its largest place), and landmass 2, the Americas. Real: 12.9 km, 44 main piers 250 m apart, built 1993-97 for C$1.3 billion.

The order is judged whole before a plot is laid: a continuous way of shallow sea or fresh water from the city's dry ground on one shore to its dry ground on the other, no cell of it deep, no longer than 36 km est., the two ends on different landmasses. Ground on an unlinked landmass holds only a port or a bridge's landing until then.

The far view below shows the same strait with the crossing the order refuses: Caribou to Wood Islands meets water deeper than 30 m 11.1 km out.

How the boards were made. docs/studies/earth2/mockups.py draws every map from earth-2.bin (136,924,697 bytes, SHA-256 b48adce1...ae9a) through tools/earth/earthread.py, with figures.py's noise and rule; run it with python3 -I mockups.py and it rewrites the mock-*.png beside this page and prints every figure the boards quote. The ground colours are TileRaster.GROUND (L69), the deck TileRaster.DECK (L81), the highway TileRaster.ROAD, the page's colours and fonts ui/Palette.java; the three greys and the shallow blue are figures.py's proposals.

  • Not the game's own renderer. The plot rule is figures.py's estimate of the game's: its noise is box-relative, the plane is drawn latitude-longitude rather than Lambert's equal-area, small lakes are frayed discs, beaches are not drawn, and the city's buildings and streets are left out.
  • Two rendering rules found while drawing. The shallow share is of a cell's sea, so it must be interpolated weighted by the sea share (read plain, every coast grew a fringe of deep sea); and the coast's fray (the water noise at ±0.3 of a share) moves the Narrows' width by ±250 m from seed to seed, which decides whether a run bridges it.
