CityBuilderSim

A macroeconomic city simulator: an age pyramid that decides the workforce, a labour market that prices it, fifteen private sectors on one template trading real goods on real markets — two of them selling to the world, one of them to the mills, one hauling the others' freight and one building the cars the households drive — a share register and an exchange where the bank is the dealer, a bank that cannot lend below what its own money costs it, a central bank whose balance sheet is the only place money is made and whose dial is the price of it, households that each keep their own books including the people the books used to leave out, illness that kills, crime that has reasons, households that eat thirteen separate foods and choose between them by income, own cars and borrow for them, and spend what is left over a counter, a road that carries three kinds of traffic and can be relieved but never deleted, a trade band that is three quarters freight, and a currency with a balance of payments behind it, a vault that spends to defend it and a real rate that moves it. This is what it currently has and does.

The month

Everything in the game happens in this order, and most of the hard bugs in its history have been a line that made it into one phase and not the other. The order is genuinely a sequence, so it is numbered — and since 18 September it is also generated: docs/month-order.md lists every top-level statement of Game.nextMonth() and SimulationEngine.simulateMonth() with its line, which is what this list is struck against.

  1. 1
    The central bank settles with the treasury, and the promises are paid
    Before anything is priced. With the autopilot on, the Taylor rule takes the dial. Then the treasury pays the interest on last month's advances, takes last month's profit back as the remittance, repays its central bank out of any cash above zero, pays down arrears, and is advanced whatever it is still short (settleTreasury()). Then the holdings dial trades the city's term paper toward its target (openMarketOperation()), so this month's quotes carry it. Then the students' grant and EI are paid on the people the month opens with, in the month they are credited, and the bank pays its profit tax.
  2. 2
    The world turns and the land office re-prices
    World prices advance, the exchange rate is taken, the cost of living goes into the labour market. What the city pays outside and what businesses pay inside are both inputs to everything below.
  3. 3
    Business credit is priced, interest billed, property tax charged
    The bank prices the money first — its premium and its cost of funds — and a failed bank lends nothing. Before the statements run, so the figures each sector banks are net of it.
  4. 4
    Every building pays to stand, and every shipper pays its freight
    1% of build cost a year, charged to every owner in cash and materials and construction points — placed as a real order with the builders, not a line item. A forty-year stock at 1% a year is about 40% of the city's whole construction output. Then the railway's invoice on the month's trade, for the same reason: an expense on fourteen sets of books and revenue on the fifteenth.
  5. 5
    Fifteen income statements are struck
    Retail, real estate, industry, construction, heavy industry, mining, materials, business services, manufacturing, agriculture, food processing, rail, automotive, luxury retail, restaurants — each at the utilisation the month was traded at, not at today's, and each from the same template. The VAT is settled from the same figures between the two halves.
  6. 6
    National accounts, then the household books
    Household spending is consumption, read from the other end, so the people cannot be shown paying a figure the shops were not shown receiving. Consumption is three counters now — the grocers', the boutiques' and the kitchens' — plus the rent.
  7. 7
    The households buy cars, then spend the rest over a counter
    With the month's money fresh and before the investor can see it: the second-hand market clears first, because nobody pays forty for a car they can have for twelve, then the showroom; then the boutiques, and the kitchens right after, because they are the same kind of purchase out of the same surplus. The cars, the pieces and the meals count as demand for next month's strike.
  8. 8
    The private sector takes its turn
    Loans advance and matured principal comes back; then each sector forecasts its own signal six months out and funds what it plans in a fixed order: sell shares, spend the till, call money home from abroad, and only then borrow.
  9. 9
    Construction advances; roads recount; the posts are counted and the labour market reprices
    Sites take the month's points, buy the material that work drew on, and the builders recognise revenue on the same figure the sites advanced by. A shop that opens this morning trades this month and puts its trucks on the road this month — roads are recounted before anything reads them. Then the posts are recounted and wages walk toward what scarcity says per band, and per licence, against this month's posts and before anybody ages.
  10. 10
    Demographics
    Age; give birth; die of three separate causes — the band's own rate, illness, and violence; migrate; rebuild the households against last month's; commit and record this month's crime; settle health, education, the police and the transit fare; advance the ring of the sick.
  11. 11
    The economy and the services sync
    Population, wages, jobs, the fill rate and the utilisation ratios reach every sector — the road ratio per sector, because only a tram can make two businesses on the same street face different jams, and the health ratio the demographics step has just set.
  12. 12
    Cash moves, every market clears, ore is lifted, the paper is paid, the government's books are re-struck
    The utility books what its customers were charged; every market clears and every maker produces in one pass; the mines ask the ground. The paper sold between the presses settles to its holders, the households taking their share first, and the month's coupons and principal are paid holder by holder. The books are re-struck at the bottom of the month, against the same period's spending and after the coupon is known, because the start-of-month call mixed two months' tax fields.
  13. 13
    The bank settles, the owners are paid, the shares trade
    The bank pays for whatever paper the households did not take and earns a month of the discount on what it holds; then lending, repayments, interest, deposit rate, branches, the carry trade, the window and the interest on its reserves at the central bank, resolution if it failed, and the cost of funds struck once and final — then the central bank strikes its month's profit, the savers are paid, then dividends, then the exchange, then whatever is still idle goes abroad, the households' by the same rule as the sectors', and a household whose paper no longer out-yields its deposits sells a little of it back to the desk.
  14. 14
    The currency moves, and the money is counted
    MoneyAudit strikes pooled after − pooled before = inflows − outflows across every pool in the city and every flow across its border. A cent of residual is a finding. The hot money moves just before the strike, because it moves a pool; then the balance of payments, the currency and the price index, each reading a month that has finished settling. The currency reads the real rate against the world's, and on a month it is pushed weaker on a deficit the central bank sells the vault's dollars against the push — a capital transaction booked after the audit, because no pool moves on it. Nothing after the audit may move a pool. Every twelfth month autosaves, also before any skip and on quit.

What moved since the last edition. The top of the month is new: the treasury settles with its central bank before anything is priced, because the advances and the remittance are money made and destroyed and have to fall inside the audit's window, and the holdings dial trades after that settle and before the market is priced so that this month's quotes carry what it did (0.7.0, 0.7.1). The students' grant (0.7.1) and EI (0.7.3) are paid at the top of the month, on the people the month opens with; for as long as either had existed the households were credited last month's bill while the treasury paid this month's, and nothing saw it because the households are outside the audit's pools. The defence sells dollars inside the currency step (0.7.2). And the construction retainer — set, saved, reset and reformed, and paid by no line of the month — is gone (0.7.1); the standing policy is the lever.

Standing rule

A flow cannot be reconstructed from the state a month ended in. That is why the save carries income statements, the VAT ledger, the inventory baseline in units, wage history, loss streaks, plots consumed, the unburied backlog, last month's household shapes, the ring of the long sick, six months of prisoners, the railway's quote and what it carried, every sector's fleet and the memory of the commute, the treasury's journal for the month that ended and the one in progress, and what the vault sold defending the currency — none of them derivable by looking at the city.

The age pyramid is the population. It used to be a display beside a formula; now nothing else decides how many people live here — and since the long sick and the crime batch, nothing dies of only one thing.

The seniors were one band of fifty years until save format 27, at a flat 4.5%. Senior care asks for a place for everybody over seventy, and in the real world about one in nine of them is in one — 2% of seventy-four-year-olds against 29.6% of the over-85s — and one band cannot hold a fifteenfold gradient, nor a mortality curve that runs from 1.4% a year at seventy to over 30% at ninety-five. The two figures are population-weighted from Canada's 2019 life table, and the split is very nearly aggregate-preserving: total deaths among the over-seventies went from 6.5% a year to about 6.7%, and a seventy-year-old has 14.9 years left against the 15.4 the single band gave. The over-85s keep two household shapes of their own, because Elder living alone is the cell in trouble first when senior care is short.

Children, teens and adults were halved when illness started killing people, so that the new cause of death would be a redistribution rather than a surcharge. A month's deaths are now struck from three rates against the same opening balance — the band's own rate (scaled by care, and by who has no home), illness from the ring of the long sick, and violence from the month's homicides — capped together at 5% a month, with the cap scaling each part alike so the shares survive it. Every one of them is recorded by band, so the Reports tab can say what the city has died of since it was founded.

Bands drain exponentially rather than on a fixed schedule, so a baby boom smears into a bulge rather than travelling through the city as a wave. Nobody is tracked individually; the bucket only has a mean.

Migration

The city moves toward a target rather than snapping to one. Housing and jobs say how attractive the city is, not how many people are in it.

target = (0.75 × jobs × residentsPerJob + 0.25 × housingCapacity) × seniorCarePull residentsPerJob = (1 + 0.125) / adultShare // 12.5% labour slack arrivals = max(0, target − population) × 0.15 × crowding × crimePull departures = population × decliningPressure × 0.05

Gross flows, not net, so the screen can say why the number moved. Crowding interpolates to zero between comfortable households and the point where every flatshare and doubled-up home has already formed — a full city still takes arrivals, just fewer. Nobody leaves unless a pay tier's real pay has been declining twelve months straight or has stopped entirely. Four more pushes: a band whose wage is pinned at the minimum and still has more workers than posts sheds 2% of the surplus a month, bankrupt households leave, rent above the target burden deters arrivals, and crime deters arrivals — calibrated off Cullen & Levitt (1999), a tenth of the city's draw gone at twice Canada's crime rate, almost all of it as people not coming rather than people leaving. Arrivals carry a skill mix drawn from a world with far more labourers than graduates, weighted by the premium each band is paid here.

Households, pay, and what they remember

Fifteen household shapes, from SENIOR_ALONE and the over-85s' ELDER_ALONE through COUPLE_TWO_CHILDREN to SHARED_ADULTS. Families are built from the adults who work; everyone else has their own books. The mix follows the age pyramid and the job tiers; when homes run short, singles form flatshares first and families double up last.

The households remember. The builder keeps last month's households as far as the people still exist for them — 1% of them re-form each month by design — shrinking each shape by the worst shortage among the bands it needs, rebuilding only the remainder, and refitting tiers to the jobs by iterative proportional fitting. Before it, a city redrew every family from scratch every month and wallets moved with the redraw. Eight seeds: orphans at the end 490–633 → 11–19, hunger 4% → 0%, unemployment 14.8% → 12.2%. Everything a cell holds moves with the people when the shapes re-form — savings, debt, shares, the dollars abroad, the student loan, and since 16 September the cars — and the move is clamped so that a pool cannot hand out more than it holds, because the day it could, four retired cells owed minus 2.7e-29 dollars and the sign of that decided the exchange rate (see how it's checked).

Homes have sizes. A studio takes two, a low-rise flat four, a house six; a household has to fit, largest families into the smallest unit that fits, and a one- or two-person flat refuses a dependant outright. Rent is two markets — see prices. An empty home now costs its owner something to hold, and so does a full one: every building pays 1% of its cost a year to stand.

Seventy-eight ledgers

Eleven working shapes × six pay tiers, four retired rows, three kinds of out-of-work, students, orphans by age band, and the prisoners. Each keeps savings, debt, a lockout clock, shares in each of the sixteen listed companies, dollars held abroad, student debt, its cars, the month's investment income, the dinners it ate out, the share of its people who could pay for care and the city's own paper — thirty-one slots a cell in the save, each keyed by name so a new row never shifts an old one, and each new slot appended to the end so that an older save reads as a city that had none of it, which it did not.

Since 0.7.2 a cell whose census count falls under half a household (HouseholdBalance.EMPTY_CELL) is empty: it keeps its count, holds nothing and is paid nothing, and every stock it held folds to its own row first and then to the city — because a cell of 1e-15 households was still being paid, and one line divided a credit by it.

The household books, and a balance sheet

Every cell shows wages, the banded wage tax, pension contributions and pensions, the health premium, rent, tuition, interest, shopping, healthcare fees, the transit fare, and what is left — plus savings, debt, shares, the city's paper and cars per household, and a waterfall: payslip, rent, fees, food; short, and they spend savings, then sell the city's paper back to the bank's desk, then bring their paper home from abroad, then sell shares at the bid, then borrow from the bank at the city's rate plus a spread that climbs with what they already owe, and only then eat less — which makes them ill. A row six months of income in debt that still cannot cover subsistence goes bankrupt: 4% a month discharged, locked out of credit for a year, a quarter of them leaving the city. A row that can be evicted and still cannot pay loses the share of its homes the shortfall covers.

Spending follows income, and since 17 September it follows wealth too. A household plans to spend 80% of what is left above subsistence at the grocer's, so shopping is a budget rather than a headcount; on top of that it spends 0.33% of its net worth a month — four percent a year, the middle of the measured propensity to consume out of wealth — and puts half of the income it does not spend on food over a luxury counter and a quarter of it on a table. Dividends and the coupon on its paper abroad are paid home and enter the plan, and so, since 0.7.1, are the coupons on the city's own paper, paid into savings as untaxed investment income. A household with no debt, no lockout and more than three months of disposable income in the bank puts 30% of the excess into shares, and sends the rest of its idle savings abroad on the same rule the sectors use. It also takes the city's paper at issue: at the settle the households go first, taking a fifth of an issue for every point it yields over the deposit rate (HOUSEHOLD_PAPER_APPETITE) and never more than half (MAX_HOUSEHOLD_PAPER_SHARE), out of savings past the cushion, and the bank buys the rest. Pensions are pay-as-you-go, and both the contribution rate and the replacement rate are Policy dials. Contributions follow workers and pensions follow pensioners, so an ageing city buys a structural deficit without changing a single policy.

And since 0.7.3 saving answers the real rate. Everything a household plans above a basket a head — the propensity's share, the wealth term, and the surplus the counter and the table spend out of — is multiplied by one factor a month struck on the real deposit rate, the bank's deposit rate less the year's inflation: ten points of real return cut it by a tenth, ten points of negative real return raise it by a tenth, and it is held between a half and one and a half. Subsistence never moves, and at a factor of one the plan is the old plan to the bit. On the default seed on 22 September savers ended the run earning 3.63% real and the households planned 0.964 of what they would spend above a basket at zero; over 4,005 months the factor ran from 0.835 to 1.131, median 0.993. What it does to prices is almost nothing, and why is in the central bank.

Why the households were rich

Household net worth ran at 45 months of the city's GDP at month 480 — dead on the real world's sixty to eighty-four — and 5,072 by month 3,840. It did not sit at a wrong level; it diverged. Over one run the foreign coupons came to $2,649bn against $584bn of every wage the city ever paid, because the households kept about 79% of their wealth abroad at the world's 2% and the coupon was rolled where it was earned, so wealth compounded at 1.58% a year mechanically, 193× over 333 years. Three changes were ensembled one at a time: paying the coupon home took the ratio from 5,380 months to 3,412; letting investment income into the plan put it back to 5,465, because investment income is a flow and a bigger city pays more of it; the wealth term took it to 2,737 and gave the best city of the four. A stock that compounds can only be bounded by a drain that reads the stock. And with the coefficient pushed to nine times the empirical range the stock still rose, while delivery fell from 76% of plans to 57% — the households are not hoarding because they are misers; they are hoarding because there is nothing to buy. The cars, the boutiques and the kitchens are the answer to that, and net worth now oscillates (432 → 137 → 128 → 214 months of GDP) instead of climbing.

What a household owns, and borrows for

A car is a household stock, beside savings, debt, shares and the dollars abroad — it follows the people, it is saved per cell by name, a household that leaves takes it — and it is the first one that is not money, so a currency reform does not touch it. It is the first durable a household in this game has ever been able to own: the measurement that decided the shape found an unskilled couple banking $3,160 of a $4,880 month and sitting on $799,100, a hundred and sixty-four months of income, with $17.6bn of household savings in the city against zero household debt. That is not a rich city, it is a city with nothing to buy. So affordability barely binds in a mature city and binds hard in a young one, and the constraint is time: 2% of the households that have never owned one buy a car in a month, a car lasts 180 months and is replaced at once when it dies, one per household is saturation, and a cell buys whole cars — because a money ratio that comes out as a physical quantity has to cross at a grain coarser than the dust, or a reformed city wants an ulp more of a car and the two cities' trade balances part company. A city whose transit could carry everybody tops out at half a car per household, for ever; a metro built after the cars does not remove them, it stops them being replaced, and the fleet decays to the new ceiling over the life of a car.

And they borrow for it. Jerus: "make it so that citizens the car requirements are lowered and instead they finance with the bank." The rule is pay what you have and borrow the difference: a household hands over every dollar it has spare and a lender finds the rest, with a fifth of the price (CAR_DEPOSIT) as a minimum in cash and half the household's remaining credit room as the most a lender advances — that second number found by DenominationCheck, because every financed household was parked within a whisker of the bankruptcy line. With no credit line the rule reduces exactly to the old cash rule, so it can never refuse a purchase the old one allowed. The first draft — a fifth down, four fifths borrowed, compulsory — cut ownership at month 266 from 42% to 11%, because these households are cash-rich and income-poor and the loan moved the constraint onto the thing they have not. Seven of eight seeds are bit-identical over four thousand months; the young city diverges in month eight and has bought twice the cars by month ten. The loan is a bridge, not a note: the grocery waterfall repays it out of spare cash within a month or two. Household debt in this game had been exactly zero in every measured city. It is not any more.

The car goes last. Jerus: "if they are doing bad they cut back expenses, sell their cars or go for cheaper groceries." Two of the three were already in — the plan shrinks by itself, and a poorer cell already buys more grain and less meat — and the car was in nothing: a family sold its shares, brought its paper home, drew its last dollar of credit and went hungry with five months of take-home parked in the drive. There is a second-hand market at home now, and the fleet does not shrink when a household sells, it changes hands. The price is struck the way every market here strikes one: a floor at 15% of new (what a scrapper pays), a ceiling at 70%, a position between them from demand against supply — so a city cannot sell its way out of a general crash: everybody offers, nobody buys, the price is scrap, and the harness asserts it (8,000 offered, 0 sold, pinned at the floor). A household offers when the wage after the fixed bills no longer buys the food and half a year of that gap beats everything it has saved and can still borrow; a one-month test found households going bankrupt with the car still in the drive thirteen times for every one that sold it. Over 4,002 months of the default run when it shipped the trigger fired in two cell-months, both fractions of a household, and over 4,005 on 22 September in none, because the households were too rich to ever sell anything — which is the finding above, and the reason the counter and the kitchens exist.

Employment insurance is a real programme. A 1.63% premium off insurable wages funds a 55% benefit capped at Canada's maximum insurable earnings scaled to the city's unskilled median — $1,903 a month for an unskilled claimant, $3,158 for anyone at or above the cap. Both rates are Policy dials. The ring is struck on the inflow of the newly unemployed, cohort by cohort: jobs lost this month join at the wage they lost, each cohort is thinned monthly by hires, deaths and departures, and the twelfth cohort falls off the end into "EI run out". Arrivals who never found work enter at the unskilled wage and get no claim. Since 0.7.3 the benefit is paid in the month it is credited: struck again at the top of the month on the pool the month opens with and paid there (Game.payEiBenefits()), so the treasury, the ledger and the audit read one figure. For as long as EI had existed the out of work were credited last month's bill while the treasury paid this month's, and nothing saw it because the households are outside the audit's pools; OutsideCheck closes every Construction Depot in a small city and asserts that the closing month pays the EI of the pool it opened with and the month after pays the bill the closing struck, each credited the month it is paid; three of its lines fail on the old order. A save from 0.7.2 pays one month's EI twice on its first month, once.

Eviction is a level, not a rate. A row that can be evicted and is short loses households × min(1, shortfall / (rent + fees + interest)) of its homes; a quarter of those leave the city and the rest are on the street. Because a cell is an average, its households run out together — evictions arrive as a cliff. For most of the project's life nobody was ever evicted in the ensemble: an out-of-work adult ended a 333-year run holding millions, because the unskilled tier's savings are what they draw on first. It moves with the run. On the default seed on 18 September, with the wealth term and the counters in, 550 households were evicted over 4,002 months and 137 of them left; on 22 September, at 0.7.3, nobody was evicted over 4,005 months, nobody was left on EI at the end, and an out-of-work adult whose claim had run out ended the run with $303,200 put by. Close the biggest employer in a young city and the path works exactly as designed: 549 evicted in the first year, 440 of them in two months, 384 on the street at the worst, 127 still there four years later (measured on 11 September; no harness reproduces it since).

Housing serves each group with its own valve: an out-of-work adult alone counts a full door, a flatsharer a fifth, a doubled-up seeker or someone on the street none — so the rent bill is split over the doors actually paid for. The transit fare is a third fee beside the health fee and the tuition, split across the rows the way the clinic's fees are: from the day transit was built until 16 September it was credited to the city and debited to nobody, and nobody saw it because no fixture had ever built a bus — see transport.

04

Health

Three classes. Health is the effect — a multiplier on output. Healthcare is the service — an employer with a payroll and a cemetery with a finite number of plots. Sickness is the ring of people who have not got better, and it is the one that kills.

Sickness is the fourth utilisation ratio

It sits beside energy, water and roads — and, since 17 September, beside the fifth ratio, the sector's own fleet of vans — multiplies with them, and is carried across a save the same way. The one difference is the whole point:

output = capacity × fill × bEnergy × bWater × bRoad × bHealth × bVans payroll = wages × fill // no bHealth, no ratio at all

Output falls, payroll does not. The employer carries it — a mill with fifty on the books pays fifty wages in a bad flu month and sells four weeks of output in three. The workforce, the pyramid and the wage bill never move. A sick worker still gets paid, still shops, still pays rent.

Five things add on top of that baseline and the total is capped at 45%: an outbreak, the unburied (up to 15 points), hunger (up to 8), the share of the city with no home at 3.7 times the baseline, and the injured — half a month off work for every violent crime.

Outbreaks land on top: roughly one every five years, peaking 8–25%, decaying 55% a month, with hospitals taking 60% off the peak but never preventing one. The roll is a pure function of the month number, so an epidemic survives a reload without the generator's state going in the save — you cannot save-scum out of one. Measured over 4,005 months on the default seed, 22 September: 52 outbreaks, the city ill for 289 months, worst month 45% — which is the cap; and the run ends at 15.1% with no outbreak in it at all, nobody unburied and two points of hunger on a baseline of 12.8% that general care covered 35% of. On 18 September the same seed ended at 36.8%, fifteen points of it the unburied; the batches since moved every seed, so the two readings are two cities, not a trend.

The people who stay sick

Each band carries a thirteen-month ring of who fell ill when. Past two months sick, a share of them dies each month — 4% for babies and seniors, 8% for the over-85s, 2% for adults, 1% for children and teens. General care is what gets people out of the ring: it cures 50% of the sick a month untreated and 90% fully served. Babies and the retired fall ill twice as often as the city rate until their own care takes the excess away.

What general care stopped doing

It used to be a ×3 swing on the death rate of every teen and adult. That is gone: general care now acts only through the sick rate and through curing the sick. A fully served adult used to die at 0.15% a year; they now die at about 0.232%, base plus a sliver of illness. The halving of the base rate does not make that back at the good end — deliberately, and it is a live lever.

The two ways to bury somebody

A cemetery is a stock: plots are consumed permanently and the land never comes back. A crematorium is a rate: almost no land, a great deal of electricity, breaking even only when busy. Households whose monthly surplus would cover a plot over ten years choose burial; the rest choose cremation; overflow runs both ways. A city with neither leaves people where they fell, and the unburied make the living ill — up to 15 points on the sick rate, cleared the month a cemetery opens.

Somebody pays for all of it

Fifteen private sectors, each keeping its own books, its own cash reserve, its own debt and its own share register — and, since 11 September, each of them one class extending a single template. Eight of the fifteen have been added since, five of them in the three days to 18 September, and the whole point of the template is that each cost an afternoon rather than a week.

Jerus: "we should make a single template, and every single sector just extends it, and adds or overrides, right? cause in the future there will be over a thousand or near a thousand sectors." The eleventh to the fifteenth

Food processing is the eleventh. Jerus: "now a new sector which makes the ready meals, processed meals, snacks, drinks, and cooking fats?" Those five are 16.3 kg of the reference basket's 50, a third of what a household eats by weight, and every gram of them arrived by ship. Three plants, each decided by a different number: the Meat Works buys meat, vegetables and grains and makes processed meats and ready meals, with the input bill at 40% of revenue; the Snack & Oils Plant turns crops into snacks and cooking fats at 19%; the Bottling Plant turns crops into drinks at 4%. It took four calibration passes and three of them were not calibration: the generic planner sizes a sector by one good and skipped every template that did not make it, so two of the three plants were not refused but invisible; a first plant was valued at the import ceiling its own arrival would end, so processed meat stood at $8.90 the month the works was financed and $7.70 the month it opened, and the sector now prices its own plant with its nameplate in the supply; and the sales tax was not in the estimate, which matters here because an import carries no input credit and a city with no herds imports every gram — month 72 of one run read $35k of revenue, $1k of operating income and $5k of tax.

Then the calibration itself, by revenue per worker, which is the ratio that decides whether a maker survives a city growing up: the first pass ran $6,000–$10,700 a head against the two bakeries' $13,500 and $17,800 and a foundry's $33,600, and a Bottling Plant was six people making a tonne a day; the three are $17,100, $16,800 and $18,000 a head now, payroll 21–22% of revenue at mid-band. What the sector is for, in one measurement: at month 98 of a real city the same Meat Works is worth $16,559 a month on meat at a farm's export floor and $1,678 on meat off a ship. The building did not change; the farms did. Eight seeds: median GDP up 11%, $375.8M to $417.9M a month, population unchanged, the sector ending every seed with $52M–$86M of assets and zero write-downs in seven of eight.

Rail is the twelfth and Automotive the thirteenth, and both are told in transport: the railway is the business that wants to be paid the freight every price in this game has always had inside it, and the assembly plant is the first thing in the city that buys a beam or a machine — ore, steel, fabrication, and now something a person drives away in.

Luxury retail is the fourteenth. Jerus: "lets add restuarants as well as luxury stores, these two will absorb some spending as well", and on the pricing, "supply v demand, thats the most important thing." A Boutique and a Department Store import what they sell — the world has no shortage of watches — and what a city can be short of is a counter, so the margin is what gets struck, on the market's own rule with a mark-up in place of a price: 1.25× the landed cost with nobody in the shop, 4.00× with a queue at the door, and the position between them from what is wanted against what the counters can serve. It is deliberately an import, so a rich city's spending leaves rather than going round again. It is also a fragile business — on one seed it finishes with 22 write-downs and a 252-month borrowing ban — and the sizing of the two buildings against a city's luxury budget has not been tuned.

Restaurants are the fifteenth, and the rule Jerus attached is what makes them a different sector rather than a second boutique: a meal out replaces groceries. So a kitchen imports nothing. A month's income statement is written at the start of the month, off last month's utilisation. The simulation then moves it. Maintenance is an operating cost, and it is demand

Every building in the city pays 1% of its build cost a year to stand — every sector, every city-maintained category, and the bank's branches — in cash, in materials and in construction points, placed as a real order with the builders rather than written off as a line. On a forty-year stock that is about 40% of the city's whole construction output, permanently. Charging it made the city bigger: population 74,043 → 77,121, GDP $81.7M → $106.2M a month, unemployment 41.5% → 37.6% — because maintenance here is somebody's order book.

Construction recognises revenue as work is delivered: an order adds to unearned revenue and a backlog, each month's points earn their share, and the builders buy their material as they build. Booking it all up front made the sector look enormously profitable in a boom and catastrophic the year after. Its payroll has a 25% idle floor — crews cost something even with nothing to build, which is what makes the shedding spiral possible and worth warning about.

The symmetric form is load-bearing. demand/supply clamped to 1 prices a perfectly balanced market at the buyer's worst price — one mine feeding one mill used to clear at $399.40 against a $400 ceiling. Here a matched pair splits the band. Supply is flow plus a slice of the stockpile, never the stockpile itself: pricing off inventory alone is circular — low price, withhold, bigger inventory reads as more supply, lower price — and measured that way food collapsed to the floor in every scenario including a genuine shortage.

Neither end of a band is a constant: both are read off the world's own prices every month, through the exchange rate, so a devaluation is import inflation mechanically — and an incentive to substitute. And since 16 September neither end is a single number either: each is the world's own price plus, or less, the cost of moving one unit, so the band a city faces is three quarters freight and one quarter the world's margin, and the freight is a price a railway can win back — see transport. The shops buy local food first and import the shortfall; the mills take local ore first and scrap for the rest. One clearing pass runs every month: flow goods are made, seller-priced goods sold, each traded good priced, offered, bid for and allocated pro rata on both sides, then imported or exported, then the stockable goods made. Every fill is a trade; nothing in these classes moves money. The households are not a sector and cannot bid in a strike: they take cars off the makers' shelf at the struck price and import the rest, through the same door the city uses for building material, and a month's purchases are demand for next month's strike. A good with a floor of zero — a van, a wagon set — strikes at exactly zero when nobody here wants one, and a saved zero is restored as zero, because for one build a guard against a missing figure dropped it and a reloaded city read a mid-band price in its place.

What a household eats

Until September 2026 one good called Food did all of this: a unit was a person fed for a month, everything they ate, and every question about a city's diet had that one answer. The shelf is itemised now, and what goes into the basket is decided by income rather than by a dial.

Engel's law is the first half: as a household gets richer the share of its money spent on food falls while the amount rises. Bennett's law is the second: the calories move off starchy staples onto meat, dairy, fish and produce. That is why grains carry a negative income elasticity — a staple is an inferior good, and a household eats less rice as it gets richer, not more.

Neither law is told what to do at the top of the ladder. A basket is scaled by the lesser of what a household can afford and what it can eat, so a poor household is money-limited and goes hungry while a rich one is calorie-limited and buys dearer food instead of more. The falling food share at the top is something the model produces rather than something it is given — which matters, because the first draft typed that share in as a constant and produced households whose calories went down as they got richer.

There is a third axis that is not income at all. Convenience food is bought for time: a working parent buys it at any income, and an out-of-work parent with the same children and the same money does not, because what they are short of is money and what they have is afternoons.

Income is measured in multiples of what bare subsistence costs rather than in dollars, so a currency reform cannot move it and the number means something you can say out loud: this household can afford four times the calories it needs to survive. The shops stock one shelf for the whole city, averaged over its households by headcount rather than by spending — a shelf stocked for money would carry the rich end's fish and fruit for a city of labourers.

The shelf is thirteen pantries

A shop can sell a basket only if it has all of it: what it can serve is the good that runs out first, which is Liebig's barrel on a shelf. That is also how a units bug was caught — shelf room was still sized in units of food when the goods became kilograms, and one person-month is exactly 50 kg of them, so every shop in the city delivered 20% of demand for ever. A city with roads and a city without scored identically until it was found; afterwards, 87% against 31%.

When the shelf was itemised all thirteen were imported. Eleven of them can be made at home now: the ovens bake bread and bakery goods out of crops and export what the city cannot eat; the farms make dairy and eggs, meat, vegetables and fruit; the plants make the five processed goods out of meat, vegetables, grains and crops. Grains and fish still only ever come off a ship. And since 18 September the shelf has a second buyer: the kitchens buy the same thirteen in the reference basket's proportions and sell them cooked, one ninetieth of a person-month a plate.

The hunger measure was reporting zero in a city that was starving

Until 17 September the figure read sold over the shops' own demand, and the shops' demand was already the least of coverage, want and what could be afforded — the shops' performance against a queue the shops had themselves decided they could serve. At month 3,840 of seed 0 the households planned 21,446,946 baskets, the shops could serve 177,760 people, and the line said 76%. A basket is one person-month, so the city wanted a hundred and twenty-two baskets a head and was allowed one. Honest: 58% of people short, 1.4% of the groceries asked for. Not a reporting change: the hungry feed the sick rate, which feeds the labour supply, and it had been fed a zero. Turning it on cost the default seed 11% of its population and 14% of its GDP — revealed, not caused — and showed a cascade nobody had seen, sickness 12.9% to 34.5%, of which hunger is eight points and the unburied fifteen. The basket count is floored to a whole basket, because dividing money by money and keeping the fraction broke the currency-reform twin 1.5e-06 at a time.

How an expansion is paid for

In a fixed order, every month: sell shares if the register says the company should be raising equity, spend the till, call money home from abroad, and only then borrow. A company under a year old sells half of what it plans; a company with nine good months raises toward its equity target; a company with a bad year raises nothing and borrows nothing.

Capacity retires, and distress retires it faster

Six consecutive losing months, genuinely spare capacity by a 25% margin, and nothing on site — then the biggest holding is thinned, never past what is actually in use, never more than a quarter in one month. The streak is carried in the save, because forgetting it made scrapping save-scummable.

Separately, a firm that cannot pay sheds plant: twenty-four months of losses and a negative balance after the credit desk has already refused it. Six months killed every founding plant in testing; two years is the runway a real business gets. Occupied homes and the bank are never scrapped. A mine over a worked-out deposit now closes inside a year instead of borrowing to pay its crew for three — which makes the bust when the ore runs out sharper, and lets the bank survive it. And a distressed landlord can no longer demolish a city out from under it: the railway's bigger business cycle pushed Real Estate into distress deep enough that it sold 5,800 of a city's 13,272 homes in seven months and left 1,137 households with nowhere for a hundred and forty, because the retirement rule asked whether the segment had spare doors and a segment's slack is spread over four sizes. It is guarded, not fixed: nobody sells a door while the housing match says somebody has nowhere, and the proper fix — slack per size — is on the list.

Two banners exist because these fail silently

Builders being laid off — construction with no orders shrinks to fit, and everything you build runs through those crews. Nowhere to build — the private sector asks for land every month, is told no, and waits. Measured at 120 consecutive months in one ordinary run.

The owners & the exchange

For most of the project's life the corporate sector had no owners: it made money, held it, and nothing ever moved it out. Sixteen companies have a share register now — the fifteen sectors and the bank — and a market to trade them on.

The register

Every company is founded with shares issued to the city's households at $1,000 each against its book equity. A company reads its own record before it asks for money: new under a year, good at nine profitable months and not declining, bad at six or fewer or a losing year, normal otherwise. Its equity target starts at 30% of assets and rises with how volatile its income has been, capped at 70%; a new company aims at half. Households subscribe first — a cell with no debt, no lockout and three months of disposable income banked puts 30% of the excess in — and the world takes the rest, but only if the yield clears the world rate plus three points.

Forty percent of a positive month is paid out, from the till, or from the company's own money abroad if the till is short. A share is priced at the higher of book equity and the dividend capitalised at the world's required return, per share.

The exchange, and the bank as dealer

The bank's trading desk is the counterparty on every trade, and quotes nothing when the bank is dead. It quotes around fair value, its own book moves the quote, it never sells what it does not hold, and it marks what it holds at the lower of the quote and fair value. Its book is capped at a multiple of the bank's equity, which is money — and until 18 September that cap was the one figure in the exchange a currency reform did not divide. It is re-struck each month after the households settle, so for the first half of every month a reformed city's desk believed it had a hundred times the room it had; a household with no savings and a grocery bill it could not meet borrowed in the plain city and sold $1.03 of shares in the reformed one, and from there household debt was 4.2% apart in one month and the exchange rate with it. The bisect blamed the fourteenth sector's inventory term, and the term was right: it had moved GDP, GDP had moved what the desk's book was worth against its limit, and a cancellation that had been landing on one side of zero started landing on the other. A bisect names what perturbed a fault, not what the fault is; a latent member of that family is not a harmless one, it is one nobody has perturbed yet.

A month on the market runs in order: emigrants sell on the way out; the world trades on yield, never in a company under a year old; households buy the best yield above the deposit rate plus a point, spending 5% of their excess savings; then companies buy themselves back — a tenth of the company a year, only after a good year, only with six months of operating cost still in the till, and if the market is more than 10% over fair value the money goes out as a special dividend instead. A household short of money sells its shares before it borrows. Shares split at a hundred times their founding price, and a cumulative split factor keeps the price history readable per founding share.

The player does not trade. What the player sees is the desk's disclosure on the bank's income statement, its inventory at the mark on the balance sheet, an Its owners block on every company — quote, yield, market value, how much the desk holds, special dividends, buybacks, splits — a price chart per company, each household cell's shares at market, and the foreign share of each register on the balance-of-payments page.

The desk foots, since 19 September. Its opened lines on the bank's statement had left out the term that is usually the largest — the change in the mark on what it holds — so a loss sat over sub-lines that did not add to it. Re-marked what it holds (Bank.getMarkChange()) is a line of its own now, and when it is the bulk of a loss the note says why, which is Jerus's question — "explain to me the trading desk, cause a bunch of times it's losing billions": with buyers it cannot fill it quotes up to five times fair value to draw sellers, and it marks what it holds at the lower of the quote and fair value, so a wave of emigrants selling into a bubble costs it the difference the same day. BankCheck asserts the lines plus the re-mark equal the total, and on a played city they did in 120 of 120 months. Whether a dealer should bid at a quote it does not believe in is Jerus's. Since 0.7.1 the same desk also buys back the city's paper from the households, at the month's market value off the curve.

VAT with input credits

Sales tax is a real value-added tax: exports zero-rated with credits still claimable, imports charged at the buyer's rate and fully creditable, the city exempt both ways, residential rent an exempt supply. One deliberate departure from the real thing — the rate follows the producer, not the product — because under a product rate most of the sector dials would be cosmetic.

Net remittance is allowed to go negative and is not floored: under producer rates a sector buying high and selling low genuinely is owed money, and flooring would quietly turn a rate cut into a partial one. When it happens the screen names who is owed. And the producer pays it — out of its own cash, as it pays the profit tax on its statement's after-tax line. Both used to be collected by the treasury from nobody; closing the money identity found them.

GDP

Measured by expenditure, C + I + G + NX, replacing an income-approach figure that went negative the moment firms started paying interest. Profit is not production.

C what the households bought over three counters — the grocers', the boutiques', the kitchens' — + rent paid I construction work put in place + the change in inventory as a VOLUME, priced today: the food shelf at fixed weights, the makers' materials, the boutiques' stockroom G utility payroll + the health service's gross cost + the police's NX everything sold abroad − everything landed: scrap, food, materials, machinery, vans, watches, and the railway's fuel

Inventory is measured as a volume rather than a value, because valuing it books every price move as production — a rule that had to be re-learned the day food became thirteen goods, since the obvious way to add kilograms of grain to kilograms of fish is to value each at its own price. The thirteen are aggregated at fixed weights instead, so the figure is still a quantity. The builders' yard is deliberately excluded from the inventory term — work in progress is at contract value and already embodies it, so counting both subtracted the same brick twice — while the materials plant's warehouse is output made and not yet sold, and counts like a mill's. The boutiques' stockroom is the third term, and it was found by a negative month: a Boutique's three months of stock, 960 pieces at $9,000, lands in one month, NX took the landed cost out and nothing said the city still had the goods, so a city that took up shopping reported negative GDP sixty times over a run. Priced at what the shop paid, the arrival nets to zero and the margin is the only thing that scores; sixty negative months became one across eight seeds and 32,040 months. Any good a sector can hold and does not consume within the month belongs in that block the day the good is written. A household's car is not in C: a household turning money into a thing it owns is a portfolio move, like a share. Land sales appear in the government's books and nowhere in GDP: selling a field moves an asset that already existed, and counting it would let a city manufacture growth by trading land with itself. Education's cost is still outside G, which healthcare's and the police's are not, and the transit payroll joined it on the outside when the modes arrived; that is an inconsistency and it is on the list.

Standing subsidies

Any sector can be put under a standing policy that covers its losses exactly, uncapped, month after month. It is booked as a capital contribution, not revenue — the sector's cash rises and its income statement still says it lost money. The city goes overdrawn to pay it, and the overdraft — the central bank's advances since 0.7.0 — is priced as debt, so an over-generous policy bills you through the interest rate; and once the advances have reached their ceiling a subsidy is paid only from cash at or above zero, and what it is refused is owed to the sector as arrears.

Who holds the paper

Jerus: "yes households should be able to hold." Since 0.7.1 every domestic bond says who holds it — the principal held by the households and by the central bank, the bank whatever is left — and the split is what makes a bond market rather than a loan. At the settle after an issue the households go first, a fifth of it for every point it yields over the deposit rate and never more than half, out of savings past the cushion; the bank buys the rest and is the residual buyer; the central bank never buys at issue, because buying from its own treasury is printing, which is the advances' line. Coupons and principal are split by the shares struck before the payment: the bank's the way they always went, the households' into savings as untaxed investment income, the central bank's destroyed and remitted. A household short of money sells its paper back to the bank's desk at the month's market value before its dollars and its shares, and a leaver sells on the way out. The discount accretes. A free number had been holding a floor.

Business credit

A separate hierarchy, because a business loan must never charge the city's cash. Interest reaches the treasury only through the sector's income statement. Rates run the city's rate + 1% to 8% on debt-to-assets, the base never under the bank's cost of funds plus a point, plus a point for each prior write-down up to three — on the default seed on 22 September, with the city's note at 1.00%, the spotless sectors with little debt borrowed at 2.00%, a leveraged landlord with no write-down at 7.40%, and a sector with three or more write-downs at 5.00%. Loans are interest-only bullets over 36 months, originated automatically and only ever to cover a hole.

The underwriter now leaves a gap. It will lend to 0.9× assets; insolvency is at 1.5×. They used to be the same number, so the desk parked every borrower exactly on the write-down line. Past 1.5× a sector is written down to 0.6× and blocked from borrowing — twelve months the first time, twenty-four the second, thirty-six after that, a ban that also stops its investment advisor expanding on credit. One default is one episode, not one a month. The write-off is recorded per sector and carried in the save. Over 4,000 months the pair of changes took write-offs from $25.8bn to $1.35bn.

There is no concentration limit, and a fixture found the hole by accident on 17 September: Materials built its first plant, had to buy a fleet of lorries as well as the plant, borrowed $57,939 from a bank with $50,292 of equity — thirty-eight per cent of the whole book in one name — and defaulted in month 70, taking the bank's equity to minus $2,628. Neither half is a bug in the bank: a sector that buys vehicles as well as machines is a riskier borrower, which is the mechanic. But a lender that can put a third of its book in one name is one default from failing, and the households' car loans now sit in the same book. It is on the list. And 0.7.2 found the hole again from the other side: on the new path Manufacturing borrowed $74M against a bank whose equity was $30M, for a Fabrication Works that loses money before interest, and was written off at month 166 and its restructured remainder at 178 in four seeds of the eight — all of the rise in the ensemble's bank failures, 8 to 19.

The bank is somebody

Every loan in the city — to a sector, to a household, and whatever of the city's own paper the households and the central bank do not hold — is the Commercial Bank's money: one building, 29 jobs a branch, its own statement under Economy, and a trading desk that makes the market in every listed company. Its book is capped by the tighter of deposits × 6 and branches × 250,000. Past the limit it does not refuse; it borrows the rest at the central bank's window — abroad, until 0.7.0 — and charges for it, ramping to 18 points on every rate in the city. A city with no bank is simply a bank with no capacity, so "credit at a punitive rate" falls out of the formula. Branches build themselves through the retail advisor when strain passes 70%.

The bank lends to households now, for cars, and the audit already carried both legs of that: the cash leaving the bank and the debt arriving on the household balance each other with nothing new written. The bank prices its own deposits. Its baseline is 45% of what it earns in interest, since 0.7.0 including what its spare cash earns at the central bank; it will never bid past a zero net interest margin; and it only bids above the baseline if it is genuinely profitable after payroll, upkeep and write-offs, lent out past 80% of capacity, and short of deposits rather than short of capital — sweeping twelve steps up to its own lending rate and forecasting what each would attract. The result is a market rate that households, the exchange and hot money all read. And it quotes no more than it charges, since 0.7.3: a founding bank's first month paid savers 45% of everything it earned, the placement of its own capital included, over $5.7k of deposits, which read as 7,567% a year and would have put the households' spending at its floor in month 3 of every seed. The rate it reports is capped at its lending rate (Bank.isDepositRateCapped()) and what it pays is unchanged; the cap binds in 17 to 236 months of each default run — 231 of them on the default seed on 22 September — a book lent at older and dearer rates paying savers more than today's lending rate, and in those months every reader of the deposit rate sees the capped figure.

A write-off costs the book, not the cash. Its capital ratio is measured against 8% required; below it the bank is insolvent, which is one of the shocks that can start a run on the currency. When the city recapitalises it, it recapitalises to 1.5× the minimum rather than to the line, so the next bad month is not another failure. The path from 278 jobs a branch to 29, a deposit limit from $60k to $250k, honest underwriting and an escalating exclusion took it from 308 failures in 4,000 months to none in seven seeds of eight.

Nothing borrows below what the money costs

For most of the project the bank could be made to lend at a rate below what it was paying for the funds. Three separate mechanisms did it — the city's bond floor, the business desk's spread over a risk-free rate, and a carry trade that borrowed abroad to buy domestic paper — and none of them was wrong on its own terms. The bank simply had no veto.

cost of funds = depositRate × (1 − wholesaleShare) + windowRate × wholesaleShare // the window: policy + a point, since 0.7.0 any rate = max(riskFree, costOfFunds + MIN_MARGIN) + its own premium // MIN_MARGIN = .01, one point

One rule, three call sites. It is blended, not marginal — the first cut used whichever source the last dollar came from, and the floor flipped between 1.9% and 13.6% across twelve consecutive months. And it is struck once, at the close of the month, and carried in the save: the live path priced mid-month and the load path priced at the end, so a city reloaded from disk quoted a different rate than the one it had just been running at — 0.0135 against 0.0133, which is the kind of divergence a harness catches and a player never would.

Sixteen seeds, before and after

Bank failures 5.0 → 0.0 median. Recapitalisation $3.8bn → $0. Closing equity $6.6bn → $9.1bn. Wholesale funding 24–47% → 0–8% of income. Months run at a loss 44–58% → 3–14%. Every seed lifetime-profitable, and the "gentler financing" calibration was not touched to get it.

The obvious next step — make the bank a real intermediary, funding domestic investment out of domestic savings — was measured and abandoned. The bank lends 1.87% of its deposits at home and runs at 31–49% of the capacity it already has. The city's savings are on the order of two hundred thousand times what its productive sector wants to borrow. The bank is demand-limited, not funding-limited, and no amount of deposit-gathering changes that.

Borrowing in somebody else's money

USD versions of all three instruments, treasury only. A foreign bond holds its face and coupon in dollars; every public getter answers in local money at today's rate, so a devaluation makes the debt dearer everywhere in the game at once without a call site knowing. Priced on exports, not GDP — the only dollars a city has ever earned came from selling something abroad: a 2% world base, deliberately below the domestic floor (that is the trap), plus a solvency term on dollar debt as years of exports and a service term on next year's bill, capped at 16%. Because the measure is taken in local money, a devaluation worsens the ratio, raises the premium, and makes the next bond dearer — Mexico 1994 and Argentina 2001 without a special case.

The world's paper has a curve since 0.7.2: foreignCurveRate(months) is the world's base plus the country premium plus the same term-premium table, so a dollar issue prices by maturity and a dollar bond is valued at its remaining months on it — the dial moving no longer moves a dollar bond's value, and the country premium moving does. On the way the dollar quote was found to be a money pump: it scaled the service the city already owed and left the new bond's own out, so a thirty-year dollar round trip netted the city $7.5M over eight rounds. It prices its own coupons into the premium now, by a fixed point (quoteForeignRate(Debt, months), walked by Game.selfPricedForeignRate()), and RestructureCheck's dollar round trip, which fails on 0.7.1, is neutral by construction. A dollar bond bought back is declared leaving the country.

The window shuts on conditions the player can see coming: 14 years of exports owed, service past 45% of exports, dollars owed and nothing sold abroad, or five years after a default. Existing paper still falls due. At issue the player chooses to convert and spend or to hold the dollars as reserves. Default tears every foreign bond off the books, no cash moves, the ratios read as perfect — and nobody lends a defaulter a dollar for five years, with a scar on the price that outlasts the closed window. It also fires on its own when the treasury goes a full year of tax revenue into the red with dollars falling due — and since the central bank advances every shortfall at the top of the month, only on a month whose own bills, a balloon falling due, come to more than a year of revenue; whether the rule should read the advances is on the list.

The central bank

Until 0.7.0 the game had a central bank, and it was the rest of the world. The commercial bank placed its spare cash abroad at the world's 2% and funded its shortfalls abroad; the policy dial only floored the lending rate on top, which is why it stopped mattering past 17%; and money was created only from outside — the founding, exports, capital coming in, the bank's wholesale funding — so the price level had nothing at home to answer to. Five batches, 0.6.11 to 0.7.3, brought the marginal role home. This is what the central bank is, what it holds, what each of its three dials does, and — measured, and said plainly — how little the rate still moves prices.

What was asked for

Jerus: "…we implement a central bank, and basically the central bank would buy gbonds or sell gbonds from thin air … the feds sheet would show how much debt it holds, like debt to itself aka money printing, like QE and QT … and that is the M2 supply or what do you think?" Decided the same night: a floor system, households who can hold the city's paper, and an autopilot beside the dial. Each batch was gated on the whole suite and on the eight seeds, and the design note that set them out is the-central-bank.md.

The books

CentralBank is a balance sheet the player reads, on the Money page under Finances. Assets: advances to the commercial bank at the window; advances to the treasury, its overdraft, which is what "printed" means on the screens; the city's paper it holds, at face; and the vault, read from ForeignAccounts rather than moved there, because the treasury still buys and sells reserves and the currency's code has to see them. Liabilities: reserves and currency, which together are M0. Currency is zero — nobody in this city holds cash outside the bank — and kept so that the day somebody does it has a line. Its equity is what the two sides leave.

Money is made and destroyed here and nowhere else. An advance, interest on reserves and the remittance are money issued; a repayment, the window's interest and the advances' interest are money retired. MoneyAudit sees the central bank as a boundary participant, like the world, and declares both under a scope of its own, so the month's issued less retired is the change in M0 to the cent — the difference between money from thin air and a residual. CentralBankCheck section 1 walks a month with every kind of flow in it and asserts exactly that. M0 is a ledger of what this bank has made and not taken back, not a reading of the commercial bank's till, because money reaches the pools without passing through a central bank — exports, capital, the founding — and the two could not both hold the identity.

M2 is what the public holds: the households' deposits, the sectors', the world's, and currency (Game.getM2()). It grows when the bank lends reserves on or the treasury spends printed money, not when the central bank buys from the bank. Both are series, with a year of each on the Money page and a column each in the year book. On the default seed on 22 September the run ends with M0 at $2.39B, all of it reserves, M2 at $78.0B, the central bank having made $18.5B and destroyed $16.1B since founding and remitted $486M to the treasury — and its equity at minus $2.39B, a loss carried from paying interest on reserves in money it made, which is exactly what printing to pay interest on reserves looks like. On 0.7.2's eight default seeds it ended below zero in every one, at minus $1.1B to minus $3.3B.

The rate is a floor

The dial is the rate the central bank pays on reserves. The commercial bank's spare cash sits there earning it, where it used to earn the world's 2% abroad, and its shortfalls are borrowed at the window at the dial plus WINDOW_PENALTY, one point, where they used to cost the city's own paper rate plus two points and a stretch; a failed bank is charged nothing at the window while it is resolved (0.7.1), because its equity is zero and a charge would fail it again. Savers get their share of what reserves earn — a bank whose only income is its reserves pays them 0.675% at a 3% dial and 2.25% at 10%, the first time the dial has ever reached a household. Nothing lends below what reserves earn and nothing borrows above what the window charges, so the dial is the price of money: the city's note prices at the dial plus its spreads and the bank's premium, and MonetaryCheck reads the bank's rate and the city's at exactly the dial at 3, 10, 20 and 40%. On the way it found a premium counted twice: the carry trade and the bank's deposit bid were priced off a rate that already carried the strain premium, and lendingRate() added it again. The carry trade itself is untouched, and is now the priced alternative rather than the marginal source.

Interest on reserves is paid in money made for it, and that is the one place the floor has no floor of its own. Held at 30% or 50% for a whole run, M0 compounds to 108 to 3×1014 $B while prices stay near one — spare cash earning the rate in new money that becomes spare cash — and the same mechanism took M0 to $1.25 quadrillion in the six broke seeds held at 0.7.1's stop of 25%. It is on the list.

Autopilot. A toggle on the monetary page hands the dial to the Taylor rule each month — the rule the page already prints, which since 0.7.2 is clamped only by the dial's bound at 100% — until the player's hand takes it back; saved, and held in a playtest by -Dplaytest.autopilot; the dial itself is held for a whole run by -Dplaytest.policyRate. With the rule on the dial on all eight seeds, 0.7.2 took bank failures from 27 to 17, the dial peaked at 30.5% and no seed spiralled: with the rule's hand on it, a credible real rate holds.

Advances, the ceiling, and who is paid

A treasury below zero at the top of the month is advanced its gap at the policy rate, repays from the first cash above zero before anything else, and may owe up to a ceiling in months of its trailing revenue. The emergency note it used to sell its bank is retired, and a save still carrying one runs it off. The ceiling is a dial since 0.7.2 — advancesCeilingMonths, 0 to MAX_ADVANCES_CEILING 36, default DEFAULT_ADVANCES_MONTHS 6, chips at 3, 6, 12, 24 and 36 months on the monetary page — saved under its own key, so an older save reads six, and held in a playtest by -Dplaytest.advancesMonths.

Past the ceiling the treasury pays by Jerus's rule, asked what a treasury past its ceiling does: "pay promises first, cut the rest." Every payment goes through Game.treasuryPays(TreasuryLine, amount). Promises — pensions, EI, health, the schools, the police and prisons, the city's own services, coupons and principal at home and abroad, the interest on the advances, the student loans already lent — are paid whatever it takes, past the ceiling if they must. Discretionary lines — the subsidies, the students' grants, repairs to the city's own buildings — are paid only from cash at or above zero once the ceiling has bound, and what they are refused is owed as interest-free arrears, by line, listed on the Government tab and paid down first when cash returns. Purchases — land, buildings, capital into the bank, reserves, buybacks — are simply not made. The central bank's profit — the interest it takes on its advances and, since 0.7.1, on the paper it holds, less what reserves cost it — is remitted to the treasury the next month as a revenue line; a loss is carried and recovered before anything is remitted again.

It is the floor the overdraft never had. On 0.7.0, held at a 10% dial, the six seeds that had compounded to $193 quadrillion and NaN ended owing $0.1–2.0B, the audit closed every month, and seed 0's bank failures went from 161 to 16; the arrears grew in a straight line rather than compounding, and none of the five that drew recovered. On 0.7.2, with the currency answering the real rate, none of the six draws at all. The default seed on 22 September never drew an advance: it ended owing nothing against a ceiling of $2.71B, six months of its revenue.

The holdings dial: QE and QT

Quantity, beside the price. CentralBank.targetShare is the share of the city's outstanding term paper — serials and term loans, not notes — the central bank aims to hold, from nothing to MAX_QE_SHARE, half, because past half the market it is the market; chips every ten points on the monetary page, saved, and held in a playtest by -Dplaytest.qeShare. Once a month, in openMarketOperation() — after the treasury has settled with its central bank and before the market is priced, so this month's quotes carry it — the holding moves toward the dial by at most QE_SPEED, a quarter, of the largest of the dial, the setting before it and the share actually held: four months at most, exactly four to or from nothing, so a sale finishes where a share of the gap never would. It never buys at issue, and it trades only paper the bank has already paid for, pro rata, at the curve's market value; buying is money issued and selling money retired; it carries the paper at face and takes what it paid above or below face into the month's profit, so the remittance carries it. The coupons on its share are destroyed and come back as the remittance: debt to itself. The setting before the dial is saved beside it and restored without moving (restoreTargetShare()), because the first build forgot the pace on every reload.

compression(months) = termPremium(months) × (share of the term paper held / MAX_QE_SHARE) × QE_COMPRESSION // QE_COMPRESSION 1.0

So at 30% held the fifty-year sits exactly 0.90 points under the table and the note has not moved, and at half the term premium is gone. With the playtest's borrowing sent home and the dial at 30% (0.7.1), the central bank bought exactly 30% of each issue's face in four months from the month after its settle, at 0.99 to 1.05 of face, and took 0.90 points off the fifty-year while it held it. It moved nothing else — the runs match the ones without it month for month until the first purchase and drift apart after, trajectory noise rather than a channel — because the playtest's advisor reads only the short end, which the compression never touches. CentralBankCheck sections 11–15 assert the purchase at QE_SPEED × 30% at face and at the market value, M0 moved by exactly it, compression(240) exact and the note untouched, the coupon destroyed and remitted, the dial to nothing selling back within the speed's months, the pace across a save, and a reform that scales the holdings and not the dial.

The defence spends

Until 0.7.2 the vault damped a push on the currency and not a dollar left it: a capacity that cost nothing, which is why the default player never saw it work. Now the central bank sells. On a month the currency is pushed weaker and the city ran a deficit, it sells the vault's capacity times the month's own deficit — its current plus financial account when negative, in dollars at the month's rate, not the trailing window the push reads — and never more than the vault holds. What damps the push is the realised absorption, what was sold over what was wanted, so a full vault damps what its cover says, one running low damps less each month, and an empty one damps nothing. A push with no deficit that month sells and damps nothing; a rise is never met, because leaning against appreciation is buying dollars, which is a decision, not something a vault does by standing there.

push = trade pressure + ratePressure // ratePressure = −realRateDifferential × RATE_PULL, held within MAX_RATE_PRESSURE either way absorption = MAX_ABSORPTION × min(1, cover / COMFORTABLE_COVER) // .85 at six months of imports dollarsSold = min(absorption × deficitUsd, vault) // only on a push to weaken, in a month that ran a deficit realised = dollarsSold / deficitUsd rate ×= 1 + push × (1 − realised) × openness × DRIFT_SPEED rate += (parity − rate) × REVERSION

A capital transaction, not money destroyed. The buyer is the world, and the local money it hands back is money the pools had already paid out for the imports: the deposit half of the textbook happened the day the goods were bought. So the vault falls by the dollars, the central bank's equity falls by their local price (vaultSpent(), a line under equity on the Money page, spent defending the currency since founding), M0 does not move, no pool moves and the audit declares nothing, and the month's profit and the remittance are untouched. It is booked at the reprice, after the audit, through dollarsSold(), with the month and the lifetime kept apart (getDefendedThisMonth(), getDefendedLifetime()). The first build retired the money instead and took seed 0's M0 to minus $255M, a bank that had destroyed more than it ever made. The Exchange page says what was sold this month and since founding, and the Trade tab's forces page shows the four terms that exist: trade, the real rate, the vault's defence and parity.

What it does with the founders' billion is spend it on structural deficits, not crises. On the eight seeds every one sells more than half of it and six all but 1%, over 22 to 225 sale months, half of it gone by month 628 to 3,747 — while the playtest's advisor buys it back with the treasury's cash, so that one seed sold US$1.29B out of a US$1B vault. MAX_ABSORPTION .85 at a COMFORTABLE_COVER of six months spends about 0.85 months of cover per deficit-sized month, so a vault at six months of cover meets the first deficit-sized month at full strength and damps less every month after, about half as hard five months in. On the default seed on 22 September the defence sold US$1.06B in 174 months, the most US$62.3M in month 725; half the founders' dollars were gone by month 726 and all but a hundredth by month 1,774, the vault ended at US$134k, and $531M of the central bank's equity went on it. Whether the defence should answer only a crisis is Jerus's.

The demand channel

For the rate to bite at home a household's saving has to answer the real return, and since 0.7.3 it does. What a household spends above a basket a head is scaled by one factor a month, struck on the real deposit rate — the bank's deposit rate less the year's inflation, Game.realDepositRate() — before the households plan. Deposits and not the curve, because the marginal saver's return is what the bank pays on every balance every month. Subsistence never moves; the propensity's share, the wealth term and the surplus the counter and the table spend out of all scale by it, so a hike does not hand what the grocer lost to the counter — scaling the want alone had pushed the surplus up, and a hike raised spending. At a factor of one every line is the old plan to the bit. Nothing is saved; the load path strikes it again. The monetary page says it in words: "savers earn X% real, so households spend Y% of what they would at zero."

spendFactor = clamp(1 − SAVING_RESPONSE × realDepositRate, SPEND_FLOOR, SPEND_CEILING) realDepositRate = the bank's deposit rate − the year's inflation // SAVING_RESPONSE 1.0, provisional, Jerus's number · SPEND_FLOOR 0.5 · SPEND_CEILING 1.5

The channel is in, and it moves very little

Most of that point is still the currency's: the rate moves inflation by making the currency dearer and imports cheaper, which it did before the channel existed. The channel fires every month and reaches almost nothing, for four reasons. Weak pass-through. The bank pays savers DEPOSIT_PASS_THROUGH, 45%, of what it earns, not a rate, so the deposit rate carries about a quarter of the dial — 11% at a 40% dial — and the factor runs only from 0.99 to 0.88 across the whole range; and the comment above the constant says Jerus asked for 55% to savers. Nothing to act on in the index. Food is four fifths of it, and the shelf does not answer money at the margin: at the founding it sits at its floor on every row, and in a grown city the want is thirty times what the shops can serve, so the scarcity mark-up reads the shops' coverage and a tenth less want moves no price. The counter and the table are not in the index, and that is where discretionary money goes. Saved money compounds: at a held high rate savings earn interest paid in new money, and the wealth term spends a fixed share of a stock growing faster than the factor cuts it. On the default eight the factor's median is 0.994; held at 10, 30 and 50% the six broke seeds swing 1.25×, 1.75× and 2.24× where they swung 1.24×, 1.90× and 2.01×, and their index ends higher at 30 and 50%. A held high rate does not deflate through demand.

A demand channel needs a price that answers to demand. The assertion is green on the transmission as a whole, not on this channel, and the next rate that bites will be a shelf priced by scarcity against money rather than coverage, or a deposit rate that passes the dial through.

Prices, the currency & the world

The city has a price level, a currency with a name, a vault of dollars, a balance of payments, hot money that comes for the spread, a rent market that no longer follows wages, and — since the surplus had nowhere to go — somewhere for its savings to sit.

A price index, and prices that ration

A fixed basket — the shelf price and rent, weighted by what households actually spent in a base month two years after the city started shopping — is the city's price level. The basket does not re-weight to hide inflation. The shelf price is cost-plus and rises to 1.6× at total shortage, so a congested city spends more at the shops for fewer goods, and the household screen says who went short.

The index remembers its own high and low water-marks, and the month each was set. An end-of-run figure says where a city finished, not what it lived through: a run that ends at 0.98 having been at 1.31 and 0.62 is not the same city as one that never moved, and until the marks were kept there was no way to tell them apart from the outside. The pair and the swing between them are carried in the save; a city saved before the marks existed opens with both sitting on today's level, which is the only honest thing to say about a past nobody recorded.

Rent is two markets: family homes and studios, each with a cost floor from the cheapest template that serves that segment, at today's cash and materials prices spread over a landlord's yield of 5.8%, times that segment's households per front door, unbounded both ways, closing a twelfth of the gap a month because a lease is twelve months. Taking one floor across the whole portfolio and applying it to both legs was what turned rent into a constant — every building earning the same $445 a head whatever it cost, and a city that built 138 low-rise blocks and no studios with 1,847 households wanting one. Struck per segment, studio rent went $445 to $706 and studio doors over 4,000 months went from zero to 19,920. Land is deliberately not in the floor: land price is a capitalised rent, an output of this market, and feeding it back in grew a number because it grew. Overbuild and rent falls below cost and the landlords eat it.

Danzik dollars, and what moves them

The local currency is the Danzik dollar, D$, against the US dollar. Since 0.7.2 its rate moves on three things: pressure from the balance of payments; the real rate differential, Game.realRateDifferential() — the policy rate less the city's inflation, against the world's base rate less the world's realised inflation — at RATE_PULL 4 a point, so money moves toward the higher real return; and a pull toward relative parity (REVERSION, a fifteen-year half-life) that says where the rate should end up. The drift that moved it every month by the whole of the inflation differential, rate *= 1 + (local − world) / 12, is deleted. With imports repricing through the rate, wages chasing the whole index and the shelf cost-plus, a gain of one on that link meant a city whose prices rose 40% a year saw its currency fall 40% a year by rule, whatever its central bank did; Jerus's 0.6.9 city, 399× its founding prices in twenty-five years, was that loop run for 306 months. The inflation differential still reaches the currency, through parity, which rises with local prices, and through the trade balance a cheap or dear currency earns — but not month by month, by rule. A spiral needs an actual outflow to continue, and a credible real rate stops it: in CurrencyCheck, ten months of 40% inflation with the real gap at nothing move the rate to 1.0067, the parity pull alone, where the drift would have taken it to 1.3649. The world has its own inflation, a persistent moving average drawn low in 1–8%, deterministic, with its level pulled back to trend so that 333-year runs stay usable.

current account exports − imports − interest paid abroad − foreign savers' interest financial account what the city's owners send abroad, less what comes home position reserves − foreign debt // revaluation lives here, not on any statement real rate gap (policy − inflation) − (world base − world inflation) // RATE_PULL 4 a point hot money spread = max(depositRate, cityRate) − worldRate − countryPremium // cityRate before strain, at most MAX_SPREAD

The caps on the channel are numerical guards now: MAX_RATE_PRESSURE went from .8 to 4.0 and binds only at a hundred-point real gap, and the hot money's appetite, CapitalFlows.MAX_SPREAD, from .06 to .25, measured against the city's rate net of the bank's strain premium (getRateBeforeStrain()), because what a strained bank charges is not a return. RATE_PULL went from 6 to 4, the one retune the batch allowed itself: at six the loop's gain was 1.44 against the deleted drift's 1.0, and one seed whose dial sat at 0.08% went to the guard with its prices at 115× founding; at four the gain is 0.96, and no default seed goes near a guard. The cost is that a credible real rate buys two thirds of the support it did. The channel is only as good as the hand on the dial. The Trade tab's forces page shows the four terms that exist — trade, the real rate, the vault's defence and parity — and what the reprice was handed rides the foreign accounts' saved array, the defence at slots 25–27 and the real differential with its two inflations at 28–30, so the page survives a reload.

And since 0.7.3 there is no guard a player will meet. ForeignAccounts.MAX_RATE and MIN_RATE were 100 and .01 and pinned the currency — the last acts of Jerus's year book were a currency at its guard with imports no longer rising — and they are 1e9 and 1e-9 now, scaled by a reform: a guard against a rate run to nothing or to infinity, never a price the game expects to see. Past a hundredth of a cent the strip's second line turns round and says what a US cent costs, US¢1 = D$1,000 at a rate of 100,000, instead of printing 0.00¢, and every page prints a rate through one formatter (Money.fxRate()). On the default seed on 22 September its dearest dollar was 1.000 local, in month 2; it spent no month weaker than twice parity and none at a guard, prices averaged +0.05% a year, and the run ended with the rate at 0.482 local to the dollar, 53% under a parity of 1.016.

The vault

The founders leave one. Jerus: "of the 3.5B you start with, 1B is in usd in the reserve, so you only see 2.5B start with" — because 99% of players would never buy reserves. Since 0.6.10 a new city opens with D$2.5B in the treasury and US$1B in the vault, bought on day one at the opening rate and booked as the purchase it is (Game.FOUNDING_CASH, FOUNDING_RESERVE_USD), and for its first ten years the Exchange page says where the dollars came from. It is kept in dollars. Checking the founding reserve found it in the wrong currency: it was a local figure at the price paid, so a hundredfold fall turned a US$1B vault into US$10M and its import cover fell with it, while the dollar debt beside it was revalued every month. The stock is dollars now, its local value the dollars times the rate, cover no longer moves with the currency, and the month's move is a revaluation line, not cash and not an audit flow. The treasury still buys and sells reserves; the central bank reads the vault as its asset and, since 0.7.2, spends it defending the currency (the central bank).

A reserve defends a currency; it does not hold one down. Jerus's rule, chosen from three after the founding reserve caused the very year book it was meant to prevent. The vault used to damp the pressure on the rate both ways, so a deep one muted the surplus a weak currency earns and the support of the policy rate — the two forces that pull a currency out of a spiral — and with the founders' dollars in it three of eight seeds reproduced Jerus's city, two of them past 140× their founding prices with the currency at its guard. It damps only a push weaker now, and the eight went to a median price swing of 1.60×, none past 2.81×, with sudden stops 8 to 0.

And the strip carries both. Between the population and the cash sit two panels: prices against founding over inflation year on year, grey within three points of the 2% target, amber outside it and red past 10% either way, so a deep deflation reads red too; and the rate both ways in the currency's own names, US$1 = D$x over D$1 = US¢y, grey at or above parity and down to 5% under it, amber more than 5% below it and red past 25%. Every figure comes through a getter, and the strip recomputes nothing.

The surplus, and where it goes

A city that exports steel used to run a surplus of 30–50% of GDP with nowhere for it to go: no household bought anything abroad, nobody was paid a dividend, and no saver could own a foreign asset. The currency simply appreciated — about 10% a year, 1.20 down to 0.44 over 250 months — until every exporter in the city was dead, twenty times a run.

Outward investment is the door. A sector with an idle till buys the world's paper when the world pays more than the bank — forty times the spread, up to nine tenths of its wealth, moving 4% of the gap a month out and 10% home — and brings it home to fund a plan, pay a dividend or clear an overdraft. Households run the identical rule on their own savings. The coupon is rolled where it is earned, an income credit matched by a financial debit, so a rentier's surplus does not appreciate the currency, and the currency is priced on the whole balance rather than the trade half. The median run went from 33% below parity to 3.5%; exports went $33M to $124M a month.

And since 17 September the households spend abroad too, through three doors the surplus did not have. A car the city did not make is an import at the ceiling; a watch always is, deliberately, so that wanting luxuries is money that leaves rather than going round again, and import substitution, if it ever comes, is something a player achieves; and every tonne the railway hauls burns fuel priced in the world's money, charged as a real import before there is any oil good, so that a reform cannot forget it. A meal is the exception — the food in it was already counted when it crossed the border, and the margin is wages and rent here.

Hot money comes for the spread net of the risk, at 8% of the gap a month, and leaves at 20%, straight into and out of the bank's deposits — which are its lending capacity, so the boom and the sudden stop are procyclical by construction. A run needs a fragile position (a material stock of it with under 25% backed by reserves) and a shock: the currency down 12% in a year, the bank insolvent, or a recent default. Always painful, never fatal.

Who owns the bank is a curve of financial deepening: a city with no savings has no domestic investors, a city with $124B on deposit owns its own banks. The bank's capital follows that curve. Its wholesale funding followed it too, which is what stopped a $156B book declaring its entire funding cost as interest paid abroad, until 0.7.0 moved the funding to the central bank's window, where the interest is paid at home and declared as money destroyed.

The policy rate, and dropping zeros

The policy rate is the player's dial, with an advisory Taylor rule beside it saying what it would do and why, obeying the Taylor principle — and since 0.7.0 an autopilot that hands the rule the dial; the dial itself, and everything it now moves, is the central bank. The playtest works the dial a quarter of the way toward the advice each time, because a rate that sat at 3% for three centuries would leave the whole monetary half untested. And once the city's own price index passes ten times founding, a currency reform unlocks: divide everything by 10, 100 or 1,000, the player's choice, never automatic. Every money constant in the game is an instance field seeded from its constant and redenominated with everything else; foreign prices are the exception, quoted in someone else's money and reached through the rate. The reform instant is bit-exact and conserves money; the following month is a few percent adrift in the discrete decisions, which is filed and has a plan.

Land

The city starts with thirty blocks, allocated so that a power station and a water plant leave two spare — land is taught on turn one.

Ten plots are on offer at any time, generated deterministically from the plot's own id, so parcel 47 is the same plot in every session and in every reload. Sizes are multiples of a floor that rises with how much the city already owns; 15% of plots are enormous tracts, and the tract is what makes the listing worth reading.

Two prices, moved by different things

what the city pays a base per sq ft, raised by blocks already owned and by population — the two premiums no longer multiply what businesses pay that, × a scarcity multiplier from 0.65 to 1.90 on allocated/available

The multiplier passes 1.0 at about 61% built — below that the city sells land at a loss, which is the intended cost of over-annexing. The earlier version multiplied by utilisation, which sat between 86% and 100% for 2,400 straight months, so "scarcity" was decoration. The city is founded with a $3.5B endowment — D$2.5B in the treasury and US$1B in the vault since 0.6.10 — enough for a coal plant at 41% of it, a works department at 400 points a month, a shed of material, and a bank standing on day one.

Ground is what a mature city runs out of — land sits at 88–90% for the last decade of most long runs — and the newest sectors are the hungriest for it. A Rail Terminal is 5,500,000 square feet, nearly three times a Coal Power Plant and the largest footprint in the catalogue; a Livestock Farm is 120 acres, the dearest ground of any farm at $108k an acre; a Metro Line is 30,000 square feet for thirty thousand journeys a month, five hundred times a gravel road's capacity per acre. That ladder is the whole reason the modes exist — see transport. Every sector's planner assumes the ground is free and none of them can see they are bidding for the same land; that is on the list.

Iron is a pooled resource. A parcel carries a deposit count and a tonnage; deposits cap how many mines can stand, and every mine draws from one reserve. Roughly 28% of ore parcels carry more than one site. Ore in the ground is worth a fraction of what it fetches once it is out — the value is in getting it out — and a deposit that is worked out closes its mine inside a year, which is what makes the bust when a mining city runs dry a real event rather than a slow leak.

Power and water

The grid starts with 10,000 W free and the reservoir with 8,000 units. Both are gated by the same utility workforce. Power is $0.01 a watt, water $0.05 a unit — about $5 per thousand gallons.

Only businesses are invoiced. Houses, depots, roads, the water plant's own 900 watts and the city's own buildings all draw power nobody pays for. The utility books exactly what its customers' statements charged them, not a recomputation at the end-of-month ratio.

Roads

The city starts with 400 units of capacity — the streets that already exist. Traffic flows freely to 90% of capacity and degrades after that, floored at 35%. Transport & vehicles

Until 16 September the entire transport model was one scalar: a road network with a capacity and a load, one throughput ratio between 0.35 and 1, and three road types that were the same road at three prices. Jerus: "decomposing the existing import/export bands into world price + transportation cost… then later, transportation efficiency can make that freight component smaller or larger." Nine batches followed in three days under the same version number, each one there to make the next possible: the band decomposes, the load splits, the modes and the fare, the railway, the vehicles, and the screen that finally shows a player any of it.

The band was a freight bill

Every traded good carried two constants and the gap between them was the whole local market — and nothing in the game said why the gap was the width it was. Each good carries a third number now, the cost of moving one unit, and the two ends split into four:

worldImportPrice = worldBuyPrice + baseFreight // the world's own ask, plus getting it here worldExportPrice = worldSellPrice − baseFreight // the world's own bid, less getting it there

Three quarters of the wedge is freight and one quarter the world's margin, and the split is a game decision rather than a measurement: it sets how much of the band good logistics can ever win back. At three quarters a 23% band bottoms out near 6%. The price table was already a freight model and nobody designed it that way: freight as a share of the world price sits at 21–25% for fifteen of the seventeen goods that had both ends, and the two outside that band are iron at 49% and materials at 43% — the two lowest-value-density things in the game, where real freight genuinely is half the delivered cost. The delivered prices are the stored literals and the world band is derived, which is the opposite of how it reads and the only way round that is exact: storing the world band and adding the freight back gives 0.8470000000000002 for steel and 0.27999999999999997 for crops, and an ulp in this codebase is never nothing. Not one price moved on the day it shipped, and eight seeds over 333 years came back byte-identical; TradeCostCheck asserts every delivered price against a table of what it was that morning, because a test that reads its own subject proves nothing.

One road load is three

Traffic { COMMUTERS, GOODS, BULK }, and which mode can carry what falls out of the classes rather than needing a table of exceptions: commuters can ride a tram and nothing else can; goods — a shop's bread and milk — are a van at a back door however many rails a city lays, and take the train only across the boundary; bulk — ore, steel, crops, grain, aggregate, beams, machines, a locomotive on a low-loader — is what a railway is for. The shares were fitted rather than guessed, by least squares against the catalogue itself:

roadLoad = 0.726 × jobs + 0.878 × homes + 0.0386 × tonnes a month // R² = 0.74 over the 56 templates that loaded a road when the fit was struck; seventy do today

The implied rate is one worker per nineteen tonnes a month — a truckload — and nobody set out to encode that. The residuals are all explainable and all one way: an Iron Mine 280 over the fit (heavy vehicles), a Penitentiary 288 under and a Long-Term Care Complex 136 under (their occupants do not commute), a University 258 over (students travel and the fit counts only wages). None of it matters, because the fit supplies the share and the stored road load supplies the magnitude: a mine keeps its 420 and learns that 68% of it is ore.

A city that has run out of ground and not out of money builds one; a city with ground to spare never should — which is the first real answer this game has had to its own late-game wall. Every mode is a relief on an unchanged baseline, and that is the design rather than an implementation detail: a city with no highway and no transit computes exactly the load it computed yesterday and degrades on exactly the curve it degraded on. A highway relieves freight, not by carrying more but because a tonne of ore on a grade-separated road does not share a junction with a school run — bulk by 40%, goods by 15%, commuters not at all. Transit takes people off the road entirely, and three things stop it being the answer to everything: at most 65% of commuters ever ride, whatever is built; what transit can carry is capped at twice the road capacity under it, because buses are road vehicles and a metro station needs a street outside it — a city that builds a metro and no streets gets a metro nobody can reach; and it never touches freight. Measured on a load of 10,000 in the played mix: 5,590 riders with room to spare, 4,800 with fewer streets, 800 with none, and with the best of everything the road still carries 39% of what it would. The road can be halved and it can never be deleted. It is also why the stream split matters to a business: an office and a mill on the same jammed road read 74% and 47% against the road's own 42%, because the mill beats the road by exactly as much of its own traffic as rides the tram, and a lorry in a jam is in a jam. A sector with nothing standing reads the city's own blend rather than a clean road, because the planner costs a first plant at the sector's operating rate and a congested city was cheerfully building plants a 38% road said it could not afford.

A fare is a price, not a charge. Nobody chooses to be policed and a patient in a clinic is not shopping; the person on a bus chose to be, and can choose a car instead. So the ridership ceiling is what a free system would carry and the fare walks it down on a straight line to nobody at the maximum: free, 5,590 riders; at the default, 5,311; at the ceiling, none. And the two lines move against each other, which is the whole decision — six times the fare takes $58,695 a month instead of $13,276 and puts 1,398 more cars on the road. Jerus asked for a dial that can be made profitable "by alot"; it can, at a ridership the city may not want. The fare is money, so a reform moves it — the twenty-third of that family and the first caught in the same edit that created the field — and the ridership curve is written against the cap rather than against dollars, so a reform moves both and nobody changes their mind about the bus. Two things were found on the way: nothing in the infrastructure category had ever had a job, so nobody had written a line to pay one, and a Metro Line is 828 posts; and the fare was charged once a month against a constant that says a single journey, so a monthly pass cost $2.50 against a $3,460 wage, seven hundredths of one percent, and the buses could not have paid for themselves at any fare a player would set. That was the transit loss the list had carried as a balance problem. It is 40 journeys a month now — out and back, twenty days — a pass is $100 or 2.9% of an unskilled wage, and the dial stays per ride because that is the number a person on a platform recognises.

The fare was collected from nobody

From the day transit was built until 16 September the city credited itself the fare and debited no household. Nobody saw it because nothing had ever built a bus: the playtest's advisor answered every jam with tarmac, so four thousand months a seed, eight seeds a run, not one city ever carried a passenger, and every fixture that needed a road bought a road. The first ensemble in which the advisor bought a Bus Network failed the money audit on all eight seeds, and the residual was the fare to six decimals — 2,186 riders at $2.50 is $5.47, and the audit said 5.465207. A harness cannot check a thing nobody does. MoneyCheck's own header says every money bug this codebase has had was a flow with one side, and the twenty-ninth harness could not see this one because its city had no buses. It builds three now. Fixing it found its sibling at once: setTransit was missing from the load path, the fifth sighting of that shape, invisible until the fare became real money.

The railway

Rail is the twelfth sector, and the business that wants to be paid the freight every price in this game has always had inside it. Jerus: "the freight cant reach zero cause freight needs profit, its its own sector… it wants and will do everything possible to stay profitable and maximize profits. freight money goes to that sector." There were two ways to write that and only one keeps the audit honest. Paying the railway out of the band would have a payment the books record as going abroad in fact going to a company in the city. So the band carries only what the lorries still move, and the railway bills the shipper at home, like any other supplier of a service — the invoice goes on the shipper's input line, where freight belongs in anybody's accounts, the money never leaves the city, and the VAT falls out for free because the ledger already credits a purchase at the supplier's own rate.

what a shipper pays a unit = (1 − share) × baseFreight // still to the lorries, inside the band + share × quote × baseFreight // to the railway, on its invoice allowed = what a month costs to run + 1.2% of the track it has sunk quote = clamp(allowed / what it would bill at the lorry rate × scarcity, 0.30, 1)

A shop's pricing rule cannot price a utility, and the measurement is what said so. Written first as Retail's shelf — cost plus a quarter — it quoted 32% of the lorry rate, earned $4.5m a month on ten billion of track, and was written down six times: a railway's running cost is a fraction of its capital, so a mark-up on running costs allows a return of nothing on the track. It prices like a regulated network now, running costs plus 1.2% a month on the rate base, floored at 30% of the lorry rate because rail is cheaper than a lorry and not free, capped at one because nobody ships by rail at more than the lorry charges, lifted up to 1.6× by what it cannot carry. It settles at 38–43%. Building too much rail does not make freight cheaper; it makes a railway that cannot pay for itself, and RailCheck asserts that a network with four times the track it needs pins at the lorries' price and earns less on four times the capital. The link that falls out of it is real and welcome: a railway earning 1.2% a month on money that costs it 1% is barely a business, so dear money is what keeps a city's trade band wide. And the band alone was lying to the sectors: at a 92% quote on 92% of the traffic it said steel's wedge had fallen from 52% to 11% when the shipper had saved eight per cent, because the railway's invoice was not in it. Every place that asks is this worth exporting reads a net price now; every place that settles a trade reads the band; with no railway the two are the same number to the bit.

Track is half a railway. Capacity is the lesser of the track laid and the wagon sets to run it, at 2,500 tonnes a set and a twenty-year life, so track with no locomotives carries nothing: a Rail Spur is twenty sets, $48m of rolling stock on a $140m spur, and a mature city's million tonnes of track is 420 sets and about 1¾ sets a month of replacement for ever, which is half a Locomotive Works running. Rolling stock is a pantry rather than an input — that one line is what makes a bought set accumulate into a fleet instead of vanishing into a month's production — and a save from before trains has trains, because it was hauling, so it had locomotives. The railway will not lay a line under 60% used, Materials' lesson relearned the afternoon a spur went up in a town of four hundred houses and a bank failed over it. It relieves 75% of the bulk it carries off the road, never all of it, because the last mile off a siding is a lorry and a spur reads as 94% trucks; bulk goes before goods, which decides almost nothing today because bulk is 99.7% of the tonnage crossing the boundary. Every tonne it hauls burns $30 of fuel in the world's money, a real import before there is any oil good, so the day OIL is a good nothing else changes.

What a railway is worth

Eight seeds, four thousand months each: population 113,178 → 178,975 (+58%), GDP $416.8M → $711.2M a month (+71%), per seed 32–91% and 14–113%. On the default seed steel's net wedge goes from 51.6% of the world price to 25.9%. Three rungs — 50,000, 200,000 and 500,000 tonnes a month at $140m, $520m and $1.24bn — $2,800 of capital per tonne a month on a spur and $2,480 on a terminal, and a terminal nearly three times a Coal Power Plant on the ground, which is the land trade Jerus asked for.

The vehicles

Automotive is the thirteenth sector, and it is not for the cars. Ore becomes steel, steel becomes fabricated steel and machinery, and until 16 September both left, because nothing in the city bought a beam or a machine. An assembly plant is the first thing that does — the fourth link of a chain that stopped at three — and the chain cannot be shortcut: fabricated steel is deliberately not importable, so a city with money and no fabrication shop cannot be in the car business, it can only buy cars. Machinery can come off a ship, at the ceiling its own header had predicted for the day something here bought one, because a car is three and a half tonnes of fabricated steel and four hundred kilos of machinery and the bulky input is the one a city must make. Cars export; vans and locomotives do not, from three measurements of one failure: allowed to sell wagon sets to the world, the playtest built 19 Locomotive Works, 9,880 posts in a city with 25,102 jobs; allowed to sell vans, 240 Commercial Vehicle Plants shipping 36,000 vans a month; and it was already building 179 Fabrication Works and zero Machine Works. An unbounded export market at a fixed floor means the single best export is built for ever and nothing else ever is; bounded world demand is the general fix and is still its own batch. A plant may draw at most a quarter of what the city fabricates, because the first supply gate asked the mills' question — is there spare local supply — and nothing fabricated is ever spare when the world will take all of it; a $172m Assembly Plant sank in month seventeen of a fixture that was measuring tuition, and the harness was right. Eight seeds: GDP $711.2M → $758.8M a month, up in six of eight; seed 0's mature city runs 84 Assembly Plants on 23% of its fabrication.

The cars are the fourth mode, and the only one that costs. Highways, transit and rail are all reliefs on an unchanged baseline; a car is the opposite, and it has to be, because the baseline this network always computed was an unmotorised city, unlabelled. It is named now rather than moved:

onRoad = (commuters − riders) × (1 + ownership × (3.0 − 1)) // exactly 1 at zero ownership, to the bit willingToRide = (1 − ownership) + ownership × 0.75 × jam // jam is a memory of the commute, a quarter a month

Only the people still driving pay it, so a city that motorises and builds transit is not charged twice for one journey, and that is what closes Jerus's loop: "people drive until the road is full, then take the tram." A car owner rides nothing on a clear morning and three quarters of them ride at a standstill — motorise, nobody rides, it jams, they ride, it clears — read off a memory of the commute rather than last month's road, or it is a two-month oscillator. Transit is a ceiling on ownership, not a brake on adoption, and the difference is the whole of whether a player has a move: on the rate it only delays, on the level a well-served city tops out at half a car per household for ever. What motorising costs, eight seeds against the same build with adoption off: 191,194 people and $848.3M a month at 80% road, against 176,228 and $695.7M at 73%, cars in 60% of households and 37% of commuters riding — and the chain is not congestion alone: a worse road costs every new plant at a lower operating rate, marginal projects stop clearing the interest test, business borrowing collapses ($6.6bn lent against $18.5bn, the bank at 45% of capacity instead of 77%), Manufacturing's assets fall from $23.4bn to $6.3bn, exports fall 26%. A six-point road ratio, compounded over three centuries. CAR_LOAD_AT_SATURATION = 3.0 is the one constant here that is a game decision rather than a measured one. The advisor learned to buy a bus the same day, and what decides between six candidates is the catalogue: each is costed in trips taken off the road per dollar — a gravel road 900 for $2,326k against a bus network 2,500 for $12,000k times the car factor — so a bus loses outright in a city where nobody drives and wins once about half the households own a car. Nobody chose that crossover.

The vans are the fifth throttle, and the first one a business buys. Energy, water, road and health all arrive; a lorry is the sector's own capital, sized from a measurement: a city of 106,612 moves 1,169,640 tonnes a month at nameplate, both sides of the door, which at 120 tonnes a vehicle is a fleet of about ten thousand, one commercial vehicle per eleven people, and eighty a month of replacement for ever. Built once without teeth, every ratio in the measured city read 100.0%, because vans are importable and a sector asking for a fleet got one in a month; you cannot put a thousand lorries on the road in a month, so a fleet is built up at an eighth a month and a firm short of its own lorries hires haulage — slowed to 60%, not stopped. Re-measured: 132 sector-months short, worst 65%, every growing sector hitting it and none stopped. A save from before vans owns a fleet, on the railway's own flag: it was moving steel, so it had lorries. And it is what finally gives the Commercial Vehicle Plant a customer.

The statement opens

Time runs on its own

There is no next month button. The city runs on a clock at five seconds a month (SECONDS_PER_MONTH) and a speed the player picks off a snapping ladder — 0.1× · 0.25× · 0.5× · 1× · 2× · 5× · 10× · 20× · 50× — with the space bar as play/pause and the arrow keys moving the ladder. The day inside the month is painted so the city looks alive between the ticks, even though nothing in the model moves until the month turns.

Three things that only matter once the month arrives unasked. A frame that takes a long time is clamped at a quarter second, so a stalled window cannot swallow thirty months while it was away — the worst a single frame can advance is 2.5 months. Redraw is capped at one in every 120ms, because at 50× a month lands every 100ms and the screen cannot usefully be redrawn faster than it can be read. And a panel is now rebuilt while the player is still looking at it, so every scrolling page has to hold its own position across the rebuild rather than snapping to the top — which took seven attempts and a trace file to get right, and is written up on its own.

The game can pause itself when something happens, and by default it does not: the setting exists, and it is off, because a clock that stops without being asked is worse than a notice that waits.

The summary is a problem list

The summary panel used to be six fixed rows — economy, tax, labour, health, land, people. Of the sixteen problems a player can actually build their way out of it could show exactly one, health, and only as a sick rate. A city with no water, no cells and nowhere to bury its dead read exactly like a city with none of those problems.

It is two lists now. NEEDS YOU carries twenty conditions that have a lever, yellow near the line and red past it, each row a door to the screen that owns the fix, and each disappearing when it is solved; when it is empty it names the condition closest to its yellow line instead, so it is never blank. HOW THE CITY IS carries seven readings with no dial — out of work, off sick, hungry, people a month, doubled up, ground used, the currency — always present, coloured when bad, and its door goes to the screen that diagnoses rather than the one that fixes.

Writing it turned up a model bug that no harness had: getEnergyRatio() and getWaterRatio() both return min(supply / demand, 1), so a row keyed off them could not appear until the lights were already out. They read load — demand over supply — now, against the 75% the dashboard has called "tight" all along.

The calendar

Month 1 is January 2000. Purely presentational, with deliberately no reverse function — nothing should derive a month from a date string.

Skipping time

A skip takes a snapshot before, samples every month during, and diffs a snapshot after. Episodes cannot be reconstructed from endpoints: a city that starved for fifty months and recovered looks identical at both ends to one that never had a problem. The report counts months short of power, water, land and road capacity, months nothing was built, epidemics and their worst month, and the peak unburied — then writes plain sentences ordered by how much each should worry you.

Rules the harnesses are written to

A fixture must cause the condition, not wait for it. Assert against the model's own constants, never a literal — a check pinned to 0.06 or 2.25 tests that nobody edited the harness. Assert the shape of a distribution, not only its total — an aggregate cannot see a missing category. Count how often a new mechanic actually fires in a real run — out-migration once shipped fully tested and fired zero times in 4,002 months, and foreign borrowing had to be told to happen. A quantity in one unit tested against a rule that assumed another is the shape that keeps recurring. A fixture has to cause the condition under test, not stand next to it — fifty-odd sightings now, the latest a fixture that measured an oven's revenue on one of its two products and reported it losing money. When a fix is safe because some case is exactly unchanged, assert that case at zero tolerance — a sector making one good must carry exactly 1.0 of its own cost, and that is a check rather than a comment. A harness cannot check a thing nobody does — the fare was collected from nobody for as long as no fixture had built a bus. When a harness goes red on your change, ask the same question of ten fixtures before believing it is your change — one fixture said the farms broke the currency reform; ten said the model had been carrying a coin-toss for months. A sentinel is a value for the code to compare, never a value to print, and the place it leaks is the screen, which was the one layer with no harness behind it — and the plainer rule beside it: play the game, because fifty-one green harnesses and a ten-fixture sweep did not find a nine-quintillion-dollar unit cost that clicking on Agriculture found in ninety seconds. Assert the premise the whole suite stands on — a month++ left twice at the top of the month passed every harness in the suite, and only the playtest's month numbers gave it away; CalendarCheck asserts one press is one month now. Is the field money? Then it belongs in the reform the day it is written — a rule cheaper than the twenty-five constants of the family before it. A held sector is held in both directions — a planner that could demolish a harness's fixture scrapped six spurs of an over-built railway, and an assertion whose subject is decided by a planner is an assertion about the planner.

Decided by dust

The money-constant family — a bound written in absolute money that a currency reform walks past — is past twenty-five sightings, and since 16 September it has two relatives that are not constants at all. The sign of a residue. Four retired households owed minus 2.7e-29 dollars, out of the monthly rebuild handing a cell weight × g / g, which can come back an ulp above weight. The magnitude is not money and nothing reads it as money; one thing reads its sign — investAbroad() asks debt <= 0 to decide who may keep money abroad — so four cells flipped, their paper came home at a tenth a month, the financial account moved by thousands, and the exchange rate, which is every import and export price in the city, moved with it: a city and its reformed twin ran together at 1e-13 for 158 months and were 2–4% apart a decade later. The fix is four clamps and no numbers, because a ratio cannot go stale in a new unit. A zero-crossing test is a threshold, and a threshold on a residue is decided by luck. A money ratio that comes out as a physical quantity. The car affordability test is money over money and the answer is cars, which the road, the market and the trade balance all read; (a/100)/(b/100) is not a/b, so a reformed city wanted an ulp more of a car, bought it somewhere else, and DenominationCheck went red on sixteen assertions. Scale-invariant in arithmetic and not in floating point, and the cure is a grain: a cell buys whole cars, and the shops sell whole baskets. The playtest flags any household position below zero at zero tolerance, every month of every seed, and was seen to fail 216 times before it was trusted.

Two more sightings since. A hair of borrowing is a discrete state (0.6.8): the waterfall clamps a negative remainder, but a positive 1e-21 becomes a debt, and investAbroad() gates on debt <= 0; the first draft of the price at the door re-split a row's bill by its paying heads, and the reform twins parted a decade later over interest 2.36e-21 against 0.0 in one cell. The re-split was dropped rather than touch the waterfall. A ghost household (0.7.2): a cell whose census count had fallen to a residue — 1e-15 households, or a real 0.3 — still held savings and was still being paid, a household to some lines and not to others, and one line divided a credit by it; DenominationCheck went red at a floating-point knife-edge and the trace found it. The cure is a grain again: under half a household a cell is empty, and DenominationCheck looks 202 times over both cities and finds no cell under half a household holding anything.

The tree, and the prose that describes it

The tree is 211 files and 138,090 lines: one flat package plus sectors/, ui/ and tools/. Game.java is the seam every system meets at, 9,277 lines and twenty-eight banner sections, and on 18 September four mechanics that already had the shape of a class left it with their text intact and their behaviour byte-for-byte unchanged — the households' car market as Motoring, the boutiques and the kitchens as LuxuryCounter, who is at risk of offending as Offending, what the city eats as CityBasket — each called from the same line of the month it always was, with every moved getter kept on Game as a delegation so that no caller changed. The currency reform stayed, because it redenominates twenty-two subsystems through Game's own fields and that is the seam doing the seam's job. The investor, the foreign borrowing and demographics are bigger and entangled, and each would be a plan of its own.

Nobody reads those files whole. tools/ reads the sources as text and writes docs/: a code map with one row per file and one page per file listing its banners and every method with its line; dials.md, every static final with its value and the sentence above it, 780 of them; month-order.md, the month as a numbered list of statements with where each call goes, which is what section 01 of this page is struck against; and harnesses.md, the 3,870 labelled assertions under their sections and which harnesses name which class — which is how it is known that nothing yet names Automotive or LuxuryRetail. Where finds a member or a banner by name and prints it; SaveDump looks inside a save without loading it. And every batch has a sixth step since 18 September, done by a context that did not make the change: a docs pass that makes every comment, header, index and count agree with the code again, because the agent that changed the code has stopped seeing the prose. Its first run found four stranded javadocs and two false headers; its mechanical half is tools.Stale, which found 84 orphaned javadocs on its first run and is at zero now; the half that needs judgement stays with a reader, and four comments whose numbers disagreed with their constants were decided by Jerus — "the code is correct, the comments are the outliers."

A bank with no lending businessHalf answered, and the other half closed by measurement. On the default seed on 22 September the bank lends $13.2bn of $77.9bn deposited and runs at 69% of its capacity. The households' trillionHalf answered, and the half that is left is a supply question. A lockupThe smallest prison is 300 cells for $360M; a city of 15,000 needs about twenty cells.
